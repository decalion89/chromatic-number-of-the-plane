# Coset colourings of unit-distance modules: a blind rotation, coarse rigidity, and propagation

This note collects, with proofs, the structural results the project found on the
unit-distance graph of the `ρ₇` module. They are stated for a general module
where that costs nothing. The exact certificates behind the numerical claims
are produced by the scripts named in brackets and re-checked in
`tests/test_kappa.py`.

## Setting

- `M ⊂ ℂ` is a finitely generated ℤ-module.
- `U ⊂ M` is a finite set of unit vectors with `U = −U`.
- `G(M, U)` is the graph on `M` in which `x ~ x + u` for `u ∈ U`.
- A **coset colouring** is `c(x) = ψ(x) + k` with `ψ ∈ Hom(M, ℤ/5)` *admissible*,
  meaning `ψ(u) ≠ 0` for every `u ∈ U`. It is a proper 5-colouring.
- For such a `ψ` and `t ∈ (ℤ/5)^*`, write `D_t = {u ∈ U : ψ(u) = t}`.
- `M` has rank `r` and is a lattice in `M ⊗ ℝ ≅ ℝ^r`.

## 1. A rotation every coset colouring is blind to

Let `ω = (1 + √−3)/2`, a primitive sixth root of unity (`notes/local_colourings.md`
writes `ω` for the cube root `(−1 + √−3)/2` instead), and
`ρ₇ = (1 + 4√−3)/7 = (2 + √−3)/(2 − √−3)`, the rotation of `ℚ(√−3)` at the
prime 7.

**Lemma 1.** `κ := ωρ₇ = (−11 + 5√−3)/14` satisfies `|κ| = 1` and
`κ − 1 = 5·(ω − 3)/7`. So `κ ≡ 1 (mod 5ℤ[ω, 1/7])`, and `κ` is not a root of
unity.

*Proof.* `ω − 3 = (−5 + √−3)/2`, so `5(ω − 3)/7 = (−25 + 5√−3)/14 = κ − 1`. We
have `|κ|² = (121 + 75)/196 = 1`. Finally, `cos κ = −11/14` is not in
`{0, ±1/2, ±1}`. ∎

**Corollary.** If `(ω − 3)x/7 ∈ M`, every homomorphism `ψ : M → ℤ/5` has
`ψ(κx) = ψ(x)`. On `five_rho7` (rank 8, 93 directions, 960 admissible `ψ`),
120 of the 186 units have `κu ∈ U`, and for each of them `κu − u ∈ 5M`.
`ω` and `ρ₇` separately are not scalar on any admissible `ψ`.

**Remark.** 5 is inert in `ℚ(√−3)`. The norm-one elements of `ℤ[ω]_(5)`
therefore reduce into the order-6 group generated by `ω mod 5`. So *every*
rotation of `ℚ(√−3)` that is integral at 5 becomes congruent to 1 mod 5 after
multiplication by a power of `ω`. `κ` is the one attached to the prime 7.

## 2. No twisted colouring: exact Stiemke certificates

A *twisted colouring* is `c = ψ + t·⌊φ/L⌋`, with `φ` a real functional and `L`
large. It is proper exactly when `φ ≥ 0` on `D_t`.

**Lemma 2 (Stiemke).** The following are equivalent (E. Stiemke, *Über positive
Lösungen homogener linearer Gleichungen*, Math. Ann. 76 (1915) 340–342):
- there is no `φ ≠ 0` with `φ ≥ 0` on `D_t`;
- `D_t` spans `M ⊗ ℝ` and admits a linear dependency `Σ λ_u u = 0` with every
  `λ_u > 0`.

**Fact.** On `five_rho7`, the second condition holds for all 960 × 4 pairs
`(ψ, t)`, with exact rational `λ` (`scripts/stiemke.py`). On `five_247_c`
(the 803 module, without `ρ₇`) it holds for only 1 664 of 6 912 pairs.

## 3. Rigidity propagates

**Theorem 1.** Let `ψ` be admissible, and suppose every `D_t` (`t = 1…4`)
positively spans `M ⊗ ℝ`. Let `c` be a proper 5-colouring of `G(M, U)` that
agrees with `ψ + k` on `{x : φ(x) < a}` for some real functional `φ ≠ 0` and
some `a ∈ ℝ`. Then `c = ψ + k` on all of `M`.

*Proof.* Set `δ = min_t max_{u ∈ D_t} (−φ(u))`. Since `D_t` positively spans,
some `u ∈ D_t` has `φ(u) < 0`, so `δ > 0`. We show by induction on `m` that
`c = ψ + k` on `{φ < a + mδ}`.

Take `x` with `a + mδ ≤ φ(x) < a + (m + 1)δ`. For each `t`, choose `u_t ∈ D_t`
with `φ(u_t) ≤ −δ`. Then `φ(x + u_t) < a + mδ`, so
`c(x + u_t) = ψ(x) + t + k`. The four points `x + u_t` are all adjacent to `x`,
so `c(x)` avoids `ψ(x) + t + k` for `t = 1, …, 4`, which leaves only
`c(x) = ψ(x) + k`. ∎

**Theorem 1′ (balls).** Fix a Euclidean norm on `M ⊗ ℝ`, and let `δ₀ > 0` be
the least inradius about 0 of the polytopes `conv(D_t)`, taken over all `(ψ, t)`.
There is an `R₀` (for instance `R₀ = max|u|²/δ₀`) with the following property:
if `c = ψ + k` on `B(z, R) ∩ M` for some `R ≥ R₀`, then `c = ψ + k` everywhere.

*Proof.* The same induction, run outward. At a point `x` just outside the known
ball, take `φ` to be the inward normal. The unit `u_t` given by the inradius
bound lands inside the ball once `R ≥ R₀`. ∎

**Corollary (coarse rigidity).** Suppose `ℝ^r` is cut into polyhedral cells
whose faces are much larger than `max|u|`, and `c = ψ_P + k_P` on each cell `P`.
Then `c` is a single coset colouring, provided both of the following hold:
- every `D_t` positively spans;
- every nonzero integer functional takes an absolute value `≥ 5` on some unit.

On `five_rho7` the second condition holds: none has `|φ(u)| ≤ 6` on all units.

*Proof.* Let `F` be a facet between cells `P` and `Q`, and suppose first that
`ψ_P ≠ ψ_Q`. Their difference must be constant along the lattice points next to
`F`. That forces `F = {φ = 0}` with `φ` a primitive integer functional and
`ψ_Q − ψ_P = λφ`. A unit `u` with `φ(u) = s ≥ 5` then crosses from level `−j`
to level `s − j` for `j = 1, …, s`. The colour changes by
`ψ_P(u) + δ + λ(s − j)`, which runs through every residue, so some `j` gives a
conflict. Hence `ψ_P = ψ_Q`, and each cell agrees with the coset colouring on
a half-space near `F`; Theorem 1 finishes the proof. ∎

**Consequence.** On `five_rho7`, any 5-colouring that is not a coset colouring
disagrees with every coset colouring on every ball of radius `R₀`. It is
*nowhere locally coset*.

If no such colouring exists, then by compactness:
- some finite subgraph forces a pair `a, a + 5e` alike;
- some finite subgraph forces a pair `a, a + 2e` apart.

Either one gives `χ(ℝ²) ≥ 6`. The first closes with Exoo–Ismailescu's rotation
`λ = (49 + 3√−11)/50`. The second closes through their two-distance theorem.
Their graph `H` lies in the module after a turn of 150°.

**Scale.** In the trace form every unit of `five_rho7` has the same length.
Sampling with qhull suggests that the inradius of `conv(D_t)` is about 0.057 of
that length or more, which would make `R₀ = max|u|²/δ₀` about 17.5 unit steps
in ℝ⁸; this is an estimate, not a proven bound. It is far beyond anything a SAT
solver can hold. Every finite graph built so far has colourings with a best
coset fit of 22–30%, which is chance level.

## 4. What remained open

Both questions below were answered later: §5 exhibits a nowhere-locally-coset
5-colouring of `five_rho7`, and §6 shows that `803 ∪ λ(803)` is 5-colourable.
- Does `G(five_rho7)` have a nowhere-locally-coset 5-colouring? If it does not,
  then `χ(ℝ²) ≥ 6`.
- Is `G(803 ∪ λ(803))` 5-colourable at all? It has:
  - no coset colouring;
  - no "nearly coset" colouring;
  - no periodic colouring through any quotient tested.

  Its grown subgraphs of 31 000 points still have 5-colourings, but tabu search
  from a random start no longer finds them.

## 5. Periodic colourings, invariant measures, and what rigidity would imply

**Ideal-periodic colourings: a negative that says little.** The `five_rho7`
module is a ℤ[ω]-module with at least 198 unit vectors. For `α ∈ {3 + ω, 4 − ω}`
(norm 13) and `α ∈ {3 + 2ω, 2 + 3ω}` (norm 19), `Cay(M/αM, U)` is not
5-colourable. The same holds for the λ-closure. The pair queries on
`M/(ker ψ ∩ αM)` are UNSAT too. **All of these are folding artefacts.**
- The torus core has 15 vertices and 75 edges, and 53 of its edges do not lift
  to the module.
- The pair is already forced on a radius-1 torus ball.

A periodic test means something only when the period is long compared with the
unit steps.

**Circular colourings (§6) do mean something, and one exists.** `five_rho7` has
a non-coset 5-colouring `c = ⌊5·frac(φ)⌋`. Here `φ` is a character of order 280
with every unit value in `[1/5, 4/5]`. So *nowhere-locally-coset colourings
exist*, and the strong rigidity conjecture is false. The colouring still
colours Exoo–Ismailescu's pair alike.

**Theorem 2 (measures suffice).** Let `M′ = M₁ + λM₁`, and let `e` be a unit of
`M₁` with `|5e − 5λe| = 1`. Suppose every `M₁`-invariant probability measure on
proper 5-colourings of `G(M₁)` has `c(0) = c(5e)` almost surely. Then `G(M′)`
is not 5-colourable.

*Proof.*
1. A colouring of `G(M′)`, averaged over Følner boxes of `M′`, gives an
   `M′`-invariant measure `ν`.
2. Restricted to `M₁`, `ν` gives `c(0) = c(5e)` almost surely.
3. Pushed forward by `c ↦ c(λ·)`, which carries translation by `m` to
   translation by `λm`, it gives `c(0) = c(5λe)` almost surely.
4. So `c(5e) = c(5λe)` almost surely, across a unit edge. ∎

**Proposition 3 (rigidity is at least as hard as a measurable question).**
Suppose every proper 5-colouring of `G(M, U)` colours `x` and `x + m` apart for
all `m ∈ u₀ + 5M`, for some unit `u₀`. Coset colourings do this. Then no
Lebesgue-measurable 5-colouring of the plane is proper along the directions
`U`. Consequently, the rigidity of any module that is dense in the plane, with
finitely many unit directions, implies that the measurable chromatic number of
the plane is at least 6, which is open (it is at least 5: K. J. Falconer, *The
realization of distances in measurable subsets covering ℝⁿ*, J. Combin. Theory
Ser. A 31 (1981) 184–189).

*Proof.* `M` is dense in the plane, so `5M` is too, and `u₀ + 5M` contains vectors
`mⱼ → 0`. For almost every `y`, the colouring `m ↦ f(y + m)` of `G(M, U)` is
proper, hence `f(y + mⱼ) ≠ f(y)` for every `j`. But
`‖f(· + mⱼ) − f‖_{L¹(B)} → 0` on every ball `B`. ∎

So a proof of rigidity cannot be purely local or fractional. Independent sets
alone cannot give it either: Croft's tortoise (H. T. Croft, *Incidence
incidents*, Eureka 30 (1967) 22–26), restricted to a generic coset,
is an independent set of `G(M, U)` of density 0.229 > 1/5. Whatever proof
exists must use the partition into five classes.

## 6. The circular gate

For `φ ∈ Hom(M, ℝ/ℤ)`, `c(x) = ⌊5·frac(φ(x))⌋` is proper iff
`frac(φ(u)) ∈ [1/5, 4/5]` for every unit `u`. Coset colourings are the points
`φ = ψ/5`.

**Proposition 4 (necessary condition for six).** If the feasible region
`F(M) = {φ : frac(φ(U)) ⊂ [1/5, 4/5]}` is nonempty, every unit-distance graph
with edges in `M` is 5-colourable. By compactness of the dual of the discrete
group ℝ², and because characters extend from subgroups: if `F(span U′) ≠ ∅`
for every finite set `U′` of unit vectors, then `χ(ℝ²) = 5`.

**Results** (MILP `scripts/circgate.py`, exact check `scripts/circverify.py`):
- **`803 ∪ λ(803)`:** slack 387/31250 > 0. The 46 496-point grown graph is
  coloured without a monochromatic edge. So the blocked module is 5-colourable.
- **`five_rho7`:** a non-coset `φ` of order 280, with `φ(5e) ∈ ℤ` on the E–I
  pair.
- **The 803-graph at 4 colours:** infeasible. This is the sanity check.

**Open, and now the right question.** Is there a module, rich enough to hold
Exoo–Ismailescu's configuration, with `F(M) = ∅`? And does any circular colouring
of `five_rho7` split the E–I pair?

## 7. The homomorphic chromatic number of the plane

**Definition.** View ℝ² as an abstract abelian group. Let `χ_hom(ℝ²)` be the
least `k` for which there is a character `φ : ℝ² → ℝ/ℤ` with
`frac(φ(u)) ∈ [1/k, 1 − 1/k]` for every unit vector `u`. Such a `φ` gives the
proper `k`-colouring `c(x) = ⌊k·frac(φ(x))⌋`: a shift by at least `1/k` and at
most `1 − 1/k` always changes the `k`-th of the circle a point lies in.

**Proposition 5.**
1. `χ(ℝ²) ≤ χ_hom(ℝ²)`, so `χ_hom(ℝ²) ≥ 5`.
2. **Compactness.** `χ_hom(ℝ²) ≤ k` if and only if every finite set `U′` of
   unit vectors admits a character `φ` of the group generated by `U′`, with
   `frac(φ(U′)) ⊂ [1/k, 1 − 1/k]`.
3. **Every finite set has a character for large `k`.** Let `U′` consist of
   `n` pairs `±u`. For a uniform random character of `span(U′)`, each
   `frac(φ(u))` is uniform. So the union bound gives a character with all
   values in `[1/k, 1 − 1/k]` as soon as `2n/k < 1`.
4. **Single new units never cut.** Let `φ` work on `U′`, and let `w` have order
   `N ≥ 2` modulo `span(U′)`. Then `φ` extends to `span(U′) + ℤw` with `w`
   still in the window: the `N` candidate values of `φ(w)` are spaced `1/N`
   apart, and at most one of any two of them can miss a window of width
   `1 − 2/k ≥ 1/2`. So obstructions come only from *cycles* of relations among
   many unit vectors at once.

*Proof of 2.* The dual of the discrete group ℝ² is compact, and each condition
`frac(φ(u)) ∈ [1/k, 1 − 1/k]` is closed. Apply the finite intersection
property. A character of a subgroup extends to the whole group because ℝ/ℤ is
divisible. ∎

**Consequences.**
- A single finite set of unit vectors with no admissible character, in **any**
  field, proves `χ_hom(ℝ²) ≥ 6`: *no 5-colouring of the plane is of the form
  `⌊5·frac(φ(x))⌋` with `φ` additive.*
- If instead every finite set passes at `k = 6`, then `χ(ℝ²) ≤ 6`, and at
  `k = 5`, then `χ(ℝ²) = 5`.

The circular gate computes exactly this, set by set. What it has found so far:
- **`k = 4`:** the 803-graph fails, so `χ_hom ≥ 5`, as it must be.
- **`k = 5`, passing sets:** `five_rho7`, the blocked `803 ∪ λ(803)`, and
  Exoo–Ismailescu's `H` and `K`.
- **`k = 5`, undecided:** the `λ`-closure of `five_rho7`.
- **Resolved since:** the rank-4 set of 162 rotation units in `ℚ(√−3, √−11)`
  (`scripts/rotunits.py`, generated by `ω`, `σ^{±1}`, `λ^{±1}`, `ρ₇^{±1}`) has
  gate value `κ(U) = 1/4` exactly (a number, not the rotation `κ` of §1; SCIP
  in relation space, 34 s), and every unit set of that field has `κ ≥ 1/4`:
  see §8.

## 8. The local criterion

Sections 8–11 were written first. `notes/local_colourings.md` restates them with
full proofs and is the reference; its numbering of the results differs from the
numbering used here.

**Proposition 6 (rational vertices).** `F_k(M)` is a finite union of polytopes
cut out by rational hyperplanes in the torus `Hom(M, ℝ/ℤ)`. If it is nonempty
it contains a character of finite order, which colours periodically through a
cyclic quotient `ℤ/N`. So every circular colouring can be replaced by an
*adelic* one: a finite sum of `p`-adic characters.

**Proposition 7 (one place colours a field).** Let `K` be a CM field, embedded
so that complex conjugation is the automorphism fixing the real subfield `K⁺`.
Its unit vectors are then the `z ∈ K` with `z z̄ = 1`. Let `v` be a finite place
of `K⁺` that does not split in `K`. Then `T(K⁺_v) = {u ∈ K_v : u ū = 1}` is
compact. Suppose `φ(z) = frac_p(L(z))`, with `L` a `ℚ_p`-linear form on `K_v`,
satisfies `‖φ(u)‖ ≥ 1/k` on `T(K⁺_v)`. Then `z ↦ ⌊k·φ(z)⌋` is a proper
`k`-colouring of the unit-distance graph with vertex set `K ⊂ ℂ = ℝ²`.

*Proof.* `φ` is additive on `K_v`, hence on `K`. Every unit vector of `K` lies
in `T(K⁺_v)`, where `φ` stays at least `1/k` from 0. ∎

**Lemma 8 (odd places).** Let `v` be odd, with `K_v/K⁺_v` unramified and
residue field `𝔽_p`. Let `r` be minimal with `φ(p^r O) = 0`.
- **If `r ≥ 2`, the values smear.** Take `u = u₀(1 + p^{r−1}t)`, corrected to
  norm 1, with `t` in the tangent line. As `t` runs over that line, the values
  run over a whole coset of `(1/p)ℤ`, unless the reduced character kills the
  line `ū₀√d·𝔽_p`. Those lines take `(p + 1)/2 ≥ 2` distinct values, so some
  coset appears. Every coset meets `(−1/k, 1/k)` once `p ≥ k/2`.
- **So `r = 1`.** Then `φ(u) = Tr(ȳū)/p` on the `p + 1` norm-one residues.
  Trace `c` is attained by norm class `n` iff `c² − 4n` is a non-residue or 0.

The places that admit a local 5-colouring are therefore `p ∈ {3, 5, 7, 19}`.
For 4 colours they are `{3, 7}`, and for 6 colours `{3, 5, 7, 11, 17, 19}`.
This was checked for `p < 10⁵`, and the Weil bound (A. Weil, *On some exponential
sums*, Proc. Natl. Acad. Sci. USA 34 (1948) 204–207) on
`Σ_{c < p/k} (c² − 4n / p)` handles larger `p`.

**Theorem 9 (the Moser field).** The unit-distance graph on `ℚ(√−3, √−11)` has
chromatic number 4.

*Proof.* `33 ≡ 1 mod 8`, so a place of `ℚ(√33)` above 2 has completion `ℚ₂`,
and `K_v = ℚ₂(ω)` is unramified: 2 does not split. `√−11 = √33·√−3/3` embeds
`K`. The norm-one units of `ℤ₂[ω]` reduce mod 4 to the six sixth roots of unity.
`φ(α + βω) = frac₂((α + 2β)/4)` is 1/4, 1/2 or 3/4 on these, so
Proposition 7 applies with `k = 4`. The Moser spindle lies in `K`. ∎

**Consequences.**
- **A necessary condition on the field.** A field hosting a 6-chromatic
  unit-distance graph must have no non-split place with a local 5-colouring.
  Every place above 5 must split, for instance, which is why `λ`'s denominator
  25 was needed. That is the denominator-five principle, seen from the field.
- **`five_rho7`'s field.** In `ℚ(√−3, √−11, √−247)` the places above 2, 3,
  5, 7, 13 and 19 split. The place above 11 does not split, and its finite plane
  is 5-colourable, so the whole field is 5-colourable (§9).
- **This is not the whole gate.** Proposition 7 is only sufficient. A finite
  module can still be coloured by characters that live on several places,
  including split places where its unit vectors have bounded denominators.
  The order-280 colouring of `five_rho7` uses the places above 2, 5 and 7, all
  split. Killing those takes unit vectors whose denominators at each split
  prime exceed what any character of bounded level can absorb. That is what
  the `λ`-closure does at 5, and what rotation words in `τ`, `ρ₇`, `λ` do at
  2, 7 and 5.

## 9. Reduction at a non-split place: the field of `five_rho7` is 5-colourable

**Proposition 10 (cf. [Madore](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.07023), Prop. 3.2, ¶6.6).** Let `v` be a finite place of `K⁺` that does not split in the
CM field `K`.
1. **Unramified, residue field `𝔽_q`.** Every unit vector reduces into the
   norm-one group `N₁ ⊂ 𝔽_{q²}`, which has order `q + 1`. Fix a representative
   of each coset of `O_v` in `K_v`, and set `g(z) = (z − rep(z)) mod π`. Then
   `g(z + u) = g(z) + (u mod π)`. Any proper colouring of `Cay(𝔽_{q²}, N₁)`, composed
   with `g`, is a proper colouring of the unit-distance graph on `K`. So
   `χ(K) ≤ χ(Cay(𝔽_{q²}, N₁))`.
2. **Ramified.** The norm-one residues are `±1`, and `χ(K) ≤ 3`.

Arcs of the circle (§8) are the special case where the colouring of the finite
plane comes from a single linear form. General colourings of the finite plane
are strictly stronger. At `q = 11` no arc works, yet `χ = 5`.

**Theorem 11.** `χ(ℚ(√−3, √−11, √−247)) = 5`.

*Proof.* Above 11, `K⁺_v = ℚ₁₁(√33)`, with `√741 ∈ ℚ₁₁` because `741 ≡ 4` is a
square mod 11. `K_v = K⁺_v(√−3)` is unramified over `K⁺_v`, because −3 is a
non-residue mod 11, so the place does not split. `Cay(𝔽₁₂₁, N₁)` is
5-colourable (SAT). The graph `five_rho7` gives the lower bound. ∎

The colouring was checked exactly on every grown graph of the project in this
field, up to 32 312 points and 282 909 edges (`scripts/reduce11.py`). On a
finitely generated module it is periodic through a finite 11-group. That is a
quotient the periodic gates never tried: they used Eisenstein ideals and
sampled cyclic groups.

**Corollary (where six can live).** A field hosting a 6-chromatic unit-distance
graph needs two things:
- **no ramified non-split place at all;**
- **every unramified non-split place has `χ ≥ 6` at every level
  `O_v/π^r`**, that is, for its local plane; level 1 is
  `Cay(𝔽_{q²}, N₁)`, and the deeper levels can only need fewer colours.

With `√−3` in `K`, a non-split place has residue field `𝔽_q` with
`q ≡ 2 (mod 3)`, or `q` a power of 3: `ℚ(i, √3)`, for example, has a non-split
place with residue field `𝔽₃`. Computed so far:

| `q` | `χ(Cay(𝔽_{q²}, N₁))` |
|---|---|
| 2 | 4 |
| 3 | 3 |
| 5 | 4 |
| 8 | 4 |
| 11 | 5 |

So the places with these residue fields must split. `ℚ(√−3, √−11, √−247)` fails at 11.
Adjoining `√−7` (de Grey's `4e` rotation) or `√−2` puts `√21` or `√6` in the
real subfield, which splits 11. `ℚ(√−3, √−7, √−11, √−247)` then has no
non-split place below 83.

**Caveats.**
- These are upper bounds from single places.
- Deeper levels `O_v/π^r` and products over several places can only lower
  them.
- Passing every local test does not make a field 6-chromatic. Whether a
  local–global principle holds for these chromatic numbers is open. The
  archimedean place carries `χ(ℝ²)` itself.

## 10. Finite planes and the choice of field

**The finite plane** `G_q = Cay(𝔽_{q²}, N₁)`, with `N₁` the `q + 1` norm-one
elements, is what reduction at a non-split place with residue field `𝔽_q`
lands in (Proposition 10). Next to `√−3`, `q ≡ 2 (mod 3)` or `q` is a power
of 3 (§9).

Its structure, for `q ≡ 5 (mod 6)`:
- **Triangles.** A triangle `0, u, v` of unit steps has `u v̄` a primitive sixth
  root of unity. So every edge lies in exactly two triangles, there is no `K₄`,
  and `G_q` is the union of `(q + 1)/6` rotated triangular tori
  `Cay(𝔽_q[ω], ρ^j μ₆)`.
- **Eigenvalues.** They are `λ(n) = Σ_c (1 − η(c² − 4n)) e(c/q)`, which depend
  only on `n = N(y)`. They are Kloosterman sums, `λ(n) = −Kl(1, n; q)`, and `|λ| ≤ 2√q` by Weil's
  bound (1948, cited in §8 above; `notes/local_colourings.md` §4). The bound was also checked for all
  `q < 400`. For the finite Euclidean planes these spectral facts go back to
  Medrano, Myers, Stark and Terras (J. Comput. Appl. Math. 68, 1996).

**Proposition 12.** For every prime `q ≥ 53`, `χ(G_q) ≥ 6`, and, given the
character-sum estimate sketched in the proof, the same holds for every deeper
quotient `Cay(O_v/π^r, T mod π^r)`.

*Proof.* By Hoffman (A. J. Hoffman, *On eigenvalues and colorings of graphs*, in
*Graph Theory and its Applications* (B. Harris, ed.), Academic Press, 1970, 79–91;
for the ratio form, W. H. Haemers, *Hoffman's ratio bound*, Linear Algebra Appl.
617 (2021) 215–219), `χ_f ≥ 1 + (q+1)/|λ_min| ≥ 1 + (q+1)/(2√q)`. This exceeds
5 for `q > 62`; for `q = 53, 59` and 61 it was computed
(`scripts/finite_hoffman.py --inert`; the first version of this proof omitted
61). A level-`j` character contributes `q^{r−j}` times its level-`j` sum. A
primitive level-`j` character `y′` sums to 0 on every fibre except those over
the residues `±u₀` with `y′u₀ ∈ 𝔽_q`, of which there are zero or two, so its
sum has modulus at most `2q^{j−1}`. Hence the ratio `d/|λ_min|` is the same at
every level. This stationary-phase step is sketched, not written out, and was
checked numerically for small `q` and `j`. ∎

LP methods stop there. The Delsarte bound (P. Delsarte, *An algebraic approach to
the association schemes of coding theory*, Philips Res. Rep. Suppl. 10 (1973))
equals the Hoffman bound, and the
triangle inequalities `f(x) + f(y) − f(x − y) ≤ 1` hardly move it.

Below 53 the numbers are these:

| `q` | `χ(G_q)` | evidence |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 4 | SAT |
| 11 | 5 | SAT |
| 17 | 5 or 6 | a 6-colouring (SAT); no 4-colouring; tabu finds no 5-colouring |
| 23 | ≥ 7? | tabu fails at 6 |
| 29, 41, 47 | ≥ 6 | three-point bound, `α < q²/5` (`notes/local_colourings.md` §14) |

The best independent sets found are 57 points for `q = 17`, against
`289/5 = 57.8`, and 213 for `q = 41`, against `336.2`. SAT did not settle
`α < q²/5`; for `q = 29, 41` and 47 Schrijver's three-point bound does
(`notes/local_colourings.md` §14). For `q = 17` it gives only 63.33, and for
`q = 23` 107.04, where proving `χ ≥ 6` needs 105 (numerical values, not
certified).

**Consequence for the search.** `ℚ(√−3, √−7, √−11)` splits 2, 3, 5, 7 and 11,
and its non-split places lie above 17, 41, 83, 101, …. It contains a
5-chromatic graph, `data/five_tuned_16_1_3_7_11.json`. No single place excludes
it at level 1 if `χ(G₁₇) ≥ 6` (`χ(G₄₁) ≥ 6` is proved in
`notes/local_colourings.md` §14). The deeper levels at 17 and 41 are open: they
map onto `G₁₇` and `G₄₁` and may need fewer colours, and the three-point bound
covers level 1 only. `ℚ(√−3, √−7, √2717)` and `ℚ(√−3, √−7, √−11, √−247)`
have no non-split place below 53, so they pass at every level given
Proposition 12, but have no Moser spindle and rank 16 respectively.

## 11. The arithmetic ladder

Every realisation of a `k`-chromatic unit-distance graph lives in some field
`K = L(i)`. The field `L` is real and generated by the coordinates, and the
unit vectors are the norm-one elements of `K/L`.

By Proposition 10, `K` then has **no non-split place whose local chromatic
number is below `k`**. This constrains *every* realisation of the graph, not a
chosen one. So each rung of the ladder needs certain primes to split, and
splitting a prime means adjoining a rotation with that prime in its
denominator:

| rung | places that must not stay non-split | how the known constructions split them |
|---|---|---|
| `k = 4` | ramified non-split places (`χ_v ≤ 3`), and those with residue field `𝔽₃` | Moser's `(5 + √−11)/6` kills 3-adic `ℚ(√−3)` |
| `k = 5` | also those with `χ_v = 4`: residue field `𝔽₂`, `𝔽₅` or `𝔽₈` next to `√−3` | de Grey's `√−15` and `√−7` rotations and the `√247` spindle split 2; 5 splits in `ℚ(√−11)` |
| `k = 6` | also those with `χ_v = 5`, such as residue field `𝔽₁₁` | `√−7` or `√−2` in the real subfield's reach: `√21` or `√6` is a non-residue unit at 11 |
| `k = 7` | also 17 if `χ(G₁₇) = 6`, and every `q` with `χ(G_q) = 6` | — |

The history of the problem fits this pattern:
- **Moser to de Grey.** Going from 4 to 5 needed a 2 in a denominator. The
  Moser field stops at 4 because of the 2-adic character.
- **The present search.** It stalled at 5 because its field keeps 11 non-split.
- **The fields left for six.** De Grey's own field
  `ℚ(√−3, √−7, √−11, √−15)` splits 2, 5 and 11; its first non-split place is
  41. The field `ℚ(√−3, √−7, √−11)` has first non-split places 17 and 41.

**Question.** Is `χ(Γ(K))` equal to the minimum of the local chromatic numbers
over the non-split places, whenever that minimum is at most 6? It holds in
every case computed:

| field | `χ(Γ(K))` | where the minimum is attained |
|---|---|---|
| `ℚ(i)` | 2 | at 2 |
| `ℚ(√−3)` | 3 | at 3 |
| Moser | 4 | at 2 |
| `ℚ(√−3, √−11, √−247)` | 5 | at 11 |
| `ℚ(√−3, √−11, √−23)` | 5 | at 11 |

If it held in general, `ℚ(√−3, √−7, √−11)` would be 6-chromatic as soon as
the local planes at 17 and 41 need six colours at every level (so far
`χ(G₄₁) ≥ 6` is proved at level 1, `notes/local_colourings.md` §14, and
`χ(G₁₇)` is 5 or 6), and then `χ(ℝ²) ≥ 6`. Nothing here proves that; the
growth run is the experiment.
