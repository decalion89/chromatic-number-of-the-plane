# Three colours and characters: the winding lemma

*Working note, 3 October 2026. Theorem W and its proof are ours; we have not found them in the literature
(§5 lists what we checked). Nobody outside the project has refereed this note.*

## 1. The statement

Let `Γ` be an abelian group, written additively, and `S = −S` a subset of `Γ ∖ {0}`. The Cayley graph
`Cay(Γ, S)` has vertex set `Γ` and an edge `{g, g + s}` for every `g ∈ Γ` and `s ∈ S`. A *character* is a
homomorphism `ξ : Γ → ℝ/ℤ`. For `x ∈ ℝ/ℤ` write `‖x‖` for the distance to `0`.

> **Theorem W.** Let `k ≥ 1`. `Cay(Γ, S)` has a homomorphism to the odd cycle `C_{2k+1}` if and only if some
> character `ξ` satisfies `ξ(s) ∈ [k/(2k+1), (k+1)/(2k+1)]` (modulo 1) for every `s ∈ S`.
>
> In particular (`k = 1`, `C₃ = K₃`): **`Cay(Γ, S)` is 3-colourable if and only if some character maps every
> `s ∈ S` into the closed arc `[1/3, 2/3]`.**

A homomorphism to `C_{2k+1}` is the same as a circular `(2k+1)/k`-colouring, so the theorem says, with
`κ(S) = max_ξ min_{s∈S} ‖ξ(s)‖` (the maximum exists by compactness of the dual group):

> `χ_c(Cay(Γ, S)) ≤ 2 + 1/k` if and only if `κ(S) ≥ k/(2k+1)`; in particular `χ ≤ 3` if and only if `κ(S) ≥ 1/3`.

The inequality `χ_c ≤ 1/κ` is classical for distance graphs (see D. D.-F. Liu, "From Rainbow to Lonely Runner: A
Survey on Coloring Parameters of Distance Graphs", §2). The content of Theorem W is the converse at the thresholds `2 + 1/k`, which include 3.

## 2. Proof

*If.* Put `n = 2k + 1` and `c(g) = 2⌊n ξ(g)⌋ mod n`. Along an edge, `n ξ` moves by an amount in `[k, k + 1]`, so
`⌊n ξ⌋` moves by `k` or `k + 1`, and `c` by `2k ≡ −1` or `2k + 2 ≡ 1` (mod `n`): `c` is a homomorphism to `C_n`.

*Only if.* Let `c : Γ → ℤ/n` be a homomorphism to `C_n` (adjacent vertices get colours differing by `±1`). It
suffices to treat the subgroup `Γ'` generated by `S` (a character of `Γ'` extends to `Γ`, as `ℝ/ℤ` is divisible),
and `Cay(Γ', S)` is connected. For `g ∈ Γ'` and `s ∈ S` let `σ(g, s) ∈ {1, −1}` be the lift of `c(g + s) − c(g)`.
Then `σ(g + s, −s) = −σ(g, s)`.

1. *Squares do not wind.* For `s, t ∈ S`, both `σ(g, s) + σ(g + s, t)` and `σ(g, t) + σ(g + t, s)` lie in
   `{−2, 0, 2}` and are congruent to `c(g + s + t) − c(g)` modulo the odd number `n ≥ 3`; so they are equal.
2. *Winding.* For a closed walk `W` from `g` with steps `s₁, …, s_N` (so `Σ s_j = 0`), the sum
   `Σ_j σ(g + s₁ + … + s_{j−1}, s_j)` is `≡ 0 (mod n)`; call it `n·ω(W)`. By (1) it does not change when two
   consecutive steps are swapped, nor when a step `s` followed by `−s` is deleted; hence `ω(W)` depends only on the
   element `Σ_j [s_j]` of the group `Z` generated by symbols `[s]` with `[−s] = −[s]`, and (conjugating by a step)
   not on the base point. So `ω` is a homomorphism from `R = ker(Z → Γ')` to `ℤ`, and `ω(r) ≡ N (mod 2)` for a
   relation realised by a walk of length `N`, since `n ω` is a sum of `N` odd numbers.
3. *Averaging.* The homomorphisms `Γ' → C_n` form a non-empty compact space `X`
   on which `Γ'` acts by translation. `Γ'` is abelian, hence amenable, so `X` carries a translation-invariant
   probability measure, and an extreme one `μ` is ergodic. `ω_c(r)` is a translation-invariant function of `c`,
   so it is `μ`-almost surely a constant `ω(r)`. Let `f(s) = ∫ σ_c(0, s) dμ(c) ∈ [−1, 1]`. By invariance
   `∫ σ_c(g, s) dμ = f(s)` for every `g`, and integrating the identity in (2) gives
   `Σ_j f(s_j) = n·ω(r)` for every relation.
4. *The character.* Put `ξ(s) = (f(s)/n + 1)/2 ∈ [k/n, (k + 1)/n]`. Then `ξ(−s) = 1 − ξ(s)`, and for a relation
   realised by a walk of length `N`, `Σ_j ξ(s_j) = (ω(r) + N)/2 ∈ ℤ` by the parity in (2). So `ξ` is additive modulo
   1 on `Z` and vanishes on `R`: it is a character of `Γ' = Z/R`, with `ξ(S) ⊆ [k/n, (k + 1)/n]`. ∎

The argument is the height-function picture of 3-colourings of the square lattice (squares have zero height
change) combined with an ergodic average of the slopes. Squares not winding is special to odd cycles, and the
analogue for four colours fails: `K₄ = Cay((ℤ/2)², (ℤ/2)² ∖ {0})` is 4-colourable, but no character of `(ℤ/2)²`
maps its three generators into `[1/4, 3/4]`.

## 3. Consequences

**Groups of small exponent.** If `Γ` has exponent `m`, characters take values in `(1/m)ℤ/ℤ`. For `m = 2` and `m = 4`
the only such value in `[1/3, 2/3]` is `1/2`, and a character with `ξ(S) = {1/2}` makes `⌊2ξ⌋` a proper
2-colouring. So:

- (Payan, 1992) a cube-like graph (a Cayley graph of `(ℤ/2)ⁿ`) is never 3-chromatic;
- (Krebs and Sankar, arXiv:2410.11028, JCTB 2026, the exponent-4 part of their main theorem) a Cayley graph of an
  abelian group of exponent 4 is never 3-chromatic.

Both papers need substantial arguments (Payan's original proof; a matrix method in Cervantes–Krebs 2023; the
neighbourhood complex in Krebs–Sankar). Theorem W gives them in two lines, for infinite groups too.

**Distance graphs.** For `D ⊂ ℤ_{>0}` finite, `G(ℤ, D)` is 3-colourable if and only if `κ(D) ≥ 1/3`, where
`κ(D) = max_α min_{d∈D} ‖αd‖`; the maximum is attained at an endpoint `α = (j + 1/3)/d` or `(j + 2/3)/d`, so this is
a finite check. `data/quadratic_planes/winding/distgraph_test.py` compares it, for all 1 747 sets
`D ⊆ [1, 24]` with `|D| = 3` and `gcd(D) = 1`, with 3-colourability of the segment `[0, 12 max D + 60]` (SAT):
they agree, and the 74 sets that need four colours are exactly Zhu's list (`D = {1, 2, 3m}` or `D = {a, b, a + b}`
with `a ≢ b (mod 3)`). `circ_test.py` checks the theorem on 3 888 random Cayley graphs of `ℤ/m × ℤ/n`.

**Planes over number fields.** Let `F` be a field with `i ∉ F`, `L = F(i)`, `T ⊂ L` the unit vectors
(`x + iy` with `x² + y² = 1`) and `A = ℤ[T]`. The plane `F²` is a disjoint union of translates of `Cay(A, T)`,
so:

> `χ(F²) ≤ 3` if and only if some character of `A` maps every unit vector into `[1/3, 2/3]`.

For a finite set `U` of unit vectors the group `ℤU` is free of rank at most `[L : ℚ]`, so the test for `U` is a
question about a torus of dimension `[L : ℚ]` (4 for a real quadratic `F`): if no character of `ℤU` maps `U` into
`[1/3, 2/3]`, then `Cay(ℤU, U)` is not 3-colourable and, by the de Bruijn–Erdős theorem, neither is some finite
unit-distance graph over `F`. A locally constant 3-colouring at a place (`notes/local_global.md`) colours a finite
level, and Theorem W applied to that level gives such a character (for instance `ξ(t) = ±1/3` at level 1 above 3).

## 4. New values: `ℚ(√83)`, `ℚ(√107)`, `ℚ(√143)`, `ℚ(√167)`, `ℚ(√203)`

`notes/local_global.md` (§3) lists `d = 83, 107, 143, 167, 203` as the values `d ≡ 11 (mod 12)` below 210 that the
growth searches could not settle. `ℚ(√83)` is the smallest (`docs/research-log.md`): with `D = 510`, for example,
colouring-guided growth stops at 6 008 points because a 3-colouring of the 2-ball around the origin extends to all
215 478 candidate points. The obstruction is not local.

For each of the five fields we take the unit vectors `((a + b√d)/D, (c + e√d)/D)` for one denominator `D`, one per
pair `±u`, and the integer relations among them (an LLL-reduced basis). A character with `ξ(U) ⊆ [1/3, 2/3]` would
give `f ∈ [1/3, 2/3]^U` with `⟨r, f⟩ ∈ ℤ` for every basis relation `r`. `certify_w2.py` rules this out by branch and
bound over the integers `⟨r, f⟩`; each leaf carries a rational Farkas vector showing that its equations have no
solution in the box. `check_w.py` checks a certificate in integer and rational arithmetic only: the vectors have
length 1 (`a² + d b² + c² + d e² = D²`, `ab + ce = 0`), each relation sums to zero, every branch covers every integer
value in its range, and every Farkas vector is valid. Using a basis of the relations, or any set of valid relations,
only weakens the conditions, so infeasibility is still a proof.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 83 | 510 | 54 | 50 | 6 886 | 4 664 | `cert_83_510_full.json.gz` | 4 |
| 107 | 1170 | 90 | 86 | 12 785 | 9 618 | `cert_107_1170.json.gz` | 4 |
| 143 | 1740 | 54 | 50 | 3 861 | 2 710 | `cert_143_1740.json.gz` | ≥ 4 |

| 203 | 1530 | 78 | 74 | 1 072 | 732 | `cert_203_1530.json.gz` | 4 |

So `Cay(ℤU, U)` is not 3-colourable and `χ(ℚ(√d)²) ≥ 4` in all five cases. For `d = 83, 107, 203`, `d ≡ 3 (mod 8)`,
so `χ(ℚ(√d)²) ≤ 4` (Fischer 1990, Theorem 10; `notes/quadratic_planes.md`, §1) and the value is 4. For `d = 143`
the place above 11 (ramified, with residue field `𝔽₁₁` and `i` outside it) reduces the unit circle to that of the
anisotropic plane over `𝔽₁₁`, which is 5-colourable, so `4 ≤ χ(ℚ(√143)²) ≤ 5`, as for `d = 47`. `ℚ(√167)` is the
first admissible field: no place gives a locally constant 4-colouring (`notes/local_global.md`, §4); we have not
looked for an upper bound below 7. With
`d = 83` and `107`, `χ(ℚ(√d)²)` is known for every squarefree `d < 143` except `d = 47`. For `d = 203` the result also
shows that no level of the place above 7 gives a 3-colouring, which `notes/local_global.md` had left open beyond level 3.

For `d = 83`, 28 of the 54 vectors already suffice (`min_83_510.json`; its certificate has 98 300 nodes and is checked
too, not stored). For `ℚ(√11)` with `D = 30` the test needs 16 vectors (`cert_11_30.json.gz`, 1 115 nodes), which
agrees with the known 76-vertex graph. Explicit finite graphs are not given for the five new fields: the averaging in
step 3 suggests that finite witnesses with these directions are large, which would explain why the growth searches
failed.

## 5. Checks and literature

- **Controls.** For 40 pairs `(d, D)` with `d ∈ {2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 15, 19, 31, 39, 43}` (fields known to be 2- or
  3-colourable) the test is feasible, as it must be. The floating-point test (`kapparel.py`) was infeasible for
  several other denominators of the five fields as well (`d = 107`: 1590, 1716, 1794; `143`: 1020, 1248, 1260, 1272,
  1440; `167`: 840, 1320, 1392, 1440, 1680, 1740), and `D = 1105` (no factor 2 or 3) is feasible for all of them, as it
  must be: those vectors are 2-integral, and the place above 2 then gives a 2-colouring of `Cay(ℤU, U)`.
- **Scan.** `scan_all.py` runs the test, with certificates, over every squarefree `d ≡ 11 (mod 12)` in a range; the
  runs up to 2000 are in progress.
- **Literature.** We looked for Theorem W in the work on 3-colourings of abelian Cayley graphs (Payan; Cervantes and
  Krebs, arXiv:2303.06262 and 2303.06267; Krebs and Sankar, arXiv:2410.11028; García-Marco, Knauer and Menara,
  arXiv:2607.26942), in the distance-graph literature through Liu's survey (where `χ_c ≤ 1/κ` and the question
  `χ_c = 1/κ` for `|D| = 3` appear, Problems 2 and 3), and in web searches on characters, circular colourings and
  odd-cycle homomorphisms of Cayley graphs. We did not find it. The proof uses only classical tools, so an earlier
  occurrence is possible; we would be glad to learn of one.
