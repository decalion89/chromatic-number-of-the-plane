# The anisotropic plane over `𝔽₁₃` has no 37 independent points

`G₁₃` is the graph on `𝔽₁₃²` in which `z ~ w` when `N(z − w) = 1`, where
`N(x, y) = x² − 2y²`. The form is anisotropic, since 2 is not a square
modulo 13. `G₁₃` is the finite plane `G_q` of `local_colourings.md` §4 for
`q = 13`. This note proves:

**Theorem.** `α(G₁₃) = 36`. Since `G₁₃` is vertex-transitive, its fractional
chromatic number is `χ_f(G₁₃) = 169/36`.

The proof is by computer: SAT solving with checked proofs. The note states the
argument, what the computer checked, how to check it again, and what has to be
trusted. The files are listed in §8. `docs/research-log.md` tells how the proof
was found.

This is a computer proof. Nobody outside the project has refereed it.

The theorem does not decide `χ(G₁₃)`, which is 5 or 6 (§7): five classes of 36
points would hold `180 ≥ 169` vertices, so the count that a bound on `α` gives
falls short here.

`G₁₃` is not the finite Euclidean plane `𝔽₁₃²` of Moorhouse (*On the chromatic
numbers of planes*, draft of 3 March 2010, Table 6.1), whose unit circle is
`x² + y² = 1`. That form is isotropic modulo 13, and each vertex there has 12
neighbours, not 14. We have not found a value of `α(G₁₃)` in the literature:
Vinh (Electron. J. Combin. 15 (2008), R5, Theorem 12) bounds the independence
number of these graphs only asymptotically, and Vinh's note on unit-quadrance
graphs (arXiv:math/0510092) computes a chromatic number only for the Euclidean
plane over `𝔽₇`.

## 1. The graph

- `G₁₃` has 169 vertices and 14 neighbours per vertex, so 1 183 edges. The code
  writes the vertex `(x, y)` as `v = 13x + y`.
- For `c ≠ 0` the *circle* `C_c = {z : N(z) = c}` has 14 points. It contains
  no unit distance for exactly five values, `c = 1, 6, 7, 9, 11`: the
  *independent circles*. (For `c = 1`: two unit vectors `u`, `w` at unit
  distance would make `u w̄` a unit vector of trace 1, a primitive sixth root of
  unity, and 6 does not divide 14. So `G₁₃` has no triangles.)
- Identify `𝔽₁₃²` with `𝔽₁₃(√2)`, so that `N` is the field norm. The maps
  `z ↦ λz + a` and `z ↦ λz̄ + a` with `N(λ) = 1` preserve `N(z − w)`. So they
  are automorphisms, and they map every circle around a point `p` onto the
  circle of the same `c` around the image of `p`. There are `169 · 28 = 4 732`
  of them.

## 2. 36 points are independent

**Lemma 1.** The 15 sets `KNOWN_G13` of
`scripts/experiments/largest_sets_whole_circles.py` are independent sets of 36
points, in 15 different orbits of the automorphisms.

*Proof.* `tests/test_g13.py` checks the pairs of each set, and
`python3 scripts/experiments/largest_sets_whole_circles.py 13 --known` checks
that no two of them lie in the same orbit. ∎

So `α(G₁₃) ≥ 36`. A proper 6-colouring, found by SAT, is stored in
`data/small_plane_colourings.json`, so `χ(G₁₃) ≤ 6`.

## 3. No 37 points are independent

Let `S` be an independent set of 37 points, and extend it to a maximal
independent set `S′`. Then `S′` has at least 37 points, and it is
*dominating*: every vertex is in `S′` or adjacent to a point of `S′`. There
are two cases.

**Case A: `S′` contains a point `p` together with a whole circle `p + C_c`,
for some `c` in `{6, 7, 9, 11}`.** (No independent set contains a point with
its unit circle, which is its neighbourhood.) Translate `p` to 0. The image of
`S′` is a solution of the formula `E37_A_c`: an independent and dominating set
of at least 37 vertices, containing 0 and every point of `C_c`. kissat finds
each of the four formulas unsatisfiable, and drat-trim verifies each proof
(`certificates/g13_alpha_part_a_checks.txt`). So Case A is impossible.

**Case B: otherwise.** Then `S′` satisfies the clauses of *formula E37_B*
other than its lex-leader clauses: it is an independent and dominating set of
at least 37 vertices that contains no point together with its whole circle
`C_c`, `c ∈ {6, 7, 9, 11}`.

**Lemma 2 (symmetry).** Formula E37_B has these clauses and, in addition,
lex-leader clauses for the 4 731 automorphisms other than the identity, on the
first 25 vertices of `lex_order()`. If an independent dominating set as in
Case B exists, formula E37_B has a solution.

*Proof.* Each automorphism maps independent dominating sets to independent
dominating sets of the same size, and maps a point with its whole circle `C_c`
to another such pair, so it maps the solutions of the other clauses to
solutions. Order the vertices as `lex_order()` does, and let `T` be the image
of `S′` whose 0/1 vector, read on the first 25 vertices, is lex-greatest among
all the images. For each automorphism `g`, the vector of `g⁻¹(T)` is one of
them, so its first 25 entries are not greater than those of `T`. The
lex-leader clauses of `g` say exactly this (Crawford, Ginsberg, Luks and Roy
1996). ∎

**The cube tree.** The file `certificates/g13_alpha_part_b_cubes.icnf` lists
4 822 *leaves*: partial assignments to the vertex variables of formula E37_B,
made by `scripts/g13/cuber2.py` with at most 14 decisions along
`lex_order()`. They are the leaves of a binary decision tree, so every
assignment satisfies exactly one of them (`check_cover()` in
`scripts/g13/cuber2.py`). Unit propagation already refutes 564 of them (the
lines `c closed`); they are certified like the others. For every leaf, kissat
finds formula E37_B plus the leaf unsatisfiable, and drat-trim verifies its
DRAT proof (`certificates/g13_alpha_part_b_checks.txt.gz`). So formula E37_B
is unsatisfiable, and by Lemma 2 Case B is impossible too.

Hence `α(G₁₃) ≤ 36`, and with Lemma 1, `α(G₁₃) = 36`. For a vertex-transitive
graph the fractional chromatic number is the number of vertices divided by the
independence number (E. R. Scheinerman and D. H. Ullman, *Fractional graph
theory*, Wiley, 1997, Proposition 3.1.1), so `χ_f(G₁₃) = 169/36`. ∎

## 4. The formulas say what they should

A refutation shows that a formula is unsatisfiable. For the argument above, each
formula must be satisfiable whenever its configuration exists: every clause
must hold once the auxiliary variables get their intended values.

- **Independence, domination and circles.** The clause `¬x_u ∨ ¬x_v` for each
  edge; the clause `x_v ∨ x_{w₁} ∨ … ∨ x_{w₁₄}` for each vertex `v` and its
  neighbours, which holds when the set is dominating; in formula E37_B, the
  clause `¬x_p ∨ ¬x_{p+q₁} ∨ … ∨ ¬x_{p+q₁₄}` for each point `p` and each
  circle `C_c = {q₁, …, q₁₄}`, `c ∈ {6, 7, 9, 11}`; in `E37_A_c`, the unit
  clauses `x_0` and `x_q`, `q ∈ C_c`.
- **Cardinality: the totalizer** (Bailleux and Boufkhad 2003). Each node `p` of
  a binary tree over the vertex variables has outputs `o_j(p)`, "at least `j`
  of the variables below `p` are true", for `j` up to `min(37, size)`. Its
  clauses are `o_{a+1}(L) ∨ o_{b+1}(R) ∨ ¬o_{a+b+1}(p)` for the children `L` and
  `R`, and the unit clause `o₃₇(root)`. Give every output its intended value.
  If `p` has at least `a + b + 1` true variables below it, then `L` has
  `a + 1` or `R` has `b + 1`, so every clause holds as soon as 37 vertices are
  true.
- **Symmetry: lex-leader chains.** For an automorphism `g`, a chain compares
  `x_v` with `x_{g(v)}` along the first 25 vertices of `lex_order()`, skipping
  those that `g` fixes. Its variable `e_i` says that the first `i` pairs agree.
  With `e_i` given that value, the chain holds exactly when the vector, read on
  those 25 vertices, is lex-greater than or equal to that of the image
  `g⁻¹(S)`.

`tests/test_g13.py` checks these encodings by brute force against their
definitions (`scripts/g13/g13cnf.py`): the totalizer for every set of up to
seven variables and every threshold; the chains for 400 random permutations of
up to seven points; and the chains of the 4 732 automorphisms themselves, on
the images of three sets: an image satisfies them exactly when its first 25
entries are the greatest in its orbit.

`scripts/g13/g13_audit.py` checks the formulas themselves, clause by clause,
from the DIMACS text of each and without the code that wrote it:

- it rebuilds `G₁₃`, its circles and its 4 732 automorphisms from the
  definitions, and checks that every edge clause is an edge, every positive
  clause of 15 vertices the closed neighbourhood of a vertex, every negative one
  a point with its whole circle `C_c`, `c ∈ {6, 7, 9, 11}`, and that the unit
  clauses of `E37_A_c` are exactly those of `{0} ∪ C_c`;
- it rebuilds the totalizer's tree from the clauses, and checks that the leaves
  are the 169 vertex variables, each once, and that every node has exactly the
  clauses above;
- it reads the 4 731 chains of E37_B off the clauses, checks that they visit
  their positions in one common order of 25 vertices, and finds for each chain
  an automorphism that sends every vertex it compares to its partner and fixes
  the vertices it skips. Each of these maps sends the edge, domination and
  circle clauses onto themselves, so the argument of Lemma 2 holds for the group
  they generate.

`tests/test_g13_audit.py` runs the audit on the five formulas, and checks that
it rejects formulas with one wrong clause: a non-edge, a moved circle point, a
changed counter clause or threshold, a chain whose map is not an automorphism,
and others.

## 5. What was computed

| step | formulas | solver | proof check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Case A | 4 | kissat 4.0.4, under a second each | drat-trim; cake_lpr |
| Case B | 4 822 leaves | kissat 4.0.4, 3.4 hours in all, at most 93 s on one leaf | drat-trim, 3.1 hours; cake_lpr on 40 leaves chosen at random |
| the cover | 1: the negated leaves | none: a resolution proof, written by `scripts/verify_g13.py` | cake_lpr |

- Every formula is rebuilt by the code, and `tests/test_g13.py` checks that its
  SHA-256 is the one in the log. The DRAT proofs are not stored: they took 22
  gigabytes. `scripts/verify_g13.py` has kissat write them again.
- The run took 1 hour 41 minutes on one machine with four solver jobs, on 27
  September 2026, as one share of a larger computation (§7). Every leaf of Case
  B was refuted within kissat's limit of 120 s, so no leaf had to be split
  further.
- cake_lpr (Tan, Heule and Myreen) is a proof checker verified in the HOL4
  theorem prover and compiled by the verified CakeML compiler. drat-trim
  converts a DRAT proof into an LRAT proof, and cake_lpr checks that, so for
  those proofs drat-trim does not have to be trusted. cake_lpr checked the four
  proofs of Case A, the proof for the cover formula, and those of 40
  leaves of Case B chosen at random (`certificates/g13_cake_lpr_checks.txt`).
  For the other leaves, the check on record is drat-trim's.

## 6. How to check it, and what has to be trusted

With kissat and drat-trim installed (`scripts/worker_setup.sh`), and optionally
cake_lpr:

    python3 -m pytest tests/test_g13.py tests/test_g13_audit.py      # a minute
    python3 scripts/verify_g13.py --no-solve                         # seconds
    python3 scripts/verify_g13.py --kissat kissat --drat-trim drat-trim \
        [--cake-lpr cake_lpr] --jobs 4                               # about 2 hours

The last command solves every formula again, has every proof checked, compares
every formula with the logs, and ends with `CONFIRMED` only if all of it holds.
`--sample 100` solves 100 random leaves of Case B in a few minutes: a spot
check, not a proof. `--shard K/N` splits the work between machines.

What has to be trusted:

- **The argument** of §3 and §4, which is short and uses no computation beyond
  the formulas.
- **The audit and the cover check**: `scripts/g13/g13_audit.py` (about 480
  lines) and `check_cover()` in `scripts/g13/cuber2.py` (a dozen lines). The
  code that writes the formulas does not have to be trusted: the audit reads
  the formulas themselves.
- **A proof checker**: drat-trim for every proof, and cake_lpr as well for the
  proofs of §5 that it checked. cake_lpr is verified, but its small C wrapper,
  the compiler toolchain and the hardware are not.
- **Python and SHA-256**, which tie each line of the logs to its formula.

This proof has no formal version in Lean.

## 7. Five or six colours?

`χ(G₁₃)` is 5 or 6. There is no 4-colouring, since the three-point bound gives
`α ≤ 42 < 169/4` (`local_colourings.md` §14); the stored colouring uses six.
A 5-colouring would have classes of at most 36 points summing to 169, so its
largest class would have 34, 35 or 36 points (`5 · 33 < 169`). Tabu search
finds no 5-colouring. Deciding the question by the method of §3 takes one
formula for each of these three sizes, with the symmetry broken on the largest
class; the research log follows that computation.
