\begin{abstract}
For a field $F$ of characteristic $0$ let $\chi(F^2)$ be the chromatic number of the graph on $F^2$ that joins $x$
and $y$ when $(x_1-y_1)^2+(x_2-y_2)^2=1$. We prove that for every number field $F$, $\chi(F^2)\le3$ if and only if
some prime of $F$ above $2$ ramifies in $F(i)$ or some prime of $F$ above $3$ has residue degree $1$. The ``if'' part
is known (colourings through a residue field); the ``only if'' part extends to all number fields our earlier
result that $\chi(\Q(\sqrt d)^2)\ge4$ for $d\equiv11\pmod{12}$. It answers, for three colours, the question whether
the chromatic number of the plane over a number field is decided by a single completion, and it classifies the
finite extensions $K$ of $\Q_p$: $\chi(K^2)$ is $2$, $3$ or at least $4$ according to explicit local conditions. We
also give an elementary family (every real field containing $\sqrt a$ and $\sqrt b$ with $a\equiv2\pmod 3$ and
$b\equiv7\pmod8$ needs four colours), new examples such as $\chi(\Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt7)^2)=4$, and, as far as we know,
the first non-integral value of the circular chromatic number of the plane over a number field:
$\chi_c(\Q(\sqrt{11})^2)=\chi_c(\Q(\sqrt{35})^2)=7/2$ (and $\chi_c(\Q_7^2)=7/2$), although these planes have chromatic
number $4$. Below $4$, the best upper bound on $\chi_c$ that a locally constant colouring of one completion gives is
$2$, $3$ or $7/2$. We show that these are the only values below $4$: for every number field $F$, $\chi_c(F^2)$ is $2$, $3$,
$7/2$ or at least $4$, and it is $7/2$ exactly when $\chi(F^2)\ge4$ and some prime of $F$ above $7$ has residue
degree $1$; below $4$ it is the least circular chromatic number of the planes over the completions of $F$, and it is
the circular chromatic number of some finite unit-distance graph in $F^2$; so is the value $4$, when it occurs (for
example over $\Q(\sqrt{59})$), as at four colours the winding argument fails only on tight $4$-cycles. The proof of this
classification has one computer-assisted step, an exact finite computation along the rational rotations whose denominators divide $325$;
without it we show that $\chi_c(F^2)\ge56/17$ whenever $\chi(F^2)\ge4$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Let $F$ be a field of characteristic $0$. The \emph{plane over $F$} is the graph on $F^2$ in which $x$ and $y$ are
adjacent when $(x_1-y_1)^2+(x_2-y_2)^2=1$, and $\chi(F^2)$ is its chromatic number. For $F=\R$ this is the
Hadwiger--Nelson problem, and $5\le\chi(\R^2)\le7$: the lower bound is due to de Grey \cite{deGrey} and to Exoo and
Ismailescu \cite{EI}, the upper bound to Isbell (see \cite{Soifer}). Over number fields the question goes back to
Woodall ($\chi(\Q^2)=2$ \cite{Woodall}) and was studied by Johnson \cite{Johnson1987}, Fischer \cite{Fischer1990},
Benda and Perles \cite{BP}, Moorhouse \cite{Moorhouse} and Madore \cite{Madore}. All upper bounds below $7$ known to us come from a colouring
that is locally constant at one completion $F_v$: reduction modulo a power of a prime. Writing $L=F(i)$, the unit
vectors of $F^2$ are the elements of norm one of $L$, and at a place where $x^2+y^2$ is anisotropic they reduce into
a finite group of roots of unity of the residue field. Two such reductions give colourings with at most three colours:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(a)] at a prime of $F$ above $2$ that ramifies in $F(i)$, every unit vector is $\equiv1$ modulo the maximal
ideal, which gives a $2$-colouring (\cite[Theorem~A$'$]{MM}, \cite[Proposition~3]{TC}; earlier, for special classes of
fields, Fischer \cite[Thm.~8]{Fischer1990}, Moorhouse \cite{Moorhouse} and Johnson \cite{Johnson1987}, whose paper we
have not seen);
\item[(b)] at a prime of $F$ above $3$ of residue degree $1$, the unit vectors reduce into $\mu_4\subset\F_9$, and
$a+bi\mapsto a+b$ gives a $3$-colouring (Madore \cite[Cor.~3.4]{Madore}; for real quadratic fields Moorhouse
\cite[Lemma~8.2 and Cor.~8.3]{Moorhouse} and Fischer \cite[Thm.~9]{Fischer1990}).
\end{itemize}
In \cite{TC} we proved that (a) is also necessary for $\chi(F^2)=2$; we call that result Theorem~A, which is why
there is no Theorem~A here. Here we prove the same at three colours.

\setcounter{theorem}{1}
\begin{theorem}\label{thm:B}
Let $F$ be a number field. Then $\chi(F^2)\le3$ if and only if (a) or (b) holds; and $\chi(F^2)\le2$ if and only
if (a) holds.
\end{theorem}

For real quadratic fields $\Q(\sqrt d)$, $d$ squarefree, (a) holds iff $d\not\equiv3\pmod4$ and (b) iff
$d\not\equiv2\pmod3$, so Theorem~\ref{thm:B} contains the main result of \cite{FC} (Theorem~1 and Corollary~2
there): $\chi(\Q(\sqrt d)^2)\ge4$ iff $d\equiv11\pmod{12}$. Whether (a) or (b) holds depends only on $F\otimes\Q_2$ and $F\otimes\Q_3$, and is computed from
a defining polynomial (the relative discriminant of $F(i)/F$, and the residue degrees above $3$). By the theorem of
de Bruijn and Erd\H{o}s \cite{dBE}, $\chi(F^2)\ge4$ if and only if some finite unit-distance graph in $F^2$ needs four
colours, and Theorem~\ref{thm:B} says that this happens unless one of the completions of $F$ has a locally constant
$3$-colouring: a local--global principle for three colours. The same proof decides the local fields
(Corollary~\ref{cor:local}): for a finite extension $K$ of $\Q_p$, $\chi(K^2)=2$ if $p=2$ and $K(i)/K$ is ramified,
$\chi(K^2)=3$ if $p=3$ and $K$ has residue degree $1$, and $\chi(K^2)\ge4$ otherwise; for instance the plane over the
unramified cubic extension of $\Q_3$ needs at least four colours, although $x^2+y^2$ is anisotropic there, as over
$\Q_3$.

Besides the fields that contain $i$, whose planes need infinitely many colours, two families of fields were known to
need four colours: those containing $\sqrt d$ with $d\equiv11\pmod{12}$ \cite{FC}, and those containing $\sqrt3$ and
$\sqrt q$ with $q\equiv2\pmod3$, by chains of unit rhombi (\cite{Repo}, \nolinkurl{notes/local_colourings.md},
Section~11, Theorem~5). They contain $\Q(\sqrt3,\sqrt{11})$ (both families), the field of the Moser spindle, whose
plane has chromatic number $4$ \cite{Fischer1994,MM}, and $\Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$ (the second), whose plane has chromatic
number $4$ \cite{P4C}. Theorem
\ref{thm:B} adds, for example, $\Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt7)$, $\Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt{31})$ and $\Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt{55})$, which contain
no unit triangle and whose quadratic subfields all have $\chi\le3$, and $\Q(2\cos\frac{2\pi}7,\sqrt7)$, of degree
$6$, where $3$ has residue degree $3$. For many fields an explicit proof is short (Proposition~\ref{prop:two}), and
for $\Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt7)$ we give a certificate checked by computer that does not depend on the general argument.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:C}
$\chi_c(\Q(\sqrt{11})^2)=\chi_c(\Q(\sqrt{35})^2)=\chi_c(\Q_7^2)=7/2$, while the three chromatic numbers are $4$.
Moreover no plane over a number field has circular chromatic number strictly between $2$ and $3$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:D}
Let $F$ be a number field with $\chi(F^2)\ge4$. Then $\chi_c(F^2)\ge56/17\approx3.294$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:E}
Let $F$ be a number field with $\chi(F^2)\ge4$. Then $\chi_c(F^2)\ge7/2$. So $\chi_c(F^2)\in\{2,3\}\cup[7/2,\infty]$
for every number field $F$, and the value $7/2$ is attained.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:F}
Let $F$ be a number field, and consider the condition
\begin{itemize}
\item[(7)] some prime of $F$ above $7$ has residue degree $1$.
\end{itemize}
Then $\chi_c(F^2)=2$ if \textup{(a)} holds; $\chi_c(F^2)=3$ if \textup{(b)} holds and \textup{(a)} fails;
$\chi_c(F^2)=7/2$ if \textup{(7)} holds and \textup{(a)}, \textup{(b)} fail; and $\chi_c(F^2)\ge4$ if \textup{(a)},
\textup{(b)} and \textup{(7)} all fail.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:E} rests on one computer-assisted step (Proposition~\ref{prop:window}), and Theorem~\ref{thm:F}
on another (Proposition~\ref{prop:window7}), which also gives Theorem~\ref{thm:E} again: exact finite computations,
whose certificates are checked by programs written separately from those that produced them, and which two
independent checkers confirmed by methods of their own. The lower bounds of Theorem~\ref{thm:C} and
Proposition~\ref{prop:fiftynine} are proved in Section~\ref{sec:circ} with exact certificates checked by computer (the
former also follow from Theorem~\ref{thm:E}), and the case $f=1$, $p\ge11$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:residue}, used
only for Corollary~\ref{cor:localvalues}, rests on a finite computation of $\kappa_1$ for the primes $11\le p<1001$ (run
up to $3000$); its case $f\ge3$, which Lemma~\ref{lem:B5seven} also uses, is proved by hand. The rest of the proofs
are by hand. Theorem~\ref{thm:D}, which
Theorem~\ref{thm:E} supersedes, is proved entirely by hand. So below $4$ the circular chromatic number of the plane
over a number field takes only the values $2$, $3$ and $7/2$, each given by a locally constant colouring of one
completion; more precisely, below $4$ it is the least circular chromatic number of the planes over its completions
(Corollary~\ref{cor:localF}): a circular local--global principle below $4$ that extends Theorem~\ref{thm:B}. Below
$4$ the value is also the circular chromatic number of a finite unit-distance graph in $F^2$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:finite}), and so is the value $4$ when $\chi_c(F^2)=4$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:finite4}). The proofs give
no bound on the size of such a graph; for the value $4$ we know none, but for $7/2$ a computer search finds explicit
ones, certified: over $\Q(\sqrt{11})$ one with $155$ vertices, and others over $\Q(\sqrt{455})$, $\Q(\sqrt{191})$ and
$\Q(\sqrt{911})$;
for the value $3$, over $\Q(\sqrt7)$, whose plane has no unit triangle, one with nine vertices, the fewest possible
by an exhaustive enumeration (Section~\ref{sec:twoprimes}). For a
real quadratic field $\Q(\sqrt d)$, $d$ squarefree, (a) or (b) holds unless $d\equiv11\pmod{12}$. If
$d\equiv11\pmod{12}$, then $\chi_c=7/2$ when $(d/7)\ne-1$ (for example $d=23$, $71$); $\chi_c=4$ when $(d/7)=-1$ and
$d\equiv11\pmod{24}$ (for example $d=59$, $83$), as then the place above $2$ gives $\chi\le4$; and only when
$(d/7)=-1$ and $d\equiv23\pmod{24}$ ($d=47$, $143$, $167$, $215$, \dots) do we not know whether $\chi_c=4$, which holds
if and only if $\chi=4$ (for $d=47$, $4\le\chi_c\le19/4$; Section~\ref{sec:twoprimes}).

The tools are those of \cite{FC}: a characterisation of $3$-colourings of Cayley graphs of abelian groups by
characters (Theorem~W$^+$, Section~\ref{sec:prelim}), and the exact shape of the characters along the rational rotations whose
denominators are powers of $5$. These rotations act as a probe: along them, a character that keeps every unit vector
in $[1/3,2/3]$ is, up to a bounded error, either a $2$-adic ``half'' character or a $3$-adic character of level one,
and a compactness argument turns this into the dichotomy between the places above $2$ and those above $3$. For
Theorem~\ref{thm:D} we show that the probe keeps this shape, with a small error also for type~q, when $[1/3,2/3]$ is
replaced by $[\theta,1-\theta]$ with $\theta>17/56$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:probe}). This probe cannot pass $10/3$:
for $\theta\le3/10$ it has $5$-adic characters. For Theorems~\ref{thm:E} and~\ref{thm:F} we add one rational rotation
with another prime, $\sigma=(5+12i)/13$. Along the rotations $i^a\rho^j\sigma^l$ a finite computation modulo $65$
shows that the probe keeps the shape c or q for $\theta>2/7$, and one modulo $325$ that for $\theta>1/4$ it has only
these two shapes and a third, $7$-adic, one; a compactness argument at the places above $2$, $3$ and $7$ then proves
the theorems.

Sections~\ref{sec:prelim}--\ref{sec:three} prove Theorem~\ref{thm:B}; Section~\ref{sec:cor} draws the consequences
(circular colourings below $3$, fields split above $2$ and $3$, local fields); Section~\ref{sec:two} gives the
explicit family; Section~\ref{sec:circ} proves Theorem~\ref{thm:C} and shows that, below $4$, the best upper bound on
$\chi_c(F^2)$ given by the restriction to $F^2$ of a locally constant colouring of a completion $F_v^2$ is $2$, $3$ or
$7/2$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:localvalues}); Section~\ref{sec:gap} proves Theorem~\ref{thm:D} and
Section~\ref{sec:twoprimes} Theorems~\ref{thm:E} and~\ref{thm:F}, with the local fields and finite witnesses;
Section~\ref{sec:checks} lists the computer checks,
and Section~\ref{sec:questions} asks what happens above $4$, at four colours, and how small finite witnesses can be.
The programs and certificates are in \cite{Repo}.

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}

\emph{Characters and colourings.} For an abelian group $\Gamma$ and $S=-S\subseteq\Gamma$, $\Cay(\Gamma,S)$ is the
graph on $\Gamma$ in which $x\sim y$ when $y-x\in S$. A \emph{character} of $\Gamma$ is a homomorphism
$\xi:\Gamma\to\R/\Z$, and $\|t\|$ is the distance from $t$ to the nearest integer. For integers $p\ge2q\ge1$, the
circular clique $K_{p/q}$ has vertex set $\Z/p$, with $a\sim b$ when $q\le(b-a\bmod p)\le p-q$, and the circular
chromatic number $\chi_c(G)$ is the infimum of the $p/q$ such that $G$ has a homomorphism to $K_{p/q}$.

\begin{thmW}[{\cite[Theorem~1]{Winding}}]
Let $p\ge2q\ge2$ with $p<4q$. Then $\Cay(\Gamma,S)$ has a homomorphism to $K_{p/q}$ if and only if some character
$\xi$ of $\Gamma$ satisfies $\xi(s)\in[q/p,1-q/p]+\Z$ for every $s\in S$. In particular $\Cay(\Gamma,S)$ is
$3$-colourable if and only if some character maps $S$ into $[1/3,2/3]+\Z$.
\end{thmW}

The ``if'' direction is classical (colour $x$ by $\lfloor p\,\xi(x)\rfloor$); the ``only if'' direction is the
content of \cite{Winding}, where it is also proved in Lean for every abelian group $\Gamma$ and finite $S$. We apply
it to the groups $\Gamma=\Z U$ spanned by finite sets $U$ of unit vectors, and in Section~\ref{sec:circ} to finite groups
$O_w/\mathfrak m^k$.

\emph{The plane as a Cayley graph.} Let $F$ be a number field with $i\notin F$, and $L=F(i)$. The map
$(x,y)\mapsto x+iy$ identifies $F^2$ with $L$, and $x^2+y^2=z\bar z$ for $z=x+iy$, where $z\mapsto\bar z$ is the
non-trivial automorphism of $L/F$. So the unit vectors form the group $T=\{z\in L:z\bar z=1\}$, and the plane over
$F$ is $\Cay(L,T)$. As a $\Q(i)$-vector space $L$ has dimension $n=[F:\Q]$, and $\Tr=\Tr_{L/\Q(i)}$ gives a
non-degenerate pairing $L\times L\to\Q(i)$.

\emph{The probe.} Let $N=5^k$, $k\ge1$, let $G_N$ be the set of $\gamma\in\Q(i)$ with $\gamma\bar\gamma=1$ and
$N\gamma\in\Z[i]$ (the rational rotations whose denominator divides $N$), $I=[1/3,2/3]$, and
\[
S_N=\{c\in\C:\ \operatorname{Re}(\bar c\gamma)\in I+\Z\text{ for every }\gamma\in G_N\}.
\]
Let $E_c=\frac{1+i}2+\Z[i]$, $E_q=\{\frac{\alpha+\beta i}3:\alpha,\beta\in\{1,2\}\}+\Z[i]$, $E=E_c\cup E_q$, and
$r=\sqrt{10}/18$. Elements of $E_c$ have \emph{type~c}, those of $E_q$ \emph{type~q}.

\begin{proposition}[{\cite[Proposition~9]{FC}}]\label{prop:shape}
Every $c\in S_N$ can be written $c=N\varepsilon+x$, either with $\varepsilon\in E_c$ and $|x|\le r$, or with
$\varepsilon\in E_q$ and $x=0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{lemma}[exact relations; cf.\ {\cite[Lemma~10]{FC}}]\label{lem:exact}
Let $c_j=N\varepsilon_j+x_j\in S_N$ be as in Proposition~\ref{prop:shape} \textup{(}$1\le j\le\ell$\textup{)},
and let $\mu_1,\dots,\mu_\ell\in\Z[i]$ satisfy $\sum_j\mu_jc_j=0$ and $N>6r\sum_j|\mu_j|$. Then
$\sum_j\mu_j\varepsilon_j=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\sum_j\mu_jN\varepsilon_j=-\sum_j\mu_jx_j$. The left side lies in $\frac N6\Z[i]$, because $6E\subseteq\Z[i]$,
and the nonzero elements of $\frac N6\Z[i]$ have modulus at least $\frac N6$; the right side has modulus at most
$r\sum_j|\mu_j|<\frac N6$. So both sides vanish.
\end{proof}

Only two properties of $E$ are used below. Let $\F_9=\Z[i]/3$ and $A'=\{\pm1\pm i\}\subset\F_9$, the set of
non-squares of $\F_9$ (equivalently, of the $y\in\F_9$ with $y^4=-1$). Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:types}
\varepsilon\in E_c\ \Rightarrow\ v_{1+i}(\varepsilon)=-1\text{ and }v_3(\varepsilon)\ge0;\qquad
\varepsilon\in E_q\ \Rightarrow\ v_{1+i}(\varepsilon)\ge0\text{ and }3\varepsilon\bmod3\in A'.
\end{equation}
In particular every $\varepsilon\in E$ has $v_{1+i}(\varepsilon)\ge-1$ and $v_3(\varepsilon)\ge-1$, with equality
in the second exactly for type~q.

\section{From colourings to a linear problem over \texorpdfstring{$\Q(i)$}{Q(i)}}\label{sec:linear}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:B1}
Let $F$ be a number field with $i\notin F$, and suppose that $F^2$ is $3$-colourable. Then for every finite
$V\subset T$ there is $\beta\in L$ with $\Tr(\beta v)\in E$ for every $v\in V$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We may enlarge $V$, so we assume that it contains a $\Q(i)$-basis $b_1,\dots,b_n$ of $L$. Such a basis exists in
$T$: the $\Q(i)$-span $W$ of $T$ is a subring of $L$, as $T$ is a group; being finite-dimensional over $\Q(i)$ and
contained in a field, it is a field. For $t\in F$ the element $z=(t+i)/(t-i)$ lies in $T$, so $W$ contains
$(z-1)^{-1}$ and $t=i(z+1)/(z-1)$; hence $W=L$.

For $v\in V$ write $D_vv=\sum_j\mu_{v,j}b_j$ with $D_v\in\Z\setminus\{0\}$ and $\mu_{v,j}\in\Z[i]$, and choose
$N=5^k$ with $N>6r\max_v(|D_v|+\sum_j|\mu_{v,j}|)$. The set $U=G_NV$ is finite, symmetric ($-1\in G_N$) and
consists of unit vectors, so $\Cay(\Z U,U)$ is a subgraph of $\Cay(L,T)$ and is $3$-colourable. By
Theorem~W$^+$ there is a character $\xi$ of $\Z U$ with $\xi(U)\subseteq I+\Z$. Extend $\xi$ to $L$ (the group
$\R/\Z$ is divisible) and restrict it to a lattice $\Lambda=\frac1M\bigoplus_j\Z[i]f_j\supseteq U$, where
$f_1,\dots,f_n$ is a $\Q$-basis of $F$, so that $L=\bigoplus_j\Q(i)f_j$. A character of $\Lambda\cong\Z^{2n}$ lifts
to a real-linear form, and every real-linear form on $\C^n$ is the real part of a $\C$-linear one; so there is a
$\Q(i)$-linear map $\varphi:L\to\C$ with $\xi(w)=\operatorname{Re}\varphi(w)\bmod1$ for $w\in\Lambda$. For $v\in V$
and $\gamma\in G_N$ we get $\xi(\gamma v)=\operatorname{Re}(\gamma\varphi(v))$, so $\overline{\varphi(v)}\in S_N$,
and $\varphi(v)\in S_N$ because $G_N$ is closed under conjugation. Write $\varphi(v)=N\varepsilon_v+x_v$ as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:shape}. Applying $\varphi$ to $D_vv-\sum_j\mu_{v,j}b_j=0$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:exact} give
$D_v\varepsilon_v=\sum_j\mu_{v,j}\varepsilon_{b_j}$. Let $\beta\in L$ be the element with
$\Tr(\beta b_j)=\varepsilon_{b_j}$ for all $j$. Then $D_v\Tr(\beta v)=\sum_j\mu_{v,j}\varepsilon_{b_j}=D_v\varepsilon_v$,
so $\Tr(\beta v)=\varepsilon_v\in E$ for every $v\in V$.
\end{proof}

Lemma~\ref{lem:B1} is also a practical test: if for some finite $V\subset T$ no such $\beta$ exists, then $F^2$ is
not $3$-colourable. Whether $\beta$ exists is a finite computation (Section~\ref{sec:checks}).

\section{Localisation at \texorpdfstring{$2$}{2} and \texorpdfstring{$3$}{3}}\label{sec:local}

For a prime $p$ let $L_p=L\otimes_\Q\Q_p=\prod_{w\mid p}L_w$, and for a place $u$ of $F$ let
$L_u=L\otimes_FF_u$ and $T(F_u)=\{z\in L_u:z\bar z=1\}$. Put $T_p=\prod_{u\mid p}T(F_u)$. Since $2$ ramifies and $3$
is inert in $\Q(i)$, $\Q(i)\otimes\Q_2=\Q_2(i)$ and $\Q(i)\otimes\Q_3=\Q_9$, the unramified quadratic extension of
$\Q_3$; the trace extends to $\Tr:L_2\to\Q_2(i)$ and $\Tr:L_3\to\Q_9$. Let $O_2=\Z_2[i]$, $O_3=\Z_9$ (the integers of
$\Q_9$), and $\cA=\{\frac{\pm1\pm i}3\}+O_3$. By \eqref{eq:types}, $E_c\subseteq(1+i)^{-1}O_2^\times\cap O_3$ and
$E_q\subseteq O_2\cap\cA$.

\begin{lemma}[weak approximation for $T$]\label{lem:WA}
For every finite set $\Sigma$ of places of $F$, $T$ is dense in $\prod_{u\in\Sigma}T(F_u)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
At a place $u$ with $i\notin F_u$, $t\mapsto(t+i)/(t-i)$ is a homeomorphism from $F_u$ onto $T(F_u)\setminus\{1\}$,
with inverse $z\mapsto i(z+1)/(z-1)$. At a place with $i\in F_u$, $L_u\cong F_u\times F_u$,
$T(F_u)=\{(s,s^{-1})\}$, and $t\mapsto(s,s^{-1})$ with $s=(t+\iota)/(t-\iota)$, $\iota^2=-1$, is a homeomorphism
from $F_u\setminus\{\pm\iota\}$ onto $T(F_u)\setminus\{1\}$. These maps send $F$ into $T$, $F$ is dense in
$\prod_{u\in\Sigma}F_u$, and $T(F_u)$ has no isolated points.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:B2}
If $F^2$ is $3$-colourable and $i\notin F$, then one of the following holds:
\begin{itemize}
\item[(c)] there is $\beta_2\in L_2$ with $v_{1+i}(\Tr(\beta_2z))=-1$ for every $z\in T_2$;
\item[(q)] there is $\beta_3\in L_3$ with $\Tr(\beta_3z)\in\cA$ for every $z\in T_3$.
\end{itemize}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix a $\Q(i)$-basis $b_1,\dots,b_n\subset T$ of $L$ (proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:B1}), and let
\[
K_2=\{\beta\in L_2:\Tr(\beta b_j)\in(1+i)^{-1}O_2\ \forall j\},\qquad K_3=\{\beta\in L_3:\Tr(\beta b_j)\in\tfrac13O_3\ \forall j\}.
\]
They are compact, since $\beta\mapsto(\Tr(\beta b_j))_j$ is a linear isomorphism onto $\Q_2(i)^n$, resp.\
$\Q_9^n$. On the compact space $\Omega=\{\mathrm c,\mathrm q\}^T\times K_2\times K_3$ consider, for $v\in T$, the
condition $P_v(\tau,\beta_2,\beta_3)$:
\[
\tau(v)=\mathrm c,\ v_{1+i}(\Tr(\beta_2v))=-1,\ \Tr(\beta_3v)\in O_3;\quad\text{or}\quad
\tau(v)=\mathrm q,\ \Tr(\beta_2v)\in O_2,\ \Tr(\beta_3v)\in\cA.
\]
Each $P_v$ defines a clopen subset of $\Omega$. For a finite $V\supseteq\{b_1,\dots,b_n\}$, Lemma~\ref{lem:B1} gives
$\beta\in L$; its images in $L_2$ and $L_3$ lie in $K_2$ and $K_3$, and with $\tau(v)$ the type of $\Tr(\beta v)$ the
triple satisfies $P_v$ for every $v\in V$, by \eqref{eq:types}. So the closed sets $\{P_v\text{ for all }v\in V\}$
have the finite intersection property, and some $(\tau,\beta_2,\beta_3)\in\Omega$ satisfies $P_v$ for every $v\in T$.

For $z\in T_2$ let $a(z)$ be the statement $v_{1+i}(\Tr(\beta_2z))=-1$ and $a'(z)$ the statement
$\Tr(\beta_2z)\in O_2$; for $y\in T_3$ let $b(y)$ be $\Tr(\beta_3y)\in O_3$ and $b'(y)$ be $\Tr(\beta_3y)\in\cA$.
They are locally constant, $a$ and $a'$ exclude each other, and so do $b$ and $b'$. The set
\[
Z=\{(z,y)\in T_2\times T_3:\ (a(z)\wedge b(y))\vee(a'(z)\wedge b'(y))\}
\]
is clopen and contains the diagonal image of $T$, which is dense by Lemma~\ref{lem:WA}; so $Z=T_2\times T_3$. If
$a(z_0)$ holds for some $z_0$, then $b(y)$ holds for every $y$, so $b'$ holds nowhere, and $a(z)$ holds for every
$z$: this is (c). Otherwise $a'$ holds everywhere, and then $b'$ holds everywhere: this is (q).
\end{proof}

\emph{Case (c).} For $z\in L$ let $g(z)=(1+i)\Tr(\beta_2z)\in\Q_2(i)$. Fix a representative $y_A$ of each class $A$
of $\Q_2(i)$ modulo $O_2$, and colour $z$ by $(g(z)-y_A)\bmod(1+i)\in O_2/(1+i)=\F_2$, where $A$ is the class of
$g(z)$. A unit step $v\in T$ adds $g(v)\in O_2^\times$ to $g(z)$: it keeps the class modulo $O_2$ and
changes the residue modulo $1+i$. This is a proper $2$-colouring of $L\cong F^2$, so by \cite[Theorem~1]{TC} some
prime of $F$ above $2$ ramifies in $F(i)$.

\section{Case (q): the places above \texorpdfstring{$3$}{3}}\label{sec:three}

Let $u$ be a place of $F$ above $3$, $f=f_u$ its residue degree, $q=3^f$, and $g_u(z)=\Tr_{L_u/\Q_9}(\beta_uz)$,
where $\beta_3=(\beta_u)_u$; then $\Tr(\beta_3y)=\sum_ug_u(y_u)$ for $y=(y_u)\in T_3$.

\emph{Even $f$.} Then $\F_9$ lies in the residue field of $F_u$, so $i\in F_u$, $L_u\cong F_u\times F_u$ and
$T(F_u)=\{(s,s^{-1}):s\in F_u^\times\}$. Write $g_u(s,t)=\ell_1(s)+\ell_2(t)$ with $\Q_3$-linear
$\ell_1,\ell_2:F_u\to\Q_9$. In case (q), $g_u(y_u)$ stays in a bounded set when $y_u$ varies and the other
components are fixed. If $\ell_1(s_0)\ne0$, then $g_u(3^{-m}s_0,3^ms_0^{-1})=3^{-m}\ell_1(s_0)+3^m\ell_2(s_0^{-1})$
is unbounded as $m\to\infty$; so $\ell_1=0$, likewise $\ell_2=0$, and $g_u=0$.

\emph{Odd $f$.} Then $L_u=F_u(i)=F_u\Q_9$ is a field, unramified of degree $2$ over $F_u$, with residue field
$\F_{q^2}$; its non-trivial automorphism $\sigma$ acts on $\Q_9$ as the Frobenius ($\sigma(i)=-i$). The group
$T(F_u)$ lies in $O_{L_u}^\times$, contains $\mu_4\subset\Q_9$ and the Teichm\"uller lifts of
$\mu_{q+1}\subset\F_{q^2}^\times$ (as $\zeta\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^{1+q}$), and reduces onto $\mu_{q+1}$. Since $g_u$
is $\Q_9$-linear, $g_u(\epsilon z)=\epsilon g_u(z)$ for $\epsilon\in\mu_4$.

\begin{lemma}[one place]\label{lem:B3}
In case \textup{(q)} there are a place $u_0\mid3$ of odd residue degree and $\beta\in L_{u_0}$ with
$\Tr_{L_{u_0}/\Q_9}(\beta z)\in\cA$ for every $z\in T(F_{u_0})$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Only the places of odd residue degree contribute. For such a place $u$ and $z,z'\in T(F_u)$, varying $y_u$ alone
gives $g_u(z)-g_u(z')\in\cA-\cA\subseteq\frac13O_3$. With $z'=1$ and $z=i$ this says
$(i-1)g_u(1)\in\frac13O_3$, and $i-1$ is a unit at $3$; so $g_u(T(F_u))\subseteq\frac13O_3$. Let $Z_u\subseteq\F_9$
be the set of residues of $3g_u(z)$, $z\in T(F_u)$; it is stable under $\mu_4$, and (q) says that $\sum_uz_u\in A'$
for every choice of $z_u\in Z_u$. Suppose two places $u\ne u'$ had nonzero elements $w\in Z_u$ and $w'\in Z_{u'}$.
Fix $z_{u''}\in Z_{u''}$ at the other places and put $c=\epsilon'w'+\sum z_{u''}$ with $\epsilon'\in\mu_4$. The four
distinct elements $c+\epsilon w$, $\epsilon\in\mu_4$, lie in $A'$, which has four elements; so they are all of $A'$,
and summing them gives $4c=c=\sum A'=0$. As this holds for every $\epsilon'\in\mu_4$, $w'=iw'$, so $w'=0$: a
contradiction. Since $0\notin A'$, exactly one place $u_0$ has $Z_{u_0}\ne\{0\}$; the other $g_u$ take values in
$O_3$, and $\beta=\beta_{u_0}$ works, because $\cA+O_3=\cA$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[level one]\label{lem:B4}
Let $u\mid3$ have odd residue degree $f$, and let $\beta\in L_u$ satisfy $\Tr_{L_u/\Q_9}(\beta z)\in\cA$ for every
$z\in T(F_u)$. Then $\lambda(x)=3\Tr_{L_u/\Q_9}(\beta x)\bmod3$ is defined on $O_{L_u}$ and vanishes on its maximal
ideal $\mathfrak m$, and the induced $\F_9$-linear map $\lambda_1:\F_{q^2}\to\F_9$ satisfies
$\lambda_1(\mu_{q+1})\subseteq A'$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The closed additive span $R$ of $T(F_u)$ is a closed subring of $O_{L_u}$. It contains $\Z_3[\mu_{q+1}]$, which is
the ring of integers $W$ of the unramified extension of $\Q_3$ of degree $2f$ (the order of $3$ modulo $q+1$ is
$2f$). Let $\pi$ be a uniformiser of $F_u$. Then $z=(\pi+i)/(\pi-i)$ and $\sigma(z)=z^{-1}$ lie in $T(F_u)$, and
$2-z-\sigma(z)=4/(\pi^2+1)$ is a unit of $O_{L_u}$ in $R$; its inverse is a limit of its powers, so
$(\pi^2+1)/4\in R$, and $\pi=(z-\sigma(z))(\pi^2+1)/(4i)\in R$. Hence $R\supseteq W[\pi]=O_{L_u}$, since $\pi$ is
also a uniformiser of $L_u$. By continuity $\Tr(\beta O_{L_u})\subseteq\frac13O_3$, so $\lambda:O_{L_u}\to\F_9$ is
defined, $\Z_9$-linear, and zero on $3O_{L_u}=\mathfrak m^e$.

Suppose $\lambda(\mathfrak m)\ne0$, and let $j\ge1$ be the largest integer with $\lambda(\mathfrak m^j)\ne0$.
Identify $\mathfrak m^j/\mathfrak m^{j+1}$ with $\F_{q^2}$ through $x\mapsto(x/\pi^j)\bmod\mathfrak m$; then
$\lambda$ induces a nonzero additive map $\lambda_j:\F_{q^2}\to\F_9$. For $\eta\in O_{F_u}$ the element
$t=(1+i\pi^j\eta)/(1-i\pi^j\eta)$ lies in $T(F_u)$ (as $\sigma(t)=t^{-1}$), and $t\equiv1+2i\pi^j\eta\pmod{\mathfrak
m^{j+1}}$. For $z\in T(F_u)$ with residue $\hat z\in\mu_{q+1}$ this gives $\lambda(zt)=\lambda(z)+\lambda_j(2i\hat z\hat\eta)$, where
$\hat\eta$ is the residue of $\eta$. As $\hat\eta$ runs over $\F_q$, the second term runs over an additive subgroup
$\Sigma_z$ of $\F_9$, and $\lambda(z)+\Sigma_z\subseteq A'$. But $A'=\{a+bi:a,b\ne0\}$ contains no coset of a
nonzero subgroup of $\F_9$, so $\Sigma_z=0$: $\lambda_j$ vanishes on $i\hat z\F_q$ for every $\hat z\in\mu_{q+1}$.
These sets span $\F_{q^2}$ additively, because the $\F_q$-span of $\mu_{q+1}$ is a subfield not contained in
$\F_q$; so $\lambda_j=0$, a contradiction. Hence $\lambda(\mathfrak m)=0$, the induced map $\lambda_1$ is
$\F_9$-linear, and $\lambda_1(\mu_{q+1})\subseteq A'$ because $T(F_u)$ reduces onto $\mu_{q+1}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[residue fields]\label{lem:B5}
Let $f$ be odd and $q=3^f$. There is an $\F_9$-linear map $\lambda_1:\F_{q^2}\to\F_9$ with
$\lambda_1(\mu_{q+1})\subseteq A'$ if and only if $f=1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $f=1$, $\mu_4\subset\F_9$ and $\lambda_1(x)=(1+i)x$ works. Let $f\ge3$. Every $\F_9$-linear map is
$\lambda_1(x)=\Tr_{\F_{q^2}/\F_9}(bx)=\sum_{j<f}(bx)^{9^j}$ for some $b\in\F_{q^2}$. In characteristic $3$,
\[
\lambda_1(z)^4=\lambda_1(z)^3\lambda_1(z)=\sum_{j,k<f}b^{e_{jk}}z^{e_{jk}},\qquad e_{jk}=3\cdot9^j+9^k.
\]
Since $A'=\{y:y^4=-1\}$, if $\lambda_1(\mu_{q+1})\subseteq A'$ then $\Phi(z)=1+\sum_{j,k}b^{e_{jk}}z^{e_{jk}}$
vanishes on $\mu_{q+1}$. On this cyclic group, of order prime to $3$, the characters $z\mapsto z^e$
($e\bmod q+1$) are linearly independent; so for every class $e\bmod q+1$ the sum of the coefficients of the
$z^{e_{jk}}$ with $e_{jk}\equiv e$ vanishes (or equals $-1$, for $e\equiv0$).

Modulo $q+1$, $g=-3$ satisfies $g^f=-3^f\equiv1$ (as $f$ is odd), $9^j\equiv g^{2j}$ and $3\cdot9^j\equiv-g^{2j+1}$.
Let $e_{j'k'}\equiv e_{jj}=4\cdot9^j$. Multiplying by $g^{-2j}$ gives $4\equiv-g^\alpha+g^\gamma$ with
$\alpha\equiv2j'+1-2j$ and $\gamma\equiv2k'-2j\pmod f$, $0\le\alpha,\gamma<f$. As integers, $(-3)^\alpha$ and
$(-3)^\gamma$ have absolute value at most $3^{f-1}$, and $4+2\cdot3^{f-1}<3^f+1$ for $f\ge3$; so the congruence is
the equality $(-3)^\gamma-(-3)^\alpha=4$. It forces $\gamma=0$ (otherwise $3$ divides the left side, or
$(-3)^\gamma=5$), then $(-3)^\alpha=-3$ and $\alpha=1$; so $j'=k'=j$, as $2$ is invertible modulo $f$. Hence the
class of $4\cdot9^j$ contains only $e_{jj}$, it is not $0$ (because $q+1>4$ and $3\nmid q+1$), and its coefficient
$b^{4\cdot9^j}$ must vanish. So $b=0$, and then $\Phi=1\ne0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B}]
If $i\in F$, then (a) and (b) fail ($F(i)=F$, and every residue field above $3$ contains $\F_9$), and
$\chi(F^2)=\infty$ by a theorem of Davies \cite{Davies} (see \cite[Proposition~3]{PP}); so
the theorem holds. Let $i\notin F$.

\emph{Only if.} Let $F^2$ be $3$-colourable. By Lemma~\ref{lem:B2}, (c) or (q) holds. In case (c), Section~\ref{sec:local}
gives (a). In case (q), Lemmas~\ref{lem:B3} and~\ref{lem:B4} give a place $u_0\mid3$ of odd residue degree $f$ and
an $\F_9$-linear $\lambda_1:\F_{q^2}\to\F_9$ with $\lambda_1(\mu_{q+1})\subseteq A'$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:B5} gives
$f=1$: this is (b).

\emph{If.} Under (a) the plane is $2$-colourable \cite[Theorem~A$'$]{MM}, \cite[Theorem~1]{TC}. Under (b), let
$u\mid3$ have residue degree $1$ and let $w$ be the place of $L$ above it. Then $i\notin F_u$, $L_w=F_u(i)$ has
residue field $\F_9$, and the unit vectors of $F_u^2$ are units of $O_w$ whose residues lie in $\mu_4$. A unit step
preserves the class of $z$ modulo $O_w$; fix a representative $x_A$ of each class $A$ and colour each $z\in A$ by
$\ell((z-x_A)\bmod\mathfrak m_w)$, where $\ell(a+bi)=a+b$ on $\F_9=\F_3+\F_3i$. Since $\ell(\pm1)=\pm1$ and
$\ell(\pm i)=\pm1$, every unit step changes the colour. This colours $F_u^2\supseteq F^2$ with three colours.
Finally $\chi(F^2)\le2$ if and only if (a), by \cite[Theorem~1]{TC}.
\end{proof}

The proof uses Theorem~W$^+$ only through Lemma~\ref{lem:B1}, for the finite sets $G_NV$; the rest is
compactness, weak approximation for the norm-one torus, the arithmetic of the places above $2$ and $3$, and, in case
(c), the two-colour theorem of \cite{TC}.

\section{Consequences}\label{sec:cor}

\begin{corollary}[no circular chromatic number between $2$ and $3$]\label{cor:B6}
Let $F$ be a number field. If $F^2$ has a homomorphism to a circular clique $K_{p/q}$ with $p/q<3$, then
$\chi(F^2)\le2$. Hence $\chi_c(F^2)=2$, $\chi_c(F^2)=3$ or $\chi_c(F^2)>3$, according as $\chi(F^2)=2$, $3$ or at
least $4$; and $F^2$ has a homomorphism to a cycle of odd length at least $5$ only if it is bipartite.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If $i\in F$ there is nothing to prove, so let $i\notin F$. For every finite $U$ as in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:B1}, Theorem~W$^+$ gives a character with $\xi(U)\subseteq[q/p,1-q/p]+\Z$, and
$q/p>1/3$. In that proof $\varphi(v)$ then satisfies $\operatorname{Re}(\overline{\varphi(v)})\in[q/p,1-q/p]+\Z$ (take
$\gamma=1$), while a point $N\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon\in E_q$ (by Proposition~\ref{prop:shape} these are the
points of type~q) has real part in $\{\frac13,\frac23\}+\Z$, as $3\nmid N$. So every $\varepsilon_v$ has type~c, the conditions $P_v$ in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:B2} can all
be taken with $\tau(v)=\mathrm c$, and (c) holds; so $\chi(F^2)\le2$. For the second statement: if
$\chi_c(F^2)<3$, then by the definition of the infimum $F^2$ maps to some $K_{p/q}$ with $p/q<3$, so $\chi(F^2)\le2$;
hence $\chi_c(F^2)=3$ when $\chi(F^2)=3$, as $\chi_c\le\chi$. If $\chi(F^2)\ge4$, then by the theorem of de Bruijn and
Erd\H{o}s \cite{dBE} some finite subgraph $H$ has $\chi(H)\ge4$, so $\chi_c(F^2)\ge\chi_c(H)>\chi(H)-1\ge3$.
\end{proof}

Call a number field $F$ \emph{split above $2$ and $3$} if $i\in F_v$ for every place $v$ of $F$ above $2$ and above
$3$, that is, if every such place splits in $F(i)$; then (a) and (b) fail. The admissible fields of
\cite[Section~4]{TC}, which must moreover satisfy $\sqrt3\notin F$ and $i\in F_v$ at the places above $7$, $11$ and
$19$, are split above $2$ and $3$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:B7}
Every number field $F$ that is split above $2$ and $3$ has $\chi(F^2)\ge4$.
\end{corollary}

A real quadratic field $\Q(\sqrt d)$ is split above $2$ and $3$ exactly when $d\equiv23\pmod{24}$, so for real
quadratic fields Corollary~\ref{cor:B7} is contained in \cite{FC} (not for imaginary ones: $\Q(\sqrt{-73})$ is split
above $2$ and $3$). Of the biquadratic fields listed as admissible in the account
of \cite[\nolinkurl{notes/local_global.md}, Section~4]{Repo}, $\Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt{47})$, $\Q(\sqrt{11},\sqrt{13})$ and
$\Q(\sqrt{10},\sqrt{38})$ (which contains $\sqrt{95}$) contain some $\sqrt d$ with $d\equiv11\pmod{12}$; for
$\Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt{31})$ and $\Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt{55})$ the bound is new, as far as we know.

\begin{corollary}[local fields]\label{cor:local}
Let $K$ be a finite extension of $\Q_p$. Then $\chi(K^2)=2$ if $p=2$ and $K(i)/K$ is ramified, $\chi(K^2)=3$ if
$p=3$ and the residue degree of $K$ is $1$, and $\chi(K^2)\ge4$ in every other case.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
In the first case the $2$-colouring of the proof of \cite[Proposition~3]{TC} colours $K^2$ itself (that proof
colours $F_v^2$, and it applies verbatim to every finite extension $K$ of $\Q_2$ with $K(i)/K$ ramified); in the
second case the $3$-colouring of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B} colours $K^2$ itself; and
$\chi(K^2)\ge3$ when $p=3$, because $\Q(\sqrt7)\subset\Q_3\subseteq K$ and $\chi(\Q(\sqrt7)^2)=3$. If $p\ge5$, then
$\chi(K^2)\ge\chi(\Q_p^2)\ge4$ by \cite[Corollary~3]{FC}. If $i\in K$, then $\chi(K^2)=\infty$ \cite[Proposition~3]{PP}.
There remain the case $p=2$, $i\notin K$, $K(i)/K$ unramified, and the case $p=3$ with odd residue degree at least
$3$. In both we find a number field $F\subset K$ for which (a) and (b) fail; then $\chi(K^2)\ge\chi(F^2)\ge4$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:B}.

Let $m=[K:\Q_p]$ and let $p'$ be the other element of $\{2,3\}$. If $p=2$, then $m$ is even: if $m$ were odd,
$\Q_2^\times/\Q_2^{\times2}$ would inject into $K^\times/K^{\times2}$, and since $-1$ and $-5$ are not squares in
$\Q_2$, $i\notin K$ and $K(i)\ne K(\sqrt5)$, which is the unramified quadratic extension of $K$; so $K(i)/K$ would be
ramified. Choose monic polynomials over $\Q_p$, $\Q_{p'}$ and $\Q_5$ of a common even degree $n$:
\begin{itemize}
\item over $\Q_p$, $h_Kh'$, where $h_K$ is the minimal polynomial of a generator of $K$ and $h'$ is a product of
distinct irreducible quadratics, coprime to $h_K$, whose roots generate $\Q_2(i)$ (if $p=2$) or $\Q_9$ (if $p=3$);
if $p=3$ and $m$ is odd, $h'$ also contains one cubic factor, coprime to $h_K$, generating the unramified cubic
extension $\Q_{27}$ of $\Q_3$;
\item over $\Q_{p'}$, a product of distinct irreducible quadratics whose roots generate $\Q_2(i)$, resp.\ $\Q_9$;
\item over $\Q_5$, an irreducible polynomial.
\end{itemize}
By weak approximation choose a monic $h\in\Q[x]$ whose coefficients are so close to the three targets that, by
Krasner's lemma, $h$ factors over $\Q_p$, $\Q_{p'}$ and $\Q_5$ with the same degrees and the same fields as the
targets. Then $F=\Q[x]/(h)$ is a field (as $h$ is irreducible over $\Q_5$); it embeds in $K$, through a root of
$h$ close to the chosen generator of $K$; its places above $p$ are the place that gives $K$ and places that contain
$i$ (or, for the cubic factor, have residue degree $3$); and its places above $p'$ all contain $i$. So no place
above $2$ ramifies in $F(i)$ and no place above $3$ has residue degree $1$.
\end{proof}

For example $\chi(\Q_{27}^2)\ge4$, although $i\notin\Q_{27}$ and $x^2+y^2$ is anisotropic over $\Q_{27}$, as over
$\Q_3$; and $\chi(\Q_2(\sqrt3)^2)\ge4$, as $\Q_2(\sqrt3,i)=\Q_2(\sqrt3,\sqrt{-3})$ is unramified over
$\Q_2(\sqrt3)$ (this also follows from $\chi(\Q(\sqrt{11})^2)=4$, since $\Q_2(\sqrt{11})=\Q_2(\sqrt3)$).
Corollary~\ref{cor:local} extends \cite[Corollary~3]{FC} ($p\ge5$) and the values $\chi(\Q_2^2)=2$ and
$\chi(\Q_3^2)=3$, which follow from Madore's results \cite{Madore} (see \cite[Theorem~2]{PP}).

\section{An explicit family: two square roots}\label{sec:two}

For many fields the general argument can be replaced by two explicit relations. For $c\ge1$ put
\[
u_c=\frac{(1+i\sqrt c)^2}{1+c}=\frac{(1-c)+2\sqrt c\,i}{1+c},\qquad\text{so that}\qquad(1+c)(u_c+\bar u_c)=2(1-c).
\]

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:two}
Let $F\subset\R$ contain $\sqrt a$ and $\sqrt b$, where $a\equiv2\pmod3$ and $b\equiv7\pmod8$ are positive integers
\textup{(}$a=b$ is allowed\textup{)}, and let $N=5^k>\frac{4\sqrt{10}}3\max(a,b)$. Then no character of the group
generated by $U=G_N\{1,u_a,\bar u_a,u_b,\bar u_b\}$ maps every element of $U$ into $[1/3,2/3]+\Z$. Hence
$\chi(F^2)\ge4$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
As in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:B1}, such a character gives numbers $w_v=\varphi(v)\in S_N$
($v\in\{1,u_a,\bar u_a,u_b,\bar u_b\}$) that satisfy the two relations exactly. The sums of the absolute values of
their coefficients are $4a$ and $4b$, and $6r\cdot4c=\frac{4\sqrt{10}}3c<N$; so Lemma~\ref{lem:exact} gives, for the
types,
\[
(1+a)(\varepsilon_{u_a}+\varepsilon_{\bar u_a})=2(1-a)\varepsilon_1,\qquad(1+b)(\varepsilon_{u_b}+\varepsilon_{\bar u_b})=2(1-b)\varepsilon_1.
\]
\emph{At $3$.} $3\mid1+a$ and $3\nmid2(1-a)$. By \eqref{eq:types} the left side of the first relation has
$v_3\ge0$, hence $v_3(\varepsilon_1)\ge0$, and $\varepsilon_1$ is not of type~q.
\emph{At $2$.} $v_2(1+b)\ge3$ and $v_2(2(1-b))=2$, as $1-b\equiv2\pmod8$. By \eqref{eq:types} the left side of the
second relation has $v_{1+i}\ge2\cdot3-1=5$, while $v_{1+i}(2(1-b)\varepsilon_1)=4+v_{1+i}(\varepsilon_1)=3$ if
$\varepsilon_1$ has type~c. So $\varepsilon_1$ is not of type~c either, a contradiction.
\end{proof}

For $a=b=d$ this is the case $d\equiv23\pmod{24}$ of \cite[Theorem~1]{FC}, where \cite[Theorem~4(1)]{FC} needs
only $d<21\cdot5^{k-1}$. Proposition~\ref{prop:two} covers, for
example, $\Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt7)$, $\Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt{31})$, $\Q(\sqrt5,\sqrt7)$, $\Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt{55})$ and
$\Q(\sqrt3,\sqrt5)$ (with $b=15$); every field containing one of them then has $\chi\ge4$ as well, as $\chi(F^2)$
can only grow with $F$. For $\Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt7)$ the bound is sharp: $\sqrt2\in\Q_7$, as $2\equiv3^2\pmod7$, so $\Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt7)$ embeds
in $\Q_7(\sqrt7)$, whose residue field is $\F_7$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:C1} gives $\chi_c\le7/2$, hence $\chi(\Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt7)^2)=4$. In
terms of Theorem~\ref{thm:B}, $\sqrt a$
makes every place above $3$ have even residue degree, and $\sqrt b$ puts $i$ in every completion above $2$.

\section{Circular colourings}\label{sec:circ}

For the plane over $\R$, DeVos, Ebrahimi, Ghebleh, Goddyn, Mohar and Naserasr proved $\chi_c(\R^2)\ge4$
\cite{DeVos}; since de Grey's theorem gives a finite subgraph with $\chi=5$, in fact $\chi_c(\R^2)>4$. For planes over
number fields the values $2$ and $3$ are immediate (a bipartite plane; a plane with $\chi=3$ containing a unit
triangle), and by Corollary~\ref{cor:B6} the planes with $\chi_c\le3$ are exactly those with $\chi\le3$. We know of no
earlier non-integral value. Theorem~\ref{thm:C} shows that $7/2$ occurs; its upper bound comes from a residue field.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:C1}
Let $v$ be a place of $F$ whose residue field is $\F_p$, with $p\equiv3\pmod4$, let $m\ge1$ be an integer, and let
$\lambda:\F_{p^2}\to\F_p$ be $\F_p$-linear with $\lambda(\mu_{p+1})\subseteq\{m,m+1,\dots,p-m\}$. Then $F_v^2$, hence $F^2$, has a homomorphism
to $K_{p/m}$. For $p=7$, $\lambda(a+bi)=2a+3b$ takes the values $2,3,4,5$ on $\mu_8\subset\F_{49}$, so $F^2$ maps to
$K_{7/2}$, and $\chi_c(F^2)\le7/2$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
As $-1$ is not a square in $\F_p$, $i\notin F_v$, and $L_w=F_v(i)$ is unramified over $F_v$ with residue field
$\F_{p^2}$. A unit vector $z$ has $z\bar z=1$, so it is a unit of $O_w$, and since the conjugation of $L_w/F_v$ reduces
to the Frobenius $x\mapsto x^p$, its residue satisfies $\hat z^{p+1}=1$. A unit step preserves the class modulo
$O_w$; fix a representative $x_A$ of each class $A$ and colour each $x\in A$ by
$\lambda((x-x_A)\bmod\mathfrak m_w)\in\Z/p$. A unit
step changes the colour by an element of $\{m,\dots,p-m\}$. For $p=7$, $\mu_8=\{\pm1,\pm i,\pm2\pm2i\}$, and $2a+3b$
takes the values $2,5,3,4,3,5,2,4$ at $1,-1,i,-i,2+2i,2-2i,-2+2i,-2-2i$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:C}]
\emph{Upper bounds.} $7$ splits in $\Q(\sqrt{11})$ ($11\equiv2^2\pmod7$) and ramifies in $\Q(\sqrt{35})$, so both
fields, and $\Q_7$, have a place with residue field $\F_7$; Proposition~\ref{prop:C1} applies.

\emph{Lower bounds.} For $d\in\{11,35\}$ let $u_n=(n+i\sqrt d)^2/(n^2+d)$, $V=\{1,u_1,\bar u_1,u_7,\bar u_7,u_{19},\bar
u_{19}\}$ and $U=G_{25}V$, a set of $140$ unit vectors ($70$ up to sign). We checked by computer that no character of
$\Z U$ maps every element of $U$ into the open interval $(2/7,5/7)+\Z$. The certificate branches on the integer
values of the relations among the vectors ($66$ relations; $17\,744$ nodes for $d=11$ and $14\,199$ for $d=35$) and
closes every leaf with an exact Farkas vector; two exact checkers that share no code accept it
(Section~\ref{sec:checks}). If $F^2$, $F=\Q(\sqrt d)$, mapped to $K_{p/q}$ with $p/q<7/2$, Theorem~W$^+$ would
give a character with values in $[q/p,1-q/p]\subset(2/7,5/7)$ on $U$. So $\chi_c(F^2)\ge7/2$. Since
$\Q(\sqrt{11})\subset\Q_7$, also $\chi_c(\Q_7^2)\ge7/2$.

\emph{Chromatic numbers.} $K_{7/2}\to K_4$ gives $\chi\le4$, and $\chi\ge\chi_c=7/2>3$ gives $\chi\ge4$ (for the two
quadratic fields this is also \cite[Theorem~1]{FC}, for $\Q_7$ \cite[Theorem~2]{PP}). The last sentence of the
theorem is Corollary~\ref{cor:B6}.
\end{proof}

The lower bound is about the infinite graph $\Cay(\Z U,U)$, to which Theorem~W$^+$ is applied directly, and gives no
finite subgraph of $\Q(\sqrt{11})^2$ with circular chromatic number exactly $7/2$; one exists by
Corollary~\ref{cor:finite}, and Section~\ref{sec:twoprimes} gives one with $155$ vertices, found by computer. The
open interval is essential:
the characters that come from the $7$-adic colourings take values in the closed interval $[2/7,5/7]$ on $U$. The
certificates were found with a floating-point mixed-integer solver, which reports
$\max_\xi\min_{u\in U}\|\xi(u)\|=2/7$ for both sets; nothing in the proof depends on it.

The circular chromatic number separates planes with the same chromatic number:

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:fiftynine}
$\chi_c(\Q(\sqrt{59})^2)\ge53/15>7/2$, while $\chi(\Q(\sqrt{59})^2)=4$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $d=59$ the same set $U=G_{25}V$ has no character into the open interval $(15/53,38/53)$: the certificate has
$127\,181$ nodes and $105\,761$ leaves, and both checkers accept it (Section~\ref{sec:checks}). As in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:C}, Theorem~W$^+$ gives $\chi_c\ge53/15$. Here $7$ is inert, so Proposition~\ref{prop:C1} does not
apply. We have $\chi\ge4$ by \cite[Theorem~1]{FC}, as $59\equiv11\pmod{12}$. And $\chi\le4$: let $v$ be the place
of $F=\Q(\sqrt{59})$ above $2$. As $59\cdot3\equiv1\pmod8$, $F_v=\Q_2(\sqrt{59})=\Q_2(\sqrt3)$, and
$L_w=F_v(i)=\Q_2(\sqrt3,\sqrt{-3})$ is unramified over $F_v$, with residue field $\F_4$. So the unit vectors are
units of $O_w$ whose residues lie in the group $\mu_3=\F_4^\times$, and, as in the proof of
Proposition~\ref{prop:C1}, colouring each class of $L_w$ modulo $O_w$ by the residue modulo $\mathfrak m_w$ is a
proper $4$-colouring (a bound of Fischer \cite{Fischer1990}).
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:F} gives more: $\chi_c(\Q(\sqrt{59})^2)=4$. Proposition~\ref{prop:fiftynine} is an explicit form of
the separation: $70$ explicit unit vectors already push $\chi_c$ above $7/2$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:sevenhalves}
$\chi_c(K^2)=7/2$ for every finite extension $K$ of $\Q_7$ with residue degree $1$, and $\chi_c(F^2)=7/2$ for every
number field $F$ that contains $\sqrt{11}$ or $\sqrt{35}$ and has a place above $7$ of residue degree $1$, for example
$\Q(\sqrt7,\sqrt{11})$ and $\Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt{35})$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$K\supseteq\Q_7\supset\Q(\sqrt{11})$ and $F\supseteq\Q(\sqrt{11})$ or $\Q(\sqrt{35})$ give $\chi_c\ge7/2$ by
Theorem~\ref{thm:C}; the residue field $\F_7$ gives $\chi_c\le7/2$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:C1} (whose proof
applies to $K$ itself).
\end{proof}

\subsection*{Locally constant colourings}
Below $4$, the best upper bounds that colourings through a single completion can give are $2$, $3$ and $7/2$
(Corollary~\ref{cor:localvalues}). Let
$K$ be a finite extension of $\Q_p$ with $i\notin K$ and $K(i)/K$ unramified (the second condition is automatic for $p$ odd;
for $p=2$ it is the local form of the failure of (a)), let $\F_q$ be the residue field of $K$ ($q=p^f$), $w$ the place of $L=K(i)$, and
\[
\kappa_1(K)=\max_\lambda\min_{z\in\mu_{q+1}}\|\lambda(z)\|,
\]
the maximum over the additive maps $\lambda:\F_{q^2}\to\frac1p\Z/\Z$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:levels}
Suppose $K^2$ has a locally constant homomorphism to $K_{P/Q}$, $P/Q<4$. Then $Q/P\le\kappa_1(K)$. Conversely $K^2$ maps to $K_{p/m}$ when $m/p=\kappa_1(K)>0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The converse is the colouring of Proposition~\ref{prop:C1}, with $\F_q$ in place of $\F_p$. Let $\pi$ be a
uniformiser of $K$ (also one of $L$) and $\sigma$ the non-trivial automorphism of $L/K$. On the compact set
$O_w$ the homomorphism is constant on the cosets of some power $\mathfrak m^k$ of the maximal ideal, and only its
restriction to $O_w$ is used. It induces a homomorphism from $\Cay(O_w/\mathfrak m^k,\bar T)$, where
$\bar T$ is the image of $T(K)$, to $K_{P/Q}$; by Theorem~W$^+$ there is a character $\xi$ of
$O_w/\mathfrak m^k$ with $\|\xi(z)\|\ge Q/P>1/4\ge1/(2p)$ for every $z\in T(K)$. We show that $\xi$ vanishes on
$\mathfrak m$; then $\xi$ is a map $\lambda$ as in the definition of $\kappa_1$, and $Q/P\le\kappa_1(K)$, since $T(K)$
reduces onto $\mu_{q+1}$ (it contains the Teichm\"uller lifts of $\mu_{q+1}$, on which $\sigma$ acts as
$x\mapsto x^q$). Suppose not, and let $J\ge1$ be the largest integer with $\xi(\mathfrak m^J)\ne0$. Through
$x\mapsto(x/\pi^J)\bmod\mathfrak m$, $\xi$ induces a nonzero additive map $\lambda_J:\F_{q^2}\to\frac1p\Z/\Z$. For
$x\in O_w$, $t=(1+\pi^Jx)/\sigma(1+\pi^Jx)$ lies in $T(K)$ and $t\equiv1+\pi^J(x-\sigma(x))\pmod{\mathfrak m^{J+1}}$,
and the residues of $x-\sigma(x)$ fill the $\F_q$-line $D=\{y-y^q:y\in\F_{q^2}\}$. For $z\in T(K)$ with residue
$\hat z$, $\xi(zt)=\xi(z)+\lambda_J(\hat zd)$, $d\in D$; the values $\lambda_J(\hat zD)$ form a subgroup of
$\frac1p\Z/\Z$, and if it were the whole group, $\xi(T(K))$ would contain a coset of $\frac1p\Z/\Z$ and a point
with $\|\cdot\|\le\frac1{2p}$. So $\lambda_J$ vanishes on $\hat zD$ for every $\hat z\in\mu_{q+1}$, hence on their
additive span, which is $D$ times the $\F_q$-span of $\mu_{q+1}$, that is $\F_{q^2}$: a contradiction.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:residue}
$\kappa_1(K)>1/4$ only when $p=3$ and $f=1$, where $\kappa_1=1/3$, or $p=7$ and $f=1$, where $\kappa_1=2/7$.
In every other case with $i\notin K$ and $K(i)/K$ unramified, $\kappa_1(K)<1/4$; it is $0$ when $p=2$ or $f\ge3$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For $p=2$: if $\lambda(\mu_{q+1})=\{\frac12\}$, summing over $\mu_{q+1}$, whose elements add up to $0$, gives
$\lambda(0)=\frac{q+1}2\equiv\frac12$, as $q+1$ is odd; so some $z$ has $\lambda(z)=0$.

Let $p$ be odd; then $p\equiv3\pmod4$ and $f$ is odd, as $i\notin K$. Let $\psi(x)=e^{2\pi ix/p}$ on $\F_p$, and
$S(b)=\sum_{z\in\mu_{q+1}}\psi(\Tr_{\F_{q^2}/\F_p}(bz))$ for $b\in\F_{q^2}^\times$. Every additive
$\lambda:\F_{q^2}\to\frac1p\Z/\Z$ is $z\mapsto\frac1p\Tr(bz)$ for some $b$. Writing the indicator function of
$\mu_{q+1}=\ker\mathrm N_{\F_{q^2}/\F_q}$ with the multiplicative characters $\chi$ of $\F_q^\times$, and using the
Davenport--Hasse relation $G(\chi\circ\mathrm N,\psi\circ\Tr)=-G(\chi,\psi_q)^2$ for Gauss sums \cite{DH}
($\psi_q=\psi\circ\Tr_{\F_q/\F_p}$; see \cite[Chapter~5]{LN}), one finds
\[
S(b)=\frac1{q-1}\sum_\chi\bar\chi(\mathrm N(b))\,G(\chi\circ\mathrm N,\psi\circ\Tr)=-\sum_{y\in\F_q^\times}\psi_q\Bigl(y+\frac{\mathrm N(b)}y\Bigr),
\]
minus a Kloosterman sum; so $|S(b)|\le2\sqrt q$ by Weil's bound \cite{Weil}. Hence, for $b\ne0$, the number of $z\in\mu_{q+1}$
with $\Tr(bz)=0$ is $\frac1p\sum_{a\in\F_p}\sum_z\psi(a\Tr(bz))=\frac{q+1}p+\frac1p\sum_{a\ne0}S(ab)\ge\frac{q+1}p-
\frac{p-1}p\,2\sqrt q$, which is positive when $f\ge3$ (then $\sqrt q\ge p^{3/2}>2(p-1)$); so $\kappa_1=0$ for $f\ge3$.

Let $f=1$, $p=4k+3$, and $\mathcal I=\{x:|x|\le k\}\subset\F_p$, the residues with $\|x/p\|<\frac14$. The number of
$z\in\mu_{p+1}$ with $\Tr(bz)\in\mathcal I$ is
\[
\frac{(p+1)|\mathcal I|}p+\frac1p\sum_{a\ne0}\Bigl(\sum_{x\in\mathcal I}\psi(-ax)\Bigr)S(ab)
\ \ge\ \frac{p^2-1}{2p}-\frac{2\sqrt p}p\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}\frac1{|\sin(\pi a/p)|}
\ \ge\ \frac{p^2-1}{2p}-2\sqrt p\,(\ln p+1),
\]
because $|\sum_{x\in\mathcal I}\psi(-ax)|=|\sin(\pi a(2k+1)/p)/\sin(\pi a/p)|\le1/|\sin(\pi a/p)|$, and, by the symmetry
$a\leftrightarrow p-a$ and $\sin x\ge2x/\pi$ on $[0,\pi/2]$, $\sum_{a=1}^{p-1}1/|\sin(\pi a/p)|\le
p\sum_{a\le(p-1)/2}1/a\le p(\ln p+1)$. The last expression is positive for $p=1001$ and increasing for $p\ge1001$
(its derivative is $\frac12+\frac1{2p^2}-\frac{\ln p+3}{\sqrt p}>0$ there). For the primes $p\equiv3\pmod4$ with
$11\le p<3000$ a direct computation
(in $\F_{p^2}=\F_p(i)$, where $bz$ runs over the circle $s^2+t^2=\mathrm N(b)$ and $\Tr(bz)=2s$) gives $\kappa_1<\frac14$;
the largest value is $\frac4{19}$. For $p=3$ and $p=7$ a direct computation gives $\kappa_1=\frac13$ and
$\kappa_1=\frac27$, attained by the maps of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B} and of Proposition~\ref{prop:C1}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[local circular values]\label{cor:localvalues}
Let $K$ be a finite extension of $\Q_p$. If $K^2$ has a locally constant homomorphism to $K_{P/Q}$ with $P/Q<4$, then
either $p=2$ and $K(i)/K$ is ramified, or $(p,f)=(3,1)$ and $P/Q\ge3$, or $(p,f)=(7,1)$ and $P/Q\ge7/2$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If $i\in K$, then $\chi(K^2)=\infty$ \cite[Proposition~3]{PP}. Otherwise combine Propositions~\ref{prop:levels}
and~\ref{prop:residue}.
\end{proof}

So, below $4$, the best upper bound on $\chi_c(F^2)$ given by the restriction to $F^2$ of a locally constant colouring
of a completion $F_v^2$ of a number field is $2$ (condition (a)), $3$ (condition (b)) or $7/2$ (a place above $7$ of
residue degree $1$).

\section{A gap above \texorpdfstring{$3$}{3}}\label{sec:gap}

By Corollary~\ref{cor:B6}, $\chi_c(F^2)$ is $2$, $3$ or larger than $3$. Theorem~\ref{thm:D} says that in the last
case it stays away from $3$. Its proof is that of Theorem~\ref{thm:B} with $I=[1/3,2/3]$ replaced by a slightly
wider interval; the new ingredient is the shape of the characters along the probe for such intervals. The proof is
entirely by hand; Section~\ref{sec:twoprimes} reaches $7/2$ with a second prime and one computer-assisted step.

\emph{Notation.} In this section $N=5^k$ with $k\ge1$, $\rho=(3+4i)/5$, $h=(1+i)/2$ and
$\Lambda_\pm=\frac{2\pm i}5\Z[i]$. For $\frac14<\theta\le\frac13$ let $S_N^\theta$ be defined as $S_N$ with
$[\theta,1-\theta]$ in place of $I$, and put
\[
s=\tfrac12-\theta,\qquad\delta=\tfrac13-\theta,\qquad B_s=\{a+bi:\ a,b\in\R,\ |a|,|b|\le s\},
\]
so that $s=\frac16+\delta$, and $P_k=\{x\in\C:\ \bar x\rho^j\in B_s\text{ for }|j|\le k\}$. For $\varepsilon\in E_q$
and $|j|\le k$, Lemma~\ref{lem:values} below gives $\eta^N_j(\varepsilon)\in\{\pm1\pm i\}$ with
$\overline{N\varepsilon}\rho^j\in h+\eta^N_j(\varepsilon)/6+\Z[i]$; for $1\le m\le k$ let
\[
Y^N_m(\varepsilon)=\{y\in\C:\ \eta^N_j(\varepsilon)/6+\bar y\rho^j\in B_s\text{ for }|j|\le m\},
\]
and $Y_k(\varepsilon)=Y^N_k(\varepsilon)$. These depend on $\theta$, on $N$, and on $\varepsilon$ only modulo
$\Z[i]$; when $N$ is fixed we write $\eta_j(\varepsilon)$ for $\eta^N_j(\varepsilon)$. We write $w_1=\operatorname{Re}w$
and $w_2=\operatorname{Im}w$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:values}
\textup{(i)} $G_N=\{i^a\rho^j:a\in\Z,\ |j|\le k\}$, and $c\in S_N^\theta$ if and only if $\bar c\rho^j\in
h+B_s+\Z[i]$ for every $|j|\le k$.

\textup{(ii)} If $\varepsilon\in E_c$ and $|j|\le k$, then $\overline{N\varepsilon}\rho^j\in h+\Z[i]$.

\textup{(iii)} If $\varepsilon=\frac{\alpha+\beta i}3+t\in E_q$ and $|j|\le k$, then
$\overline{N\varepsilon}\rho^j\equiv(-1)^k(-i)^j\frac{\alpha-\beta i}3\pmod{\Z[i]}$. This lies in
$h+\eta/6+\Z[i]$ for a unique $\eta\in\{\pm1\pm i\}$; moreover $\eta_{j-1}(\varepsilon)=i\,\eta_j(\varepsilon)$ for
$1-k\le j\le k$ (so $\eta_{j-2}(\varepsilon)=-\eta_j(\varepsilon)$), and $\eta_j(\bar\varepsilon)=
\overline{\eta_{-j}(\varepsilon)}$.

\textup{(iv)} $N\varepsilon+P_k\subseteq S_N^\theta$ for $\varepsilon\in E_c$, and $N\varepsilon+Y_k(\varepsilon)\subseteq
S_N^\theta$ for $\varepsilon\in E_q$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) If $\gamma\in G_N$, then $N\gamma\in\Z[i]$ has norm $5^{2k}$, so $N\gamma=u(2+i)^a(2-i)^b$ with a unit $u$ and
$a+b=2k$, and $\gamma=u\big(\frac{2+i}{2-i}\big)^{a-k}=u\rho^{a-k}$; conversely
$N\rho^{\pm j}=5^{k-j}(3\pm4i)^j\in\Z[i]$ for $0\le j\le k$. Since $\operatorname{Re}(\pm iw)=\mp\operatorname{Im}w$
and $[\theta,1-\theta]+\Z=\frac12+[-s,s]+\Z$ is symmetric, $\operatorname{Re}(\bar c\gamma)\in[\theta,1-\theta]+\Z$
for all $\gamma\in G_N$ if and only if both coordinates of every $\bar c\rho^j$ lie in $\frac12+[-s,s]+\Z$.

(ii) Write $\varepsilon=h+t$. The Gaussian integer $N\rho^j$ has odd norm, so $N\rho^j=1+(1+i)m$ with
$m\in\Z[i]$, and $\bar hN\rho^j=\bar h+m\equiv h$, as $\bar h=h-i$; and $\bar tN\rho^j\in\Z[i]$.

(iii) $\overline{N\varepsilon}\rho^j\equiv\frac{\alpha-\beta i}3N\rho^j$, and $N\rho^j\equiv(-1)^k(-i)^j\pmod3$,
because $5\equiv-1$ and $3\pm4i\equiv\pm i\pmod3$. Modulo $\Z[i]$, $\frac{\alpha-\beta i}3$ times a unit has both
coordinates in $\{\frac13,\frac23\}$, so it lies in $h+\frac{\pm1\pm i}6+\Z[i]$. Since $(-i)^{j-1}=i(-i)^j$ and
$ih\equiv h$, $\eta_{j-1}=i\eta_j$. Finally $\overline{N\bar\varepsilon}\rho^j$ is the conjugate of
$\overline{N\varepsilon}\rho^{-j}$, and $\bar h\equiv h$.

(iv) follows from (i)--(iii).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:radii}
\textup{(i)} $P_1=sP$, where $P$ is the $12$-gon with vertices $\pm1\pm\frac i3$, $\pm\frac13\pm i$ and
$\frac57(\pm1\pm i)$; so $|x|\le s\sqrt{10}/3$ for $x\in P_k$.

\textup{(ii)} $|y|\le\sqrt2\,\delta$ for $y\in Y^N_m(\varepsilon)$, $1\le m\le k$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) $P_1$ is defined by the twelve inequalities $\pm(\bar x\rho^j)_e\le s$, $j\in\{0,\pm1\}$, $e\in\{1,2\}$, which
are homogeneous in $(x,s)$; intersecting their boundary lines in pairs gives the vertices. For instance at
$x=1+\frac i3$ we have $\bar x=1-\frac i3$, $\bar x\rho=\frac{13}{15}+\frac35i$ and $\bar x\rho^{-1}=\frac13-i$.
Every vertex has modulus at most $\sqrt{10}/3$, and $P_k\subseteq P_1$.

(ii) As $s=\frac16+\delta$, the condition $\eta_j/6+\bar y\rho^j\in B_s$ implies
$\eta_{j,e}(\bar y\rho^j)_e\le\delta$ for $e=1,2$, where $\eta_j=\eta_{j,1}+\eta_{j,2}i$. So
$Y^N_m(\varepsilon)\subseteq Y^N_1(\varepsilon)\subseteq\delta X(\varepsilon)$, where $X(\varepsilon)$ is defined by
the six inequalities $\eta^N_{j,e}(\bar y\rho^j)_e\le1$, $|j|\le1$ (for $\delta=0$ read $\delta X(\varepsilon)$ as
$\{0\}$: as $X(\varepsilon)$ is bounded, the same inequalities with right-hand side $0$ have only the solution $0$).
By Lemma~\ref{lem:values}(iii), $\eta^N_j(\varepsilon)=\eta^5_j(\pm\varepsilon)$ for $|j|\le1$; replacing
$\varepsilon$ by $i\varepsilon$ replaces $\eta_j$ by $-i\eta_j$ and $X$ by $iX$, and multiplication by $i$ permutes
the four classes of $E_q$ modulo $\Z[i]$ cyclically. So every $X(\varepsilon)$ is a rotation of $X(\frac{1+i}3)$ at
level $5$, where $\eta_{-1}=1+i$, $\eta_0=1-i$, $\eta_1=-1-i$. This is the hexagon with vertices $-\frac54$, $-\frac54i$,
$-\frac57(1+i)$, $1-\frac i2$, $1+i$ and $-\frac12+i$, of moduli at most $\sqrt2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:gapC}
Let $0<s<11/56$ and $k\ge1$, and let $x\in\C$ with $|x|\le s\sqrt2$ and $\bar x\rho^{k-2}\in B_s$. Then
$\bar x\rho^k+\nu\notin B_s+\Z[i]$ for every $\nu\in\Lambda_+\setminus\Z[i]$. The same holds with $\rho^{-k}$,
$\rho^{2-k}$ and $\Lambda_-$ in place of $\rho^k$, $\rho^{k-2}$ and $\Lambda_+$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $u=\bar x\rho^k$ and $w=\bar x\rho^{k-2}=u\rho^{-2}=u(-7-24i)/25$, so that $w_1=(-7u_1+24u_2)/25$ and
$w_2=-(24u_1+7u_2)/25$. Suppose $u+\nu\in B_s+\Z[i]$; changing $\nu$ by a Gaussian integer we may assume
$u+\nu\in B_s$. The non-zero classes of $\Lambda_+/\Z[i]$ are represented by $\pm\frac{2+i}5$ and
$\pm\frac{-1+2i}5$, whose coordinates have absolute value at most $\frac25$; every other element of the class has a
coordinate of absolute value at least $\frac35$, which $u_e+\nu_e\in[-s,s]$ excludes, as $|u_e|\le|x|\le s\sqrt2$ and
$s(1+\sqrt2)<\frac35$. If $\nu=\pm\frac{2+i}5$, then $\mp u_1\ge\frac25-s$ and $\mp u_2\ge\frac15-s$, so
$\pm w_2=\mp(24u_1+7u_2)/25\ge(11-31s)/25$. If $\nu=\pm\frac{-1+2i}5$, then $\pm u_1\ge\frac15-s$ and $\mp
u_2\ge\frac25-s$, so $\mp w_1=\pm(7u_1-24u_2)/25\ge(11-31s)/25$. In both cases
$|w_e|\ge(11-31s)/25>s$, as $56s<11$, contradicting $w\in B_s$. The second statement follows by complex conjugation,
which exchanges $\rho$ and $\rho^{-1}$, and $\Lambda_+$ and $\Lambda_-$, and preserves $B_s$ and $\Z[i]$ (apply the
first to $\bar x$ and $\bar\nu$).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:gapQ}
Let $0\le\delta<5/168$, $k\ge2$ and $\varepsilon\in E_q$, and let $y\in\C$ with $|y|<\frac16$ and
$\eta_{k-2}(\varepsilon)/6+\bar y\rho^{k-2}\in B_s$. Then $\overline{N\varepsilon+y}\,\rho^k+\nu\notin h+B_s+\Z[i]$ for
every $\nu\in\Lambda_+\setminus\Z[i]$. The same holds with $\rho^{-k}$, $\rho^{2-k}$, $\eta_{2-k}(\varepsilon)$ and
$\Lambda_-$ in place of $\rho^k$, $\rho^{k-2}$, $\eta_{k-2}(\varepsilon)$ and $\Lambda_+$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Replacing $(\varepsilon,y,\nu)$ by $(i\varepsilon,iy,-i\nu)$ multiplies $\overline{N\varepsilon+y}\,\rho^k+\nu$ by
$-i$ and replaces $\eta_j$ by $-i\eta_j$; it preserves $h+B_s+\Z[i]$ (as $-ih\equiv h$), $E_q$, $\Lambda_+$ and the
hypotheses. So we may assume $\eta_k(\varepsilon)=1+i$, and then $\eta_{k-2}(\varepsilon)=-1-i$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:values}). Let $u=\bar y\rho^k$, so that $\bar y\rho^{k-2}=u(-7-24i)/25$. Suppose the conclusion
fails. Then $\frac16+\nu_e+u_e\in[-s,s]+\Z$ for $e=1,2$, where we take $\nu_e\in\{\pm\frac15,\pm\frac25\}$. As
$|u_e|<\frac16$ and $s<\frac15$, the values $\nu_e=\frac15$ and $\nu_e=\frac25$ are impossible ($\frac16+\nu_e+u_e$
then lies in $(\frac15,\frac{11}{15})$), and $\nu_e=-\frac25$ forces $u_e\ge\frac1{15}-\delta$. Of the four non-zero
classes $\pm(\frac25,\frac15)$, $\pm(-\frac15,\frac25)$ of $\Lambda_+/\Z[i]$ only $\nu=(-\frac25,-\frac15)$ remains,
so $u_1\ge\frac1{15}-\delta$. On the other hand the hypothesis at $\rho^{k-2}$ says that both coordinates of
$u(-7-24i)/25$ are at least $-\delta$: $7u_1-24u_2\le25\delta$ and $24u_1+7u_2\le25\delta$. Adding $7$ times the
first to $24$ times the second gives $625u_1\le775\delta$, that is $u_1\le\frac{31}{25}\delta$. Hence
$\frac1{15}\le\frac{56}{25}\delta$, that is $\delta\ge\frac5{168}$, a contradiction. The second statement follows by
complex conjugation, as in Lemma~\ref{lem:gapC}: $\bar\varepsilon\in E_q$, $\bar h\equiv h$, and
$\eta_{k-2}(\bar\varepsilon)=\overline{\eta_{2-k}(\varepsilon)}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:values}(iii).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:probe}
Let $17/56<\theta\le1/3$, $k\ge1$ and $N=5^k$. Then
\[
S_N^\theta=\bigcup_{\varepsilon\in E_c}(N\varepsilon+P_k)\ \cup\ \bigcup_{\varepsilon\in E_q}(N\varepsilon+Y_k(\varepsilon)).
\]
In particular every $c\in S_N^\theta$ is $c=N\varepsilon+x$, either with $\varepsilon\in E_c$ and $|x|\le
s\sqrt{10}/3$, or with $\varepsilon\in E_q$ and $|x|\le\sqrt2\,\delta$.
\end{proposition}

For $\theta=1/3$ we have $s\sqrt{10}/3=\sqrt{10}/18$ and $\delta=0$, and this is Proposition~\ref{prop:shape}.

\begin{proof}
The inclusion $\supseteq$ is Lemma~\ref{lem:values}(iv). Note that $\theta>17/56$ means $s<11/56$ and
$\delta<5/168$, and that then $s(1+\sqrt2)<\frac35$. The sets on the right are invariant under $N\Z[i]$ and under
multiplication by $i$ (which maps $E_c$ and $E_q$ to themselves modulo $\Z[i]$), and so is $S_N^\theta$. We use
induction on $k$. For $\lambda\in\Z[i]$ put $\nu_\pm(\lambda)=\frac N5\bar\lambda\rho^{\pm k}$. Since $\frac
N5\rho^k=\frac{(2+i)^{2k}}5=\frac{(2+i)^{2k-1}}{2-i}$, we have $\nu_\pm(\lambda)\in\Lambda_\pm$, and
$\nu_+(\lambda)\in\Z[i]$ if and only if $2+i$ divides $\lambda$; likewise $\nu_-(\lambda)\in\Z[i]$ if and only if
$2-i$ divides $\lambda$. So both are Gaussian integers if and only if $5\mid\lambda$.

\emph{The case $k=1$.} Let $c\in S_5^\theta$. The condition at $\rho^0$ gives $c\in h+B_s+\Z[i]$, so
$c=5h+x+\lambda$ with $x\in B_s$ and $\lambda\in\Z[i]$, which we take modulo $5$; put $z=\bar x$, so $|z|\le s\sqrt2$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:values}(ii) the conditions at $\rho^{\pm1}$ read $z\rho^{\pm1}+\nu_\pm(\lambda)\in B_s+\Z[i]$, and
as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:gapC} the shift can be taken to be the representative of $\nu_\pm(\lambda)$
modulo $\Z[i]$ with coordinates in $[-\frac25,\frac25]$. If both $\nu_\pm(\lambda)$ are Gaussian integers, then
$5\mid\lambda$ and $x\in P_1$ (as $|z\rho^{\pm1}|\le s\sqrt2<1-s$). If exactly one is, say $\nu_-(\lambda)$, then
$z\rho^{-1}\in B_s$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:gapC} with $k=1$ contradicts the condition at $\rho$; the other case is
symmetric. In the remaining $16$ classes, multiplication by $i$ leaves four orbits, represented by $\lambda=1$, $2$,
$2+2i$ and $1+i$. Write $(z\rho)_{1,2}=\frac15(3z_1-4z_2,\,4z_1+3z_2)$ and
$(z\rho^{-1})_{1,2}=\frac15(3z_1+4z_2,\,-4z_1+3z_2)$.
\begin{itemize}
\item $\lambda=1$: $\nu_\pm\equiv\frac{-2\mp i}5$, so $(z\rho)_1,(z\rho^{-1})_1\ge\frac25-s$. Their sum is
$\frac65z_1\le\frac65s$, so $\frac45-2s\le\frac65s$ and $s\ge\frac14$.
\item $\lambda=2$: $\nu_\pm\equiv\frac{1\mp2i}5$, so $(z\rho)_1,(z\rho^{-1})_1\le s-\frac15$ and
$(z\rho)_2\ge\frac25-s$. As $7(z\rho)_1+25(z\rho^{-1})_1-24(z\rho)_2=0$, we get $0\le32(s-\frac15)+24(s-\frac25)$, so
$s\ge\frac27$.
\item $\lambda=2+2i$: $\nu_+\equiv\frac{-1+2i}5$ and $\nu_-\equiv\frac{-2+i}5$, so $(z\rho)_2\le s-\frac25$,
$(z\rho^{-1})_1\ge\frac25-s$ and $(z\rho^{-1})_2\le s-\frac15$. As $25(z\rho)_2-24(z\rho^{-1})_1+7(z\rho^{-1})_2=0$, we
get $0\le49(s-\frac25)+7(s-\frac15)$, so $s\ge\frac38$.
\item $\lambda=1+i$: then $c=5\varepsilon+y$ with $\varepsilon=\frac{2+2i}3\in E_q$ and $y=x+\frac{1+i}6$, so
$|y|\le\sqrt2(s+\frac16)<0.52$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:values}(iii), $\bar c\rho^j\in h+\eta_j(\varepsilon)/6+\bar
y\rho^j+\Z[i]$, and as $|\bar y\rho^j|<0.52$ and $\frac16+0.52<1-s$, the condition at $\rho^j$ says
$\eta_j(\varepsilon)/6+\bar y\rho^j\in B_s$. So $y\in Y^5_1(\varepsilon)$.
\end{itemize}
The first three orbits are thus empty, as $s<11/56<\frac14$.

\emph{The step from $k-1$ to $k\ge2$.} Let $c\in S_N^\theta$. As $G_{N/5}\subseteq G_N$, $c\in S_{N/5}^\theta$, and by
induction $c=\frac N5\varepsilon'+x$ with $\varepsilon'\in E_c$ and $x\in P_{k-1}$, or with $\varepsilon'\in E_q$ and
$x\in Y^{N/5}_{k-1}(\varepsilon')$.

If $\varepsilon'=h+t\in E_c$, then $\frac N5\varepsilon'=Nh+\frac N5(t-2-2i)$, so $c=Nh+x+\frac N5\lambda$ with
$\lambda\in\Z[i]$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:values}(ii) the condition at $\rho^{\pm k}$ reads $\bar x\rho^{\pm k}+
\nu_\pm(\lambda)\in B_s+\Z[i]$. Here $|x|\le s\sqrt{10}/3<s\sqrt2$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:radii}, and $\bar
x\rho^{\pm(k-2)}\in B_s$ as $x\in P_{k-1}$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:gapC} both $\nu_\pm(\lambda)$ are Gaussian integers,
so $5\mid\lambda$ and $c\in Nh+x+N\Z[i]$; and $\bar x\rho^{\pm k}\in B_s+\Z[i]$ with $|x|<1-s$ gives $x\in P_k$.

If $\varepsilon'=\frac{\alpha'+\beta'i}3+t'\in E_q$, let $\alpha,\beta\in\{1,2\}$ with $\alpha\equiv2\alpha'$ and
$\beta\equiv2\beta'\pmod3$, and $\varepsilon=\frac{\alpha+\beta i}3$. Then $\frac N5\varepsilon'-N\varepsilon\in\frac
N5\Z[i]$, because $\alpha'-5\alpha\equiv\alpha'-2\alpha\equiv0\pmod3$, and likewise for $\beta$; so
$c=N\varepsilon+x+\frac N5\lambda$ with $\lambda\in\Z[i]$. For $|j|\le k-1$ we have $\frac N5\rho^j\in\Z[i]$, so
$\eta^N_j(\varepsilon)=\eta^{N/5}_j(\varepsilon')$ for $|j|\le k-1$, and $x\in Y^N_{k-1}(\varepsilon)$. In
particular $|x|\le\sqrt2\,\delta<\frac16$, and the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:gapQ} hold at $\rho^{\pm(k-2)}$. The
condition at $\rho^{\pm k}$ says $\overline{N\varepsilon+x}\,\rho^{\pm k}+\nu_\pm(\lambda)\in h+B_s+\Z[i]$, so
by Lemma~\ref{lem:gapQ} both $\nu_\pm(\lambda)$ are Gaussian integers, $5\mid\lambda$ and $c\in N\varepsilon+x+N\Z[i]$.
Finally $\eta_{\pm k}(\varepsilon)/6+\bar x\rho^{\pm k}\in B_s+\Z[i]$ with $|x|<\frac12$ gives $x\in Y_k(\varepsilon)$.
The bounds on $|x|$ are Lemma~\ref{lem:radii}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:D}]
Suppose that $F^2$ has a homomorphism to $K_{p/q}$ with $p/q<56/17$; we show that $\chi(F^2)\le3$. If $i\in F$ this
cannot happen, as then $\chi(F^2)=\infty$ (proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B}). If $p/q\le3$, then $\chi(F^2)\le
\chi(K_{p/q})\le3$. Otherwise $\theta=q/p\in(17/56,1/3)$, and we follow the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:B1} for a finite
$V\subset T$. Theorem~W$^+$ applies, as $p<4q$, and gives a character with $\xi(U)\subseteq[\theta,1-\theta]+\Z$; so
$\varphi(v)\in S_N^\theta$ for $v\in V$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:probe} writes $\varphi(v)=N\varepsilon_v+x_v$ with
$\varepsilon_v\in E$ and $|x_v|\le r_\theta=\max(s\sqrt{10}/3,\sqrt2\,\delta)$ (so $r_{1/3}=r$).
Lemma~\ref{lem:exact} and its proof hold for such points with $r_\theta$ in place of $r$. So, choosing
$N>6r_\theta\max_v(|D_v|+\sum_j|\mu_{v,j}|)$, we obtain $\beta\in L$ with
$\Tr(\beta v)\in E$ for every $v\in V$: the conclusion of Lemma~\ref{lem:B1}. The proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:B2} uses
the $3$-colouring only through this conclusion, and the rest of the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B} only through (c) or
(q). So (a) or (b) holds, and $\chi(F^2)\le3$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:B}. Finally, if $\chi(F^2)\ge4$ and
$\chi_c(F^2)<56/17$, the definition of $\chi_c$ as an infimum gives a homomorphism to some $K_{p/q}$ with
$p/q<56/17$, which we have excluded.
\end{proof}

\emph{A computer-assisted extension.} With exact computations the same method gives
$\chi_c(F^2)\ge10^7/3001611>3.3315$ whenever $\chi(F^2)\ge4$; Theorem~\ref{thm:E} supersedes this, but the
computations show where the one-prime probe stops. Let $\theta^*=0.3001611$. It suffices that for every $k\ge17$ the
conclusion of Proposition~\ref{prop:probe} holds for $\theta^*$, so that every $c\in S_N^{\theta^*}$ is
$N\varepsilon+x$ with $\varepsilon\in E$ and $|x|\le r_{\theta^*}<0.22$: then for $p/q<1/\theta^*$ the argument above
applies with $\theta=q/p$, since $S_N^\theta\subseteq S_N^{\theta^*}$, with $r_{\theta^*}$ in place of $r_\theta$ and
$k\ge17$. We computed
$S_N^{\theta^*}$ exactly, as a finite union of rational polygons modulo $N$, level by level, with three programs
written separately (two of them by independent checkers): all three find the same number of components at every level $k\le19$,
and only the components of Proposition~\ref{prop:probe} for $k=17,18,19$. For $k\ge18$ the induction step of
Proposition~\ref{prop:probe} goes through with Lemmas~\ref{lem:gapC} and~\ref{lem:gapQ} replaced by the same
statements under the hypotheses $x\in P_{k-1}$, resp.\ $y\in Y^N_{k-1}(\varepsilon)$, for every shift
$\nu\in\Lambda_\pm\setminus\Z[i]$ (the programs do not use the reduction to the shifts of the hand proofs). The
infimum of the $\theta$ for which these statements hold is an exact linear-programming value. It is non-increasing in
$k$: in the coordinates $\bar x\rho^k$, resp.\ $\bar y\rho^k$, and since $\eta_{k-m}=i^m\eta_k$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:values}), the system at level $k+1$ contains the one at level $k$. All three programs find that it is
at most $0.30005568<\theta^*$ for $k=12$, $13$ and $14$ (two of them up to $k=22$).

\begin{remark}
(1) For a statement valid for every $k$, the bound $17/56$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:probe} is best possible: for
$\theta=17/56$, $S_N^\theta$ has further isolated points for $N=5$, $25$, $125$ and $625$ ($8$, $20$, $12$ and $4$
of them modulo $N\Z[i]$), at which $\min_\gamma\|\operatorname{Re}(\bar c\gamma)\|=17/56$. But the constant $56/17$
comes from the first levels only: by the same computations, for $\theta>333/1106$ the set $S_{5^5}^\theta$ has only
the components of Proposition~\ref{prop:probe}, and the threshold of the induction step is $3303/10981<333/1106$ for
$4\le k\le6$, hence at most that for $k\ge6$; so the levels $k\le5$ and the threshold at $k=6$ already give
$\chi_c(F^2)\ge1106/333\approx3.3213$.

(2) The method cannot reach beyond $10/3$. For every $k$ the point $c_k=Nh+\frac{\rho^k}5-5^{k-1}(2-i)$ lies in
$S_N^{3/10}$, with $\min_\gamma\|\operatorname{Re}(\bar c_k\gamma)\|=\frac3{10}$: for $-k\le j<k$,
$5^{k-1}(2+i)\rho^j\in\Z[i]$ and $\bar c_k\rho^j\equiv h+\rho^{j-k}/5\pmod{\Z[i]}$, at distance $\frac15$ from
$h$; and $\bar c_k\rho^k\equiv h+\frac15-\frac{2+i}5=h-\frac{1+i}5$, because $(2+i)^{2k+1}\equiv2+i\pmod5$. But its distance to
$NE_c$ is at least $\sqrt5\cdot5^{k-1}-\frac15$, and to $NE_q$ at least $\frac76\,5^{k-1}-\frac15$. So for
$\theta\le3/10$ the probe has characters of a third, $5$-adic, kind. Section~\ref{sec:twoprimes} removes them with a
rotation whose denominator is a power of another prime.

(3) The least $\theta$ above which $S_{5^k}^\theta$ has only the components of Proposition~\ref{prop:probe} is
$17/56$ for $k\le4$, $333/1106$ for $k=5$, $3303/10981$ for $k=6$ and about $0.30016093$ for $k=17$. We expect that it
tends to $3/10$, as do the thresholds of the induction step (about $0.300036$ for $20\le k\le22$); that would give
$\chi_c(F^2)\ge10/3$ by this method.
\end{remark}

\section{Two primes}\label{sec:twoprimes}

The probe of Section~\ref{sec:gap} uses only the rotations $i^a\rho^j$, whose denominators are powers of $5$, and for
$\theta\le3/10$ it has characters of a third kind (Remark~(2) at the end of Section~\ref{sec:gap}). The plane over a
number field contains every rational rotation. We add one with another prime, $\sigma=\frac{3+2i}{3-2i}=
\frac{5+12i}{13}$ (in this section $\sigma$ is this rotation, not the automorphism of Section~\ref{sec:three}), and for
$k,m\ge0$ put
\begin{align*}
G(k,m)&=\{i^a\rho^j\sigma^l:\ a\in\Z,\ |j|\le k,\ |l|\le m\},\\
S^\theta(k,m)&=\{c\in\C:\ \operatorname{Re}(\bar c\gamma)\in[\theta,1-\theta]+\Z\text{ for every }\gamma\in G(k,m)\},
\end{align*}
so that $S^\theta(k,0)=S_N^\theta$ for $N=5^k$. We keep the notation of Section~\ref{sec:gap}, with
$\frac14<\theta\le\frac13$. If $i\notin F$, then $G(k,m)\subset T$: its elements lie in $\Q(i)$ and have norm one, and
the automorphism of $L/F$ restricts to complex conjugation on $\Q(i)$.

For $c\in\C$ and $\gamma\in\Q(i)$, the \emph{strip index} of $c$ at $\gamma$ is
$n_\gamma(c)=\lfloor\operatorname{Re}(\bar c\gamma)\rfloor$. For $c\in S^\theta(k,m)$ and $\gamma\in G(k,m)$,
$\operatorname{Re}(\bar c\gamma)\in[n_\gamma(c)+\theta,n_\gamma(c)+1-\theta]$, and the \emph{margins}
$\operatorname{Re}(\bar c\gamma)-n_\gamma(c)$ and $n_\gamma(c)+1-\operatorname{Re}(\bar c\gamma)$ are at least $\theta$.
The points of $S^\theta(k,m)$ with a given vector of strip indices form a compact convex polygon, on which the margins
are affine functions; two different vectors give disjoint polygons, as some functional takes its values in two
different strips. So the connected components of $S^\theta(k,m)$ correspond to the index vectors that occur, and a
point of $S^\theta(k,m)$ lies in $S^{\theta'}(k,m)$ for $\theta'<\theta$, with the same strip indices. As
$65\gamma\in\Z[i]$ for $\gamma\in G(1,1)$, $S^\theta(1,1)$ is invariant under translation by $65\Z[i]$; its \emph{type
points} are the points of $65h+65\Z[i]$ and of $\frac{65}3(\alpha+\beta i)+65\Z[i]$, $\alpha,\beta\in\{1,2\}$.

\begin{proposition}[computer-assisted]\label{prop:window}
Let $\frac27<\theta\le\frac13$ and $c\in S^\theta(1,1)$. Then there is a type point $c_0$ such that $c$ and $c_0$
have the same strip index at every $\gamma\in G(1,1)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $r_0=\frac7{25}<\frac27$; then $S^\theta(1,1)\subseteq S^{r_0}(1,1)$. As $\operatorname{Re}(\bar c\cdot
i\gamma)=-\operatorname{Im}(\bar c\gamma)$ and $[\theta,1-\theta]+\Z$ is symmetric, only the $18$ functionals
$\operatorname{Re}(\bar c\gamma)$ and $\operatorname{Im}(\bar c\gamma)$ with $\gamma=\rho^j\sigma^l$, $|j|,|l|\le1$,
matter: at a point of $S^{r_0}(1,1)$ and at a type point no value $\operatorname{Re}(\bar c\gamma)$ is an integer, so
$n_{-\gamma}=-n_\gamma-1$ and $n_{i\gamma}=-n_{-i\gamma}-1$, and the strip indices at these $18$ functionals determine
those at all $36$ rotations. The proof is a finite computation with exact certificates of two kinds
(Section~\ref{sec:checks}).

\emph{Completeness.} After a translation by $65\Z[i]$, $c$ lies in a cell $[a+r_0,a+1-r_0]\times[b+r_0,b+1-r_0]$
with $0\le a,b\le64$, which fixes the strip indices at $\gamma=1$ and $\gamma=-i$. For each of the other $16$
functionals, in a fixed order, the possible strip indices are the integers $n$ for which $[n+r_0,n+1-r_0]$ meets the
range of the functional on the cell. A tree lists, for each cell and each branch, every possible index either as a
branch to follow or as closed; a closed branch carries a Farkas certificate: three inequalities valid on the branch
(sides of the cell, and sides of the strips chosen so far) and rational multipliers $\lambda\ge0$ whose combination
is a false inequality between rational constants. The open branches end in $13$ leaves ($15\,620$ branch nodes,
$4\,364$ closed branches), so every $c\in S^{r_0}(1,1)$ has, up to $65\Z[i]$, one of $13$ explicit index vectors.

\emph{The $13$ vectors.} Five are those of the type points. Each of the other eight comes with three of its margins
$\psi_1,\psi_2,\psi_3$ and rationals $\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\lambda_3\ge0$ with $\sum\lambda_i=1$ and
$\sum\lambda_i\psi_i=\frac27$ identically (the linear parts cancel). At a point of $S^\theta(1,1)$ every margin is at
least $\theta>\frac27$, so no such point has one of these eight vectors.
\end{proof}

The eight vectors excluded at the end are those of the $7$-torsion points $\frac{65}7(a+bi)$ with
$(a,b)\in\{(1,2),(1,5),(2,1),(2,6),(5,1),(5,6),(6,2),(6,5)\}$, at which the least margin is exactly $\frac27$. On
$G(1,1)$ they give the characters of the $7$-adic colourings of Proposition~\ref{prop:C1} (the map
$\lambda(a+bi)=2a+3b$ gives $\frac{65}7(1+5i)$). The bound $\frac27$ is optimal for every finite set of rational
rotations: their denominators are prime to $7$ (as $7\equiv3\pmod4$), and if $D$ is a common denominator,
$d\equiv D^{-1}\pmod7$ and $\lambda(a+bi)=sa+tb$ is a map as in Proposition~\ref{prop:C1} with values in
$\{2,3,4,5\}$ on $\mu_8$, then at each of these rotations $\gamma$ the point $Dd(s+ti)/7$ has the value
$\lambda(\gamma\bmod7)/7$, whose distance to $\Z$ is at least $\frac27$. Both primes are needed, and so are the mixed rotations: with the primes $13$ and $17$ the
corresponding window keeps $12$ other components above $\frac27$ (some with largest least margin $\frac25$), and the
rotations $1$, $\rho^{\pm1}$, $\sigma^{\pm1}$ alone keep $180$ other components (some with largest least margin
$\frac{55}{144}>\frac13$); the primes $5$ and
$17$ work as well as $5$ and $13$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:window}
Let $\frac27<\theta\le\frac13$, $k\ge1$, $N=5^k$, and let $C\in\C$ satisfy
$\operatorname{Re}(\bar C\gamma)\in[\theta,1-\theta]+\Z$ for every $\gamma\in G(k,1)$. Then
$C\in N\varepsilon+P_k+N\Z[i]$ for some $\varepsilon\in E_c$, or $C\in N\varepsilon+Y_k(\varepsilon)+N\Z[i]$ for some
$\varepsilon\in E_q$. In particular $C=N\varepsilon+x$ with $\varepsilon\in E$ and
$|x|\le r_\theta=\max(s\sqrt{10}/3,\sqrt2\,\delta)<0.23$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Digits.} For $|j|\le k$ the conditions at $\rho^j$ and $-i\rho^j$ say that both coordinates of $\bar C\rho^j$
lie in $\frac12+[-s,s]+\Z$, so $\bar C\rho^j=h+g_j+n_j$ with $g_j\in B_s$ and $n_j\in\Z[i]$, unique as $s<\frac12$;
here $n_j=n_{\rho^j}(C)+i\,n_{-i\rho^j}(C)$. For $-k<j\le k$ let $\nu_j=g_j-\rho g_{j-1}$. As $\bar C\rho^j=\rho\cdot\bar
C\rho^{j-1}$,
\[
\nu_j=(\rho-1)h+\rho n_{j-1}-n_j,
\]
so $\nu_j$ depends only on the strip indices of $C$ at $\rho^{j-1}$, $-i\rho^{j-1}$, $\rho^j$ and $-i\rho^j$. For any
$c\in\C$ we define $n_t(c)=n_{\rho^t}(c)+i\,n_{-i\rho^t}(c)$, $g_t(c)=\bar c\rho^t-h-n_t(c)$ and
$\nu_t(c)=g_t(c)-\rho g_{t-1}(c)=(\rho-1)h+\rho n_{t-1}(c)-n_t(c)$ in the same way.

\emph{Type points.} Let $|t|\le1$. If $c_0\in65h+65\Z[i]$, then, as $65\rho^t$ is a Gaussian integer of odd norm,
$\bar c_0\rho^t\equiv h\pmod{\Z[i]}$ (as in Lemma~\ref{lem:values}(ii)), and $\nu_0(c_0)=\nu_1(c_0)=0$. If
$c_0\in\frac{65}3(\alpha+\beta i)+65\Z[i]$, then $\bar c_0\rho^t\equiv\frac{\alpha-\beta i}3u_t\pmod{\Z[i]}$ with
$u_t\equiv65\rho^t\equiv-(-i)^t\pmod3$, as $65\equiv-1$ and $\rho\equiv-i\pmod3$. So $g_t(c_0)=\eta_t/6$, with
$\eta_t\in\{\pm1\pm i\}$ and $\eta_{t+1}=-i\eta_t$ (as $-ih\equiv h$), and
\[
\nu_t=\frac{\eta_t-\rho\eta_{t-1}}6=-\frac{(i+\rho)\,\eta_{t-1}}6=-\frac{1+3i}{10}\,\eta_{t-1}\ne0,\qquad
\nu_1=-i\nu_0.
\]

\emph{Windows.} Let $-k<j<k$ and $c=\rho^{-j}C$, so that $\bar c=\rho^j\bar C$. For $\gamma\in G(1,1)$ we have
$\rho^j\gamma\in G(k,1)$, so $c\in S^\theta(1,1)$, and by Proposition~\ref{prop:window} $c$ has the strip indices
of some type point $c_0$. The strip indices of $c$ at $\rho^t$ and $-i\rho^t$ are those of $C$ at $\rho^{j+t}$ and
$-i\rho^{j+t}$; so $(\nu_j,\nu_{j+1})=(\nu_0(c_0),\nu_1(c_0))$, which is $(0,0)$ or $(\nu,-i\nu)$ with
$\nu=-\frac{1+3i}{10}\eta$, $\eta\in\{\pm1\pm i\}$.

\emph{Pure words.} The pairs $(\nu_j,\nu_{j+1})$, $-k<j<k$, cover $\nu_{1-k},\dots,\nu_k$, and consecutive pairs
share a digit. So either all $\nu_j$ vanish, or none does and $\nu_{j+1}=-i\nu_j$ for $1-k\le j<k$.

\emph{Identification.} If all digits vanish, then $g_j=\rho^jg_0$ for $|j|\le k$. Let $x=\bar g_0$; then $\bar
x\rho^j=g_j\in B_s$, so $x\in P_k$, and $\bar C\rho^j\equiv h+\bar x\rho^j\equiv\overline{Nh+x}\,\rho^j
\pmod{\Z[i]}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:values}(ii). Hence $\operatorname{Re}(\bar w\gamma)\in\Z$ for $w=C-Nh-x$ and every
$\gamma$ in $G_N$, so for every $\gamma$ in the $\Z$-span of $G_N$. This span is $\frac1N\Z[i]$: it is a

\subsection*{The places above \texorpdfstring{$7$}{7}}
Let $\F_{49}=\Z[i]/7$, let $\mu_8\subset\F_{49}$ be the group of elements of norm one, which are the residues of the
rational rotations ($\rho\equiv2+5i$ has order $8$, and $\rho^2\equiv-i$), and let
\[
A'_7=\{e\in\F_{49}:\ \operatorname{Re}(\bar ez)\in\{2,3,4,5\}\text{ for every }z\in\mu_8\},
\]
where $\operatorname{Re}(a+bi)=a$ for $a,b\in\F_7$. Elements of $E_7=\{\varepsilon\in\frac17\Z[i]:\
7\varepsilon\bmod7\in A'_7\}$ have \emph{type~$7$}; let $E'=E\cup E_7$, so that $42E'\subseteq\Z[i]$. For
$\varepsilon\in E_7$ the character $a\mapsto\operatorname{Re}(\bar\varepsilon\tilde a)\bmod1$ of $\Z_{(7)}[i]$, where
$\tilde a\in\Z[i]$ is congruent to $a$ modulo $7\Z_{(7)}[i]$ (it factors through $\F_{49}$), takes values in
$\frac17\{2,3,4,5\}+\Z$ at every rational rotation: these are the characters of the $7$-adic colourings of
Proposition~\ref{prop:C1}. Let $\tilde\rho=2+5i$, and for $j\in\Z$ let $\zeta_j(\varepsilon)\in\{\pm\frac1{14},
\pm\frac3{14}\}^2$ be given by $\bar\varepsilon\tilde\rho^{\,j}\in h+\zeta_j(\varepsilon)+\Z[i]$ (with $\tilde\rho^{\,j}$
for $j<0$ read through $\tilde\rho^{\,8}\equiv1\pmod 7$).

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:seven}
\textup{(i)} $A'_7=\{e\in\F_{49}:\ e\bar e=-1\}$, that is, $A'_7$ is the set of the $a+bi$ with
$(a,b)\in\{(2,3),(2,4),(3,2),(3,5),(4,2),(4,5),(5,3),(5,4)\}$. It is a single $\mu_8$-orbit, it is closed under conjugation, its elements add up to $0$, and $0\notin A'_7$.

\textup{(ii)} $A'_7$ contains no coset of a nonzero additive subgroup of $\F_{49}$.

\textup{(iii)} $\operatorname{Re}(A'_7)=\{2,3,4,5\}$.

\textup{(iv)} For $\varepsilon\in E_7$, $\zeta_{j+2}(\varepsilon)=-i\zeta_j(\varepsilon)$, and the digits
$\zeta_j(\varepsilon)-\rho\zeta_{j-1}(\varepsilon)$ form a sequence of period $8$ in which the digit $0$ alternates with digits $u\frac{2+i}5$, $u\in\mu_4\subset\C$; the eight classes of
$E_7$ modulo $\Z[i]$ give its eight shifts, as $\zeta_t(\varepsilon')=\zeta_{t+j}(\varepsilon)$ when
$7\varepsilon'\equiv7\varepsilon\,\overline{\tilde\rho}^{\,j}\pmod7$ \textup{(}$\tilde\rho\,\overline{\tilde\rho}\equiv1
\pmod7$\textup{)}. Two consecutive digits, at given positions, of a sequence of type c \textup{(}all digits
$0$\textup{)}, q \textup{(}the digits of the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:window}, all nonzero\textup{)} or $7$ determine its
type and its class.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $e=a+bi$ and $z=x+yi$ with $a,b,x,y\in\F_7$. Then $\operatorname{Re}(\bar ez)=ax+by$ and $e\bar e=a^2+b^2$,
which vanishes only for $e=0$, as $-1$ is not a square modulo $7$; the nonzero squares modulo $7$ are $1$, $2$ and
$4$.

(i) The group $\mu_8$ is the conic $x^2+y^2=1$. Let $e\ne0$ and $t\in\F_7$. The points of the line $ax+by=t$ are
$\frac t{e\bar e}(a,b)+s(-b,a)$, $s\in\F_7$, and such a point lies on the conic if and only if
$(e\bar e)^2s^2=e\bar e-t^2$. So $t$ is a value of $z\mapsto\operatorname{Re}(\bar ez)$ on $\mu_8$ if and only if
$e\bar e-t^2$ is $0$ or a square. Hence $e\in A'_7$ if and only if $e\ne0$ and neither $e\bar e$ nor $e\bar e-1$ is
$0$ or a square (the values $t=0$ and $t=\pm1$), that is, $e\bar e$ and $e\bar e-1$ lie in $\{3,5,6\}$, which means
$e\bar e=6=-1$. The solutions of $a^2+b^2=-1$ have $\{a^2,b^2\}=\{2,4\}$; they are the eight elements listed. As
$(ez)\overline{(ez)}=e\bar e$ for $z\in\mu_8$, $A'_7=e_0\mu_8$ for every $e_0\in A'_7$. It is closed under
conjugation, its elements add up to $e_0\sum_{z\in\mu_8}z=0$, as $\mu_8=-\mu_8$, and
$0\notin A'_7$.

(ii) A nonzero additive subgroup of $\F_{49}\cong\F_7^2$ is a line or all of $\F_{49}$, so each of its cosets contains
a set $\{e+sd:\ s\in\F_7\}$ with $d\ne0$, which has $7$ elements. On it $(e+sd)\overline{(e+sd)}=e\bar e+s(e\bar d+\bar
ed)+s^2d\bar d$ is a polynomial of degree $2$ in $s$, as $d\bar d\ne0$; so at most two of these elements lie in
$A'_7$, and the coset is not contained in it.

(iii) This is read off the list in (i).

(iv) By the list in (i), both coordinates of every element of $A'_7$ lie in $\{2,3,4,5\}$, and for these $c$,
$\frac c7-\frac12=\frac{2c-7}{14}\in\{\pm\frac1{14},\pm\frac3{14}\}$; as $7\bar\varepsilon\tilde\rho^{\,j}\bmod7$ lies in
$A'_7$, which is closed under conjugation and stable under $\mu_8$, $\zeta_j(\varepsilon)$ is defined. As $\tilde\rho^{\,2}=-21+20i\equiv-i\pmod7$ and $7\bar\varepsilon\in\Z[i]$,
$\bar\varepsilon\tilde\rho^{\,j+2}\equiv-i\,\bar\varepsilon\tilde\rho^{\,j}\pmod{\Z[i]}$; as $-ih\equiv h\pmod{\Z[i]}$
and $\{\pm\frac1{14},\pm\frac3{14}\}^2$ is stable under multiplication by $-i$, $\zeta_{j+2}(\varepsilon)=
-i\zeta_j(\varepsilon)$. So the digits $\nu_j=\zeta_j(\varepsilon)-\rho\zeta_{j-1}(\varepsilon)$ satisfy
$\nu_{j+2}=-i\nu_j$. For $7\varepsilon\equiv2+3i$ we have $7\bar\varepsilon\equiv2+4i$, $(2+4i)\tilde\rho\equiv5+4i$
and $(2+4i)\tilde\rho^{\,2}\equiv4+5i\pmod7$, so $\zeta_0=\frac{-3+i}{14}$, $\zeta_1=\frac{3+i}{14}$ and
$\zeta_2=\frac{1+3i}{14}$; as $\rho\zeta_0=-\frac{13+9i}{70}$ and $\rho\zeta_1=\frac{1+3i}{14}$, we get
$\nu_1=\frac{2+i}5$ and $\nu_2=0$. Hence $\nu_{2m}=0$ and $\nu_{2m+1}=(-i)^m\frac{2+i}5$ for every $m$, a sequence
of period $8$ of the stated form. If $7\varepsilon'\equiv7\varepsilon\,\overline{\tilde\rho}^{\,j}\pmod7$, then
$\bar\varepsilon'\tilde\rho^{\,t}\equiv\bar\varepsilon\tilde\rho^{\,t+j}\pmod{\Z[i]}$, so
$\zeta_t(\varepsilon')=\zeta_{t+j}(\varepsilon)$; as $\overline{\tilde\rho}\equiv\tilde\rho^{\,-1}$ generates $\mu_8$
and $A'_7$ is one $\mu_8$-orbit, the eight classes of $E_7$ give the eight shifts $j=0,\dots,7$. Finally, two
consecutive digits are both $0$ for type c, both nonzero for type q, and one $0$ and one nonzero for type $7$. For
type q the class is the sequence $(\eta_t)$, with $\eta_{t+1}=-i\eta_t$, and one digit
$\nu_j=-\frac{1+3i}{10}\eta_{j-1}$ determines it, as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:window}. For type $7$, the position of the nonzero digit $(-i)^m\frac{2+i}5$ and $m$ modulo $4$
determine the shift modulo $8$, hence the class.
\end{proof}

The set $S^\theta(2,1)$ is invariant under $325\Z[i]$, and its \emph{type points} are the points of $325h+325\Z[i]$
(type c), of $\frac{325}3(\alpha+\beta i)+325\Z[i]$ with $\alpha,\beta\in\{1,2\}$ (type q), and of

Strip indices are floors, so they are defined at these points also for $\theta>\frac27$.

\begin{proposition}[computer-assisted]\label{prop:window7}
Let $\frac14<\theta\le\frac13$ and $c\in S^\theta(2,1)$. Then there is a type point $c_0$, of type c, q or $7$,
such that $c$ and $c_0$ have the same strip index at every $\gamma\in G(2,1)$. If $\theta>\frac27$, then $c_0$ has
type c or q.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
As for Proposition~\ref{prop:window}, with $r_0=\frac{249}{1000}<\frac14$ and the $30$ functionals of the $15$
rotations $\rho^j\sigma^l$, $|j|\le2$, $|l|\le1$: the tree over the $325^2$ cells has $550\,474$ branch nodes,
$199\,920$ closed branches and $29$ leaves. Of the $29$ index vectors, five are those of the points of type c and q,
eight those of the points of type $7$, and each of the other $16$ has a certificate $\lambda\ge0$, $\sum\lambda_i=1$,
$\sum\lambda_i\psi_i=\frac14$ on three margins, which excludes it for $\theta>\frac14$. Each of the eight vectors of
type $7$ has a certificate of the same kind, with three positive multipliers and $\sum\lambda_i\psi_i=\frac27$
identically, which excludes it for $\theta>\frac27$; these certificates use margins at the rotations $\rho^{-2}\sigma^l$, outside $G(1,1)$.
\end{proof}

The $16$ excluded vectors are $2$-adic: their largest least margin $\frac14$ is attained at $4$- and $8$-torsion
points such as $\frac{325}4(1+i)$ and $\frac{325}8(2+i)$. So $\frac14$ is a limit of the method, consistent with
$\chi_c(\Q(\sqrt{59})^2)=4$. The window $G(1,1)$ does not suffice below $\frac27$: for $\theta=0.2501$ it has other
components, with largest least margins $\frac{11}{42}$ and $\frac{25}{96}$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:window7}

$|x|\le r'=(\frac14+\frac3{14})\sqrt2<0.66$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Define $g_j$, $n_j$ and the digits $\nu_j$ as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:window}. For $|j|\le k-2$ the point
$c=\rho^{-j}C$ lies in $S^\theta(2,1)$, as $\rho^jG(2,1)\subseteq G(k,1)$; by Proposition~\ref{prop:window7} the digits

Lemma~\ref{lem:seven}(iv) for type $7$, as the values of such a point at $\rho^t$ are those of a character of type $7$.

given positions. By Lemma~\ref{lem:seven}(iv) two of them determine the type and the class of each window: if the

$-1,0,1,2$), then the window at $j+1$ has type $7$ and the class $\varepsilon_{j+1}$ with
$7\varepsilon_{j+1}\equiv7\varepsilon_j\overline{\tilde\rho}\pmod7$, and likewise for the types c and q. So all

and with $x=\overline{g_0-\zeta_0(\varepsilon)}$ we get $g_j=\zeta_j(\varepsilon)+\bar x\rho^j$ for $|j|\le k$. So

$|g_0|+|\zeta_0(\varepsilon)|\le\sqrt2(s+\frac3{14})$, each less than $r'$ as $s<\frac14$.
\end{proof}

\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:B4} applies with $7$, $\Q_{49}$, $\F_{49}$ and $A'_7$ in place of $3$, $\Q_9$, $\F_9$ and
$A'$ (there $\sigma$ is the automorphism of $L_u/F_u$): it uses that the prime is odd, that the order of $7$ modulo $q+1$ is $2f$, and, at the end,
Lemma~\ref{lem:seven}(ii) in place of the fact that $A'$ contains no coset of a nonzero subgroup of $\F_9$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[residue fields]\label{lem:B5seven}
Let $f$ be odd and $q=7^f$. If some $\F_{49}$-linear map $\lambda_1:\F_{q^2}\to\F_{49}$ satisfies
$\lambda_1(\mu_{q+1})\subseteq A'_7$, then $f=1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:seven}(iii), $\operatorname{Re}\circ\lambda_1:\F_{q^2}\to\F_7$ is additive and takes values in
$\{2,3,4,5\}$ on $\mu_{q+1}$; read in $\frac17\Z/\Z$, it stays at distance at least $\frac27$ from $0$ there. So
$\kappa_1>0$ for the unramified extension of $\Q_7$ of degree $f$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:residue} gives $f=1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:F}]
If (a) holds, $\chi_c(F^2)=\chi(F^2)=2$. If (b) holds and (a) fails, $\chi(F^2)=3$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:B}, so
$\chi_c(F^2)=3$ by Corollary~\ref{cor:B6}. If (7) holds and (a), (b) fail, $\chi(F^2)\ge4$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:B}, so
$\chi_c(F^2)\ge7/2$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:E} (which also follows from Proposition~\ref{prop:window7}; Remark~(1)
below), and $\chi_c(F^2)\le7/2$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:C1}, as a place above
$7$ of residue degree $1$ has residue field $\F_7$.

It remains to show that if $F^2$ has a homomorphism to $K_{p/q}$ with $p/q<4$, then (a), (b) or (7) holds; then
$\chi_c(F^2)\ge4$ when all three fail. If $i\in F$ there is no such homomorphism, as $\chi(F^2)=\infty$. If
$p/q\le3$, then $\chi(F^2)\le3$, and (a) or (b) holds by Theorem~\ref{thm:B}. Otherwise $3<p/q<4$, and by
Lemmas~\ref{lem:B1seven} and~\ref{lem:B2seven} we are in case (c), (q) or (7$'$). Cases (c) and (q) give (a) or (b)
as in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B}. In case (7$'$), Lemmas~\ref{lem:B3seven}, \ref{lem:B4seven}
and~\ref{lem:B5seven} give a place above $7$ of residue degree $1$: this is (7).
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[local fields]\label{cor:localF}
Let $K$ be a finite extension of $\Q_p$. Then $\chi_c(K^2)=2$ if $p=2$ and $K(i)/K$ is ramified; $\chi_c(K^2)=3$ if
$p=3$ and $K$ has residue degree $1$; $\chi_c(K^2)=7/2$ if $p=7$ and $K$ has residue degree $1$; and
$\chi_c(K^2)\ge4$ in every other case. Consequently, for every number field $F$,
\[
\min\bigl(\chi_c(F^2),4\bigr)=\min\Bigl(4,\ \min_v\chi_c(F_v^2)\Bigr),
\]
the inner minimum over all places $v$ of $F$: below $4$, the circular chromatic number of the plane over a number
field is the least circular chromatic number of the planes over its completions.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
In the first three cases the upper bounds are the colourings of Corollary~\ref{cor:local} (for $p=2$ and $p=3$) and
of Proposition~\ref{prop:C1}, which colour $K^2$ itself. The lower bounds: a bipartite plane with an edge has
$\chi_c=2$; for $p=3$, $K\supseteq\Q_3\supset\Q(\sqrt7)$ and $\chi_c(\Q(\sqrt7)^2)=3$ by Corollary~\ref{cor:B6}, as
$\chi(\Q(\sqrt7)^2)=3$; for $p=7$, Corollary~\ref{cor:sevenhalves}. In the other cases, if $i\in K$ then
$\chi(K^2)=\infty$. Otherwise we find, as in the proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:local}, a number field $F\subset K$ for
which (a), (b) and (7) fail; then $\chi_c(K^2)\ge\chi_c(F^2)\ge4$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:F}. The construction there
works with $\Q_7$ added to the primes $p$, $p'$ (and with $7$ in the role of $p$ when $p=7$): over $\Q_7$ take a
product of distinct irreducible quadratics whose roots generate $\Q_{49}$, so that the places of $F$ above $7$ contain
$i$; over $\Q_p$ take $h_Kh'$ as there, where for $p=7$ the factor $h'$ consists of such quadratics and, if
$[K:\Q_7]$ is odd, one cubic factor generating the unramified cubic extension of $\Q_7$; and choose the auxiliary
prime of the irreducibility condition outside $\{2,3,7,p\}$. If $p\notin\{2,3,7\}$, take over $\Q_p$ the polynomial
$h_K$ times distinct linear factors coprime to it, to adjust the degree. Then every place of $F$ above $2$ contains $i$ or is the
place giving $K$, where $K(i)/K$ is unramified; every place above $3$ contains $i$ or has residue degree at least $3$;
and every place above $7$ contains $i$ or has odd residue degree at least $3$.

For the last statement, $F^2\subseteq F_v^2$ gives $\chi_c(F^2)\le\chi_c(F_v^2)$ for every $v$. If $\chi_c(F^2)<4$,
Theorem~\ref{thm:F} gives (a), (b) or (7), with $\chi_c(F^2)=2$, $3$ or $7/2$ accordingly, and the completion at the
corresponding place has the same value by the first part.
\end{proof}

For example $\chi_c(\Q_{19}^2)$ and $\chi_c(\Q(\sqrt{47})^2)$ lie in $[4,19/4]$: the lower bound is
Corollary~\ref{cor:localF}, resp.\ Theorem~\ref{thm:F}, and the upper bound is Proposition~\ref{prop:C1} at $19$
with $m=4$, as $\lambda(a+bi)=4a+5b$ takes its values in $\{4,\dots,15\}$ on $\mu_{20}\subset\F_{361}$ (and
$\kappa_1=4/19$; as $47\equiv3^2\pmod{19}$, $\sqrt{47}\in\Q_{19}$). The same holds for $\Q(\sqrt5,\sqrt7)$. So if
$\chi(\Q(\sqrt{47})^2)=5$, which is open \cite[Corollary~2]{FC}, its circular chromatic number lies in $(4,19/4]$ and is not
an integer.

\subsection*{Finite witnesses}
By compactness, $\chi_c(F^2)$ is the supremum of $\chi_c(H)$ over the finite subgraphs $H$ of $F^2$: if every finite
subgraph has a homomorphism to $K_{p/q}$, so does $F^2$. Below $4$ the supremum is attained
(Corollary~\ref{cor:finite}), and at $4$ as well (Theorem~\ref{thm:finite4}). In this subsection $s$ denotes an element of a connection set, not the number
$\frac12-\theta$. For a homomorphism $c$
of a graph to $K_{p/q}$, the \emph{tight digraph} $D_c$ has an arc $x\to y$ for every edge $xy$ with
$c(y)-c(x)\equiv q\pmod p$, and a \emph{tight cycle} of $c$ is a directed cycle of $D_c$. For a finite set $S$ in an
abelian group let $\kappa(S)=\max_\xi\min_{s\in S}\|\xi(s)\|$, over the characters $\xi$ of $\Z S$ (a maximum, as the
dual group is compact); if $\chi_c(\Cay(\Z S,S))<4$, then $\chi_c(\Cay(\Z S,S))=1/\kappa(S)$
\cite[Corollary~2]{Winding}. The next lemma is the easy half of a characterisation due to Guichard \cite{Guichard}
(cf.\ \cite{Zhu}); we include its short proof.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:tight}
Let $H$ be a finite graph with a homomorphism to $K_{p/q}$. If every homomorphism of $H$ to $K_{p/q}$ has a tight
cycle, then $\chi_c(H)=p/q$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose that $\chi_c(H)<p/q$. By definition $H$ has a homomorphism $c'$ to some $K_{p'/q'}$ with $p'/q'<p/q$. For
$x\in V(H)$ let $g(x)=\frac p{p'}c'(x)\in[0,p)$, reading $c'(x)$ in $\{0,\dots,p'-1\}$, and $c(x)=\lfloor
g(x)\rfloor\bmod p$. For an edge $xy$ let $D\in[\frac{pq'}{p'},p-\frac{pq'}{p'}]$ be the lift of $g(y)-g(x)$ modulo
$p$; as $\frac{pq'}{p'}>q$, we have $q<D<p-q$. The integer $\lfloor g(x)+D\rfloor-\lfloor g(x)\rfloor$ is
$\lfloor D\rfloor$ or $\lceil D\rceil$, so it lies in $[q,p-q]$, and it is congruent to $c(y)-c(x)$; so $c$ is a
homomorphism to $K_{p/q}$, and the arc $x\to y$ is tight exactly when this integer is $q$. Along a closed walk these
integers have the same sum as the lifts $D$, as the floors telescope. On a tight cycle of length $m$ the first sum is
$mq$, while the second is larger than $mq$. So $c$ has no tight cycle.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:winding}
Let $\Gamma$ be an abelian group, $S=-S\subseteq\Gamma$ finite with $\Gamma=\Z S$, $p<4q$, and $c$ a homomorphism of
$\Cay(\Gamma,S)$ to $K_{p/q}$. For $x\in\Gamma$ and $s\in S$ let $\ell(x,s)\in[q,p-q]$ be the integer congruent to
$c(x+s)-c(x)$ modulo $p$, and $a(s)=\mathrm M_x\,\ell(x,s)$ for an invariant mean $\mathrm M$ on $\Gamma$. Then
$a(-s)=p-a(s)$, and $s_1+\dots+s_m\mapsto\frac1p\sum_ka(s_k)\bmod1$ \textup{(}$s_k\in S$\textup{)} is a well-defined
character $\xi$ of $\Gamma$, with $\xi(s)=a(s)/p\in[\frac qp,1-\frac qp]$ for $s\in S$. If $u_1,\dots,u_r\in S$ and
positive integers $n_1,\dots,n_r$ satisfy $\sum_in_iu_i=0$ and $a(u_i)=q$ for every $i$, then every closed walk in
$\Cay(\Gamma,S)$ with $n_i$ steps $u_i$ is a closed walk in $D_c$, and so $c$ has a tight cycle.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first part is the proof of Theorem~W$^+$ in \cite{Winding}. For $s,t\in S$ the integers
$\ell(x,s)+\ell(x+s,t)$ and $\ell(x,t)+\ell(x+t,s)$ are congruent modulo $p$ and lie in $[2q,2p-2q]$, an interval of
length $2p-4q<p$; so they are equal. For a closed walk $W$ with steps $s_1,\dots,s_m$ started at $x$, with
$x_k=x+s_1+\dots+s_k$, the sum $\Lambda(W,x)=\sum_k\ell(x_{k-1},s_k)$ is a multiple of $p$, and for $t\in S$ the
identity gives $\Lambda(W,x+t)-\Lambda(W,x)=\sum_k\bigl(\ell(x_k,t)-\ell(x_{k-1},t)\bigr)=0$. As $S$ generates
$\Gamma$, $\Lambda(W,x)$ does not depend on $x$, so by invariance of $\mathrm M$ it equals $\sum_ka(s_k)$. As
$\ell(x+s,-s)=p-\ell(x,s)$, invariance also gives $a(-s)=p-a(s)$. So $\xi$ is well defined: two expressions of the
same element give a closed walk. For the walk of the statement, $\Lambda(W,x)=q\sum_in_i$ for every $x$, while each
of its $\sum_in_i$ terms is at least $q$; so every term is $q$. A closed walk in $D_c$ contains a directed cycle.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[{\cite[Corollary~16]{Winding}}]\label{lem:finiteS}
Let $\Gamma$ be an abelian group, $S=-S\subseteq\Gamma$ finite with $\Gamma=\Z S$, and suppose that
$\chi_c(\Cay(\Gamma,S))=p/q$ with $2<p/q<4$. Then every homomorphism of $\Cay(\Gamma,S)$ to $K_{p/q}$ has a tight
cycle, and some finite subgraph of $\Cay(\Gamma,S)$ has circular chromatic number $p/q$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Here $\kappa(S)=q/p$, so $\Cay(\Gamma,S)$ has a homomorphism to $K_{p/q}$. Let $c$ be one, and $a$, $\xi$ as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:winding}; let $S_q=\{s\in S:a(s)=q\}$. Suppose that no nonzero nonnegative integers $n_t$ give
$\sum_{t\in S_q}n_tt=0$. Then the images $\bar t$ of the elements of $S_q$ in $\Gamma\otimes\R$ have no nonzero
nonnegative real relation: in a basis of $\Gamma\otimes\Q$ the cone of such relations is cut out by rational
equations, so if it were nonzero it would contain a nonzero rational, hence integral, point $(n_t)$, and then
$\sum n_tt$ would be a torsion element, killed by some $m\ge1$. By Gordan's theorem there is a linear form on
$\Gamma\otimes\R$, hence a homomorphism $h:\Gamma\to\R$, with $h(t)>0$ for every $t\in S_q$. For $\varepsilon>0$,
$\xi_\varepsilon=\xi+\varepsilon h\bmod1$ is a character of $\Gamma$, and for $s\in S$: if $a(s)=q$, then
$\xi_\varepsilon(s)=\frac qp+\varepsilon h(s)$; if $a(s)=p-q$, then $-s\in S_q$ and
$\xi_\varepsilon(s)=1-\frac qp-\varepsilon h(-s)$; otherwise $\xi(s)$ lies in the open interval
$(\frac qp,1-\frac qp)$, which is nonempty as $p>2q$. As $S$ is finite, for small $\varepsilon$ all values
$\xi_\varepsilon(s)$ lie in that open interval, so $\kappa(S)>q/p$: a contradiction. So such $n_t$ exist, and
Lemma~\ref{lem:winding}, applied to the $t$ with $n_t>0$, gives a tight cycle of $c$.

If every finite subgraph of $\Cay(\Gamma,S)$ had a homomorphism to $K_{p/q}$ without tight cycles, so would
$\Cay(\Gamma,S)$: for each finite $H\subset\Gamma$ the maps $\Gamma\to\Z/p$ whose restriction to $H$ is such a
homomorphism of the subgraph induced on $H$ form a nonempty closed subset of the compact space $(\Z/p)^\Gamma$;
these subsets have the finite intersection property, and a tight cycle is finite. So some finite subgraph $H$ has a
tight cycle for every homomorphism to $K_{p/q}$, and $\chi_c(H)=p/q$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:tight}, as
$\chi_c(H)\le p/q$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[finite witnesses]\label{cor:finite}
If $\chi_c(F^2)<4$, then some finite subgraph of $F^2$ has circular chromatic number $\chi_c(F^2)$. If
$\chi_c(F^2)\in\{3,7/2\}$, there is a finite set $U\subset T$ with $\chi_c(\Cay(\Z U,U))=\chi_c(F^2)$, and the
subgraph can be taken in $\Cay(\Z U,U)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By Theorem~\ref{thm:F} the value is $2$, $3$ or $7/2$, and $i\notin F$, as otherwise $\chi(F^2)=\infty$. For $2$ an
edge is a witness. Fix a $\Q(i)$-basis $b_1,\dots,b_n\subset T$ of $L$ as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:B2}. It is
enough to find a finite $U\subset T$ with $\chi_c(\Cay(\Z U,U))=\chi_c(F^2)$, as Lemma~\ref{lem:finiteS} then gives
the witness inside $\Cay(\Z U,U)\subseteq F^2$.

Let $\chi_c(F^2)=7/2$, so that (7) holds and (a) and (b) fail. If for every finite $V\supseteq\{b_j\}$ there were
$\beta\in L$ with $\Tr(\beta v)\in E$ for every $v\in V$, the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:B2} (the images of $\beta$ lie
in $K_2$ and $K_3$, and the types of the $\Tr(\beta v)$ give $\tau$) and the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:B} would give
(a) or (b). So there is a finite $V_0\supseteq\{b_j\}$ for which no such $\beta$ exists. Let $k$, with $6\mid k$, and
$N=5^k$ be as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:B1seven} for $V_0$ (they do not depend on $\theta$), and
$U_0=G(k,1)V_0$. If $\kappa(U_0)>\frac27$, take $\theta$ with $\frac27<\theta\le\min(\kappa(U_0),\frac13)$ and a
character $\xi$ of $\Z U_0$ with $\xi(U_0)\subseteq[\theta,1-\theta]+\Z$. The proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:B1seven}, in
which the third case of Lemma~\ref{lem:window7} does not occur as $\theta>\frac27$, gives $\beta\in L$ with
$\Tr(\beta v)\in E$ for every $v\in V_0$: impossible. So $\kappa(U_0)\le\frac27$. The homomorphism of $F^2$ to
$K_{7/2}$ of Proposition~\ref{prop:C1} restricts to $\Cay(\Z U_0,U_0)$, so by Theorem~W$^+$, $\kappa(U_0)\ge\frac27$.
Hence $\kappa(U_0)=\frac27$ and $\chi_c(\Cay(\Z U_0,U_0))=\frac72$.

Let $\chi_c(F^2)=3$, so that (b) holds and (a) fails (Corollary~\ref{cor:B6}, Theorem~\ref{thm:B}). As in the proof
of Corollary~\ref{cor:B6}, if for every finite $V\supseteq\{b_j\}$ there were $\beta\in L$ with $\Tr(\beta v)\in E_c$
for every $v\in V$, the conditions $P_v$ of the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:B2} could all be taken with $\tau(v)=\mathrm
c$, and (c), hence (a), would hold. So there is a finite $V_0\supseteq\{b_j\}$ for which no such $\beta$ exists. Let
$N$ be as in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:B1} for $V_0$, and $U_0=G_NV_0$. If $\kappa(U_0)>\frac13$, a character with
$\xi(U_0)\subseteq[\theta,1-\theta]+\Z$, $\frac13<\theta\le\kappa(U_0)$, gives as in the proof of
Corollary~\ref{cor:B6} points $\varphi(v)=N\varepsilon_v+x_v$ with every $\varepsilon_v$ of type c (a point of type q
would have real part in $\{\frac13,\frac23\}+\Z$, as $3\nmid N$), and then $\beta$ with $\Tr(\beta v)=\varepsilon_v
\in E_c$ for every $v\in V_0$: impossible. So $\kappa(U_0)\le\frac13$; as $F^2$ is $3$-colourable, $\kappa(U_0)\ge
\frac13$ by Theorem~W$^+$, and $\chi_c(\Cay(\Z U_0,U_0))=3$.
\end{proof}

with different variable numberings and arc indicators, and the DRAT proofs of both are verified, one of them also,

and $293$ vertices again with programs of its own: a third encoding (renamed variables, two-sided arc indicators),
cycle lists rebuilt from scratch, \texttt{cake\_lpr} on the stored proofs, and the explicit homomorphisms
$Nc+\mathrm{pos}$ of the graphs $H-v$ (\nolinkurl{data/number_fields/circular/finite_witness/}); those with $155$
and $324$ vertices (over $\Q(\sqrt{11})$ and $\Q(\sqrt{911})$), found afterwards, were checked by the two programs above
and, by us, with the referee's programs, with the same results. As $\sqrt{11}\in\Q_7$, $H_{11}$ is also an explicit
witness for $\Q_7$ and for every finite extension $K$ of $\Q_7$ with $\chi_c(K^2)<4$.

The same procedure with $K_3$ in place of $K_{7/2}$ (an arc is now tight when the colour increases by $1$ modulo
$3$) gives a witness for the value $3$ in a plane without unit triangles. Over $\Q(\sqrt7)$, where $\chi_c=3$, it
starts from $0$, the $204$ unit vectors with denominator $160$ and two more points that close a $5$-cycle, stops at
$607$ vertices, and vertex deletion leaves nine points; translated by $(1,0)$, they are
\begin{gather*}
P_0=(-1,0),\quad P_1=\Bigl(-\frac{3+\sqrt7}8,-\frac{5+\sqrt7}8\Bigr),\quad P_2=\Bigl(\frac14,-\frac{\sqrt7}4\Bigr),
\quad P_3=\Bigl(\frac{\sqrt7}4,\frac14\Bigr),\\
P_4=\Bigl(-\frac14,\frac{\sqrt7}4\Bigr),\quad P_5=\Bigl(\frac{3-\sqrt7}8,\frac{\sqrt7-5}8\Bigr),\quad P_6=(1,0),
\quad P_7=(0,0),\quad S=(0,1).
\end{gather*}
The pairs at distance $1$ among them form the $8$-cycle $P_0P_1\cdots P_7$, the chords $P_0P_4$, $P_1P_5$, $P_2P_6$
and the path $P_3SP_7$: their unit-distance graph $H_7$ is the Wagner graph, the M\"obius ladder on $8$ vertices,
with one of its four chords subdivided. It contains the hexagon $P_0P_4P_3P_2P_6P_7$, the paths $P_0P_1P_2$,
$P_4P_5P_6$ and $P_3SP_7$ that join its opposite vertices, and one more edge, $P_1P_5$. The subgraph
$M=H_7-P_1P_5$, a hexagon $v_0\cdots v_5$ whose three long diagonals $v_jv_{j+3}$ are subdivided by vertices $m_j$
(here $v_0,\dots,v_5=P_0,P_4,P_3,P_2,P_6,P_7$ and $m_0,m_1,m_2=P_1,P_5,S$), already has a tight cycle in every proper
$3$-colouring $c$. Indeed, for an arc $a\to b$ let $\delta(a,b)=\pm1$ with $c(b)-c(a)\equiv\delta(a,b)\pmod3$;
along a closed walk the sum of $\delta$ is divisible by $3$, so it is $\pm3$ on a $5$-cycle, and on a $6$-cycle it is
$0$ or $\pm6$, and $\pm6$ makes the cycle tight in one direction. Let $Z_k$ be the $5$-cycle
$v_kv_{k+1}v_{k+2}v_{k+3}m_k$ (indices of $v$ modulo $6$, of $m$ modulo $3$). As $1$-chains, $C_k=Z_{k+1}-Z_k$ is the
$6$-cycle $v_km_kv_{k+3}v_{k+4}m_{k+1}v_{k+1}$ and $Z_0+Z_3$ is the hexagon (each cycle read in the order written);
$C_{k+3}$ is $C_k$ reversed, and $Z_3-Z_0=C_0+C_1+C_2$. If none of $C_0,C_1,C_2$ is tight in either direction, their
sums are $0$, the sums over $Z_0$ and $Z_3$ are equal, to $\pm3$, and the hexagon, with sum $\pm6$, is tight. (In
$H_7$ the cycle $C_0=P_0P_1P_2P_6P_5P_4$ has the chord $P_1P_5$ and is never tight; the cycles stored with $H_7$ are
$C_1$, $C_2$ and the hexagon, in both directions.) As $H_7$ is $3$-colourable, $\chi_c(M)=\chi_c(H_7)=3$ by
Lemma~\ref{lem:tight}. Equivalently, $H_7$ has no homomorphism to $K_{8/3}$, which is the Wagner graph itself (the
vertices $0,3,6,1,4,7,2,5$ of $K_{8/3}$ form an $8$-cycle, and its other edges join opposite vertices): the
circular chromatic number of a graph with $9$ vertices is a fraction with numerator at most $9$
\cite{Vince,BondyHell,Zhu}, and
$8/3$ is the largest such fraction below $3$. The Wagner graph has $\chi_c=8/3$, $H_7$ is vertex-critical, and nine
vertices are the fewest possible in a plane without unit triangles: every triangle-free graph with at most $8$
vertices maps to $K_{8/3}$. More precisely, of the $1\,897$ triangle-free graphs with $9$ vertices, up to
isomorphism, exactly three have $\chi_c=3$: $M$, $H_7=M+m_0m_1$ and $M+m_0m_1+m_1m_2$. All three are unit-distance
graphs over $\Q(\sqrt7)$, and over $\Q(\sqrt{31})$. Without unit triangles, nine points with the twelve unit pairs of
$M$ span $M$ or $M$ plus one or two edges between the $m_j$, as any other pair at distance $1$ would close a
triangle, and a direct search for equilateral hexagons whose long diagonals are sums of two unit vectors
(\nolinkurl{hexagon_search.py}) finds all three over both fields. For example, the points
$v_0,\dots,v_5=(\frac{\sqrt7}4,\frac14)$, $(\frac{\sqrt7}4-1,\frac14)$, $(-\frac34,-\frac{\sqrt7}4)$, $(0,0)$,
$(-1,0)$, $(-\frac14,\frac{\sqrt7}4)$ and $m_0,m_1,m_2=(0,1)$, $(\frac{\sqrt7}4-1,-\frac34)$,
$(-\frac12-\frac{\sqrt7}4,\frac14)$ span $M$ itself (\nolinkurl{witness_q7m.json.gz}): a witness with the fewest
vertices and, among those, the fewest edges. The growth over $\Q(\sqrt{31})$ gives nine points that span
$M+m_0m_1+m_1m_2$ (\nolinkurl{witness_q31.json.gz}). These facts are checked by enumeration:
\nolinkurl{check_small.py} checks the points of the three stored witnesses, all $3^9$ colour maps, the absence of a
homomorphism to $K_{8/3}$ and the vertex-criticality; \nolinkurl{small_triangle_free.py} runs over the $4\,682\,270$
triangle-free graphs on $8$ labelled vertices, and \nolinkurl{nine_vertices.py} over the triangle-free graphs on $9$
vertices up to isomorphism, with two separate tests of $\chi_c=3$; certified SAT refutations confirm the tight
cycles once more. As $\sqrt7\in\Q_3$, $H_7$ is also an explicit witness for $\Q_3$ and for every finite extension $K$
of $\Q_3$ with $\chi_c(K^2)<4$.

\subsection*{Finite witnesses at four}
At $p=4q$ the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:winding} breaks in one place only, and what breaks it is itself a tight cycle.
For a homomorphism $c$ of $\Cay(\Gamma,S)$ to $K_4=K_{4/1}$, with $\ell$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:winding}, a
\emph{tight square} is a tight cycle $x\to x+s\to x+s+t\to x+t\to x$ with $s,t\in S$, that is,
$\ell(x,s)=\ell(x+s,t)=1$ and $\ell(x,t)=\ell(x+t,s)=3$; its four vertices have the colours $c(x),c(x)+1,c(x)+2,
c(x)+3$, so they are distinct.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:square}
Let $\Gamma$ be an abelian group, $S=-S\subseteq\Gamma\setminus\{0\}$ with $\Gamma=\Z S$, and $c$ a homomorphism of
$\Cay(\Gamma,S)$ to $K_4$ without tight squares. With $a(s)=\mathrm M_x\,\ell(x,s)$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:winding},
the conclusions of Lemma~\ref{lem:winding} hold with $p=4$ and $q=1$: $\xi(s)=a(s)/4$ defines a character of
$\Gamma$ with $\xi(S)\subseteq[\frac14,\frac34]$, and if $u_1,\dots,u_r\in S$ and positive integers $n_i$ satisfy
$\sum_in_iu_i=0$ and $a(u_i)=1$, then $c$ has a tight cycle. Conversely, if a character $\xi$ maps $S$ into
$[\frac14,\frac34]$, then $x\mapsto\lfloor4\tilde\xi(x)\rfloor\bmod4$, where $\tilde\xi(x)\in[0,1)$ is the lift of
$\xi(x)$, is a homomorphism of $\Cay(\Gamma,S)$ to $K_4$ without tight squares.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $x\in\Gamma$ and $s,t\in S$ the integers $A=\ell(x,s)+\ell(x+s,t)$ and $B=\ell(x,t)+\ell(x+t,s)$ are congruent
modulo $4$ and lie in $[2,6]$. So $A=B$ or $\{A,B\}=\{2,6\}$; exchanging $s$ and $t$ exchanges $A$ and $B$, and
$A=2$, $B=6$ says exactly, as $\ell(y+u,-u)=4-\ell(y,u)$, that $x\to x+s\to x+s+t\to x+t\to x$ is a tight square.
So $A=B$ for all $x$, $s$, $t$, and this is all that the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:winding} uses of $p<4q$; the rest
of that proof applies word for word. Conversely, for $s\in S$ let $\sigma\in[\frac14,\frac34]$ be the lift of
$\xi(s)$. As $\tilde\xi(x+s)-\tilde\xi(x)-\sigma\in\Z$, the integer congruent to $c(x+s)-c(x)$ that the lemma
calls $\ell(x,s)$ is $\lfloor4\tilde\xi(x)+4\sigma\rfloor-\lfloor4\tilde\xi(x)\rfloor\in\{\lfloor4\sigma\rfloor,
\lfloor4\sigma\rfloor+1\}$ (it is $3$ if $4\sigma=3$), which lies in $[1,3]$; so $c$ is a homomorphism to $K_4$,
while a tight square would need $\ell(x,s)=1$ and $\ell(x+t,s)=3$.
\end{proof}

So at four colours Theorem~W$^+$ holds for homomorphisms without tight squares: $\Cay(\Gamma,S)$ has a homomorphism
to $K_4$ without tight squares if and only if some character maps $S$ into $[\frac14,\frac34]$. Finiteness of $S$
is not used, and the same proof works for $K_{4q/q}$ for every $q$. In the example
$K_4=\Cay((\Z/2)^2,(\Z/2)^2\setminus\{0\})$ of \cite{Winding} every $4$-colouring is a tight square. A tight
$4$-cycle need not be a square: for $\Gamma=\Z/8$, $S=\{\pm1,\pm2\}$, $c(2k)=k$ and $c(2k+1)=k+2$, the cycle
$0\to2\to4\to6\to0$ is tight, there is no tight square, and $a/4$ is the character $x\mapsto5x/8$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:finiteS4}
Let $\Gamma$, $S$ be as in Lemma~\ref{lem:square}, with $S$ finite, $\Cay(\Gamma,S)$ $4$-colourable and
$\kappa(S)\le\frac14$. Then every homomorphism of $\Cay(\Gamma,S)$ to $K_4$ has a tight cycle, and some finite
subgraph of $\Cay(\Gamma,S)$ has circular chromatic number $4$. If $\kappa(S)<\frac14$, every such homomorphism
has a tight square.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $c$ be a homomorphism without tight cycles, $\xi$ and $a$ as in Lemma~\ref{lem:square}, and
$S_1=\{s\in S:a(s)=1\}$, which is disjoint from $-S_1$ as $a(-s)=4-a(s)$. If nonnegative integers $n_t$, not all
$0$, gave $\sum_{t\in S_1}n_tt=0$, Lemma~\ref{lem:square} would give a tight cycle. Otherwise, as in the proof of
Lemma~\ref{lem:finiteS} (where a nonnegative real relation among the images of $S_1$ in $\Gamma\otimes\R$ is
replaced by an integral one, and torsion by a multiple), there is a homomorphism $h\colon\Gamma\to\R$ with $h>0$ on
$S_1$ (any $h$ if $S_1$ is empty), and for small $\varepsilon>0$ the character $\xi+\varepsilon h$ maps $S$ into
$(\frac14,\frac34)+\Z$: if $a(s)=1$ it gives $\frac14+\varepsilon h(s)$, if $a(s)=3$ then $-s\in S_1$ and it gives
$\frac34-\varepsilon h(-s)$, and otherwise $\xi(s)$ is already inside. So $\kappa(S)>\frac14$, a contradiction. The
rest is the compactness argument of Lemma~\ref{lem:finiteS}, with Lemma~\ref{lem:tight} for $p/q=4$. If
$\kappa(S)<\frac14$, Lemma~\ref{lem:square} itself excludes homomorphisms without tight squares.
\end{proof}

Conversely, if a finite subgraph $H$ of $F^2$ has $\chi_c(H)=4$, then $\kappa(U_H)\le\frac14$ for the set $U_H$ of its
edge vectors and their negatives: each component of $H$ lies in a coset of $\Z U_H$, and if $\kappa(U_H)>\frac14$,
then for a rational $q/p\in(\frac14,\kappa(U_H)]$ a character with $\xi(U_H)\subseteq[q/p,1-q/p]$ gives a
homomorphism of $H$ to $K_{p/q}$ (the \emph{if} direction of Theorem~W$^+$ holds for every $p/q\ge2$), so
$\chi_c(H)\le p/q<4$. So, when $\chi(F^2)\le4$, some finite subgraph of $F^2$ has circular chromatic number $4$ if and
only if some finite $U\subset T$ has $\kappa(U)\le\frac14$.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:quarter}
Let $F$ be a number field with $i\notin F$ for which \textup{(a)}, \textup{(b)} and \textup{(7)} fail. Then some
finite $U\subset T$ has $\kappa(U)\le\frac14$: no character of $\Z U$ maps every element of $U$ into the open
interval $(\frac14,\frac34)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
As in the proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:finite} for $7/2$. If for every finite $V\supseteq\{b_j\}$ there were
$\beta\in L$ with $\Tr(\beta v)\in E'$ for every $v\in V$, the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:B2seven}, which uses
Lemma~\ref{lem:B1seven} only through this conclusion (such a $\beta$ lies in $K_2\times K_3\times K_7$, and the
types of the $\Tr(\beta v)$ give $\tau$), and Lemmas~\ref{lem:B3seven}--\ref{lem:B5seven} would give (c), (q) or
(7$'$), hence (a), (b) or (7). So some finite $V_0\supseteq\{b_j\}$ has no such $\beta$. Let $k$ be the least
multiple of $6$ with $5^k>42r'\max_{v\in V_0}(|D_v|+\sum_j|\mu_{v,j}|)$ (in the fixed basis; $r'$ does not depend
on $\theta$), $N=5^k$, and $U_0=G(k,1)V_0$, a finite symmetric subset of $T$. If $\kappa(U_0)>\frac14$, take $\theta$
with $\frac14<\theta\le\min(\kappa(U_0),\frac13)$ and a character $\xi$ of $\Z U_0$ with
$\xi(U_0)\subseteq[\theta,1-\theta]+\Z$; the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:B1seven}, which uses the homomorphism only
through such a character (Proposition~\ref{prop:window7} excludes its sixteen extra vectors for every
$\theta>\frac14$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:window7} needs only $\frac12-\theta<\frac14$), gives $\beta$ with
$\Tr(\beta v)\in E'$ for every $v\in V_0$: impossible. So $\kappa(U_0)\le\frac14$.
\end{proof}

For $i\notin F$ the converse holds too: if (a), (b) or (7) holds, the corresponding colouring and Theorem~W$^+$ give
$\kappa(U)\ge\frac27$ for every finite $U\subset T$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:finite4}
For every number field $F$ with $\chi_c(F^2)\le4$, some finite unit-distance graph in $F^2$ has circular chromatic
number $\chi_c(F^2)$. In particular the value $4$ is attained by finite unit-distance graphs over $\Q(\sqrt{59})$,
$\Q(\sqrt{83})$ and $\Q(\sqrt3,\sqrt{11})$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Below $4$ this is Corollary~\ref{cor:finite}. If $\chi_c(F^2)=4$, every finite subgraph has $\chi_c\le4$, hence
$\chi\le4$, and $\chi(F^2)\le4$ by the theorem of de Bruijn and Erd\H{o}s; so $i\notin F$, and (a), (b) and (7)
fail by Theorem~\ref{thm:F}, as otherwise $\chi_c(F^2)<4$. Proposition~\ref{prop:quarter} gives a finite $U\subset T$
with $\kappa(U)\le\frac14$, $\Cay(\Z U,U)\subseteq F^2$ is $4$-colourable, and Corollary~\ref{cor:finiteS4} gives a
finite subgraph $H$ with $\chi_c(H)=4$; the unit-distance graph induced on its vertices also has $\chi_c=4$, as
$4=\chi_c(H)\le\chi_c\le\chi_c(F^2)=4$. For $\Q(\sqrt{59})$ and $\Q(\sqrt{83})$, $\chi_c=4$ by the discussion after
Theorem~\ref{thm:F}; for $\Q(\sqrt3,\sqrt{11})$, $\chi=4$ and (7) fails, as $7$ is inert in $\Q(\sqrt3)$.
\end{proof}

An independent checker refereed this subsection with two programs of its own, which test Lemma~\ref{lem:square},
Corollary~\ref{cor:finiteS4} and the four-colour form of Theorem~W$^+$ on every abelian group of order at most $16$
(\nolinkurl{data/number_fields/circular/at_four/indep/}). The proof gives no bound on the size of the witness, and we
know none. Colouring-guided growths over $\Q(\sqrt{59})$ with the unit vectors of denominator $210$, $1050$ and
$2730$ stopped at their limits; for $210$ this was bound to happen, as the character
$\frac{(89a+59b+89c+59e)}{118}\bmod1$ of the points $(\frac{a+b\sqrt{59}}{210},\frac{c+e\sqrt{59}}{210})$ keeps all
$108$ unit vectors with denominator $210$ at distance at least $\frac{15}{59}$ from $\Z$, so that every graph built
from them maps to $K_{59/15}$.

certificates of value $\frac27$ for the vectors of type $7$ in Proposition~\ref{prop:window7} were added after the
checkers' reports, by a program of our own (an exact search among the margins that are active at the $7$-adic points),
and are checked by a further program that rebuilds $325\rho^j\sigma^l$ as products of $2\pm i$ and $3\pm2i$; an independent
checker had found the same value $\frac27$ by exact linear programming. For Lemma~\ref{lem:window7}, an
independent checker lifted $S^\theta(k,1)$ exactly to the levels $k=6$ and $k=12$ and found only the components of the
stated form (and, at $k=5$, components of type $7$ not of that form, so the hypothesis $6\mid k$ is needed). The
conclusion of
Lemma~\ref{lem:window} was tested exactly at every level $k\le7$ for $\theta=\frac3{10}$ and $k\le6$ for
$\theta=\frac{201}{700}$: modulo $13\cdot5^k$ there are exactly five components, all of the stated form, while for
$\theta=\frac7{25}$ the test finds the eight components of type $7$ at each level $k\le3$. Lemma~\ref{lem:seven}, proved above by
hand, was also checked by two programs written separately, and its proof by an independent checker with a program
of its own that checks (i)--(iv) exhaustively (\nolinkurl{indep_L13/}); Lemma~\ref{lem:B5seven} directly for $f=3$ (no $\F_{49}$-linear map
$\F_{7^6}\to\F_{49}$ maps $\mu_{344}$ into $A'_7$), and Lemma~\ref{lem:B4seven} on the finite models
$\F_{49}[\pi]/(\pi^e)$, $e\le3$. The torsion characters of the last Remark were found by two programs; for orders up
to $400$, one of them counts each character at its exact order. For Corollary~\ref{cor:finite}, which needs no
computation beyond those of Theorem~\ref{thm:F}, two independent checkers tested Lemmas~\ref{lem:tight}--\ref{lem:finiteS}
with programs of their own: on $291$ small graphs and $766$ tight relations; on $115$ random finite sets $S$ in $\Z$,
$\Z^2$ and $\Z\times\Z/m$ with $2<\chi_c<4$, at all of whose $409$ optimal points (vertices of the optimal set, and
midpoints and centroids of them that stay optimal) the tight elements have a nonnegative relation
(\nolinkurl{indep_FW2/test_kappa_relations.py} with arguments $1$ and $180$); and by SAT on finite pieces of $13$ such Cayley graphs, all of which have finite witnesses
(for instance the segment $\{0,\dots,18\}$ of the distance graph with distances $3,4,9,12$, of circular chromatic
number $7/2$). One of them clipped $S^{2/7}(2,1)$ exactly: $13$ components, one of type c, four of type q, and the
eight points of type $7$, each a component by itself. As consistency checks, a program
(\nolinkurl{tight_walks.py}) finds that each of the eight certificates of type $7$ has three positive multipliers,
with margins exactly $\frac27$ at its $7$-adic point and integer relations among the tight rotations
(multiplicities $63,52,25$ or $13,14,15$); the same holds for the tight elements of the $7$-adic characters on the
$140$ vectors of Theorem~\ref{thm:C}; and the program checks the Moser spindle. The explicit finite witnesses of
Section~\ref{sec:twoprimes}, their certificates and the records of every check are in

\section{Questions}\label{sec:questions}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Above four.} By Theorem~\ref{thm:F} and Corollary~\ref{cor:localF}, below $4$ the value of
$\chi_c(F^2)$ is the least value of $\chi_c(F_v^2)$ over the completions, and it is given by a locally constant
homomorphism of one completion to a circular clique. Is this true above $4$? There Theorem~W$^+$ is not available: a
homomorphism of a Cayley graph to $K_{p/q}$ with $p/q\ge4$ need not come from a character. In particular, is there a
number field $F$ with $4<\chi_c(F^2)<5$? Every $F$ with $\chi(F^2)=5$ and a place of residue field $\F_{19}$ would be
one, as $\kappa_1=4/19$ there gives $\chi_c\le19/4$; for example $\Q(\sqrt{47})$ if $\chi(\Q(\sqrt{47})^2)=5$. The
$5$-chromatic unit-distance graphs we know (those of de Grey \cite{deGrey}, Exoo and Ismailescu \cite{EI}, Heule
\cite{Heule} and Parts \cite{Parts}, and one over $\Q(\sqrt{-3},\sqrt{-7},\sqrt{-11})$ in \cite{Repo},
\nolinkurl{notes/local_colourings.md}, Section~5) lie over fields that contain $\sqrt3$, or $\sqrt{-3}$ and $\sqrt{-7}$,
and $3$ and $-7$ are not squares modulo $19$.
\item \emph{Four colours.} Is $\chi(F^2)$, in general, the least number of colours of a locally constant colouring
of one completion? (For real fields this is \cite[Question~8]{TC}, which also allows Borel colourings at the real
places.) Theorem~\ref{thm:B} answers this at three colours. Among real quadratic fields, ordered by $d$, the first open case
is $\Q(\sqrt{47})$, where
$4\le\chi\le5$ (\cite[Corollary~2]{FC} and \cite{Moorhouse}); by Theorem~\ref{thm:F}, $\chi_c(\Q(\sqrt{47})^2)\ge4$,
so $\chi_c(\Q(\sqrt{47})^2)=4$ if and only if $\chi(\Q(\sqrt{47})^2)=4$ (if $\chi_c(F^2)=4$, every finite subgraph has
$\chi_c\le4$, hence $\chi\le4$, and $\chi(F^2)\le4$ by the theorem of de Bruijn and Erd\H{o}s).
\item \emph{Finite witnesses.} By Corollary~\ref{cor:finite} some finite unit-distance graph in $\Q(\sqrt{11})^2$
has circular chromatic number $7/2$; the proof, by compactness, gives no bound on its size, and the smallest we know
has $155$ vertices and is vertex-critical (Section~\ref{sec:twoprimes}). How small can it be? (For the value $3$ in
a plane without unit triangles, nine vertices are needed, and they suffice over $\Q(\sqrt7)$ and $\Q(\sqrt{31})$;
Section~\ref{sec:twoprimes}. Over other such planes the least number is open; over $\Q(\sqrt{15})$ the smallest
witness we found has $13$ vertices (\nolinkurl{witness_q15.json.gz}), and none of the three nine-vertex graphs
appeared with the denominators we tried.)
When $\chi_c(F^2)=4$ the value is attained as well (Theorem~\ref{thm:finite4}, for example over $\Q(\sqrt{59})$), but the
proof gives no bound on the size of a witness, and we know none: how small can one be over $\Q(\sqrt{59})$? Its edge
vectors $U$ must satisfy $\kappa(U)\le\frac14$, which excludes those with denominator $210$ (Section~\ref{sec:twoprimes}).
\end{enumerate}
